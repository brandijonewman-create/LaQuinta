Most people who search for Arizona golf communities start with Scottsdale, and for good reason. But Arizona is a big state with very different climates, and some of its best golf communities are far from Scottsdale, in the Phoenix suburbs, around Tucson and up in the cool pine country of the north.

This guide looks at golf communities statewide, region by region. For Scottsdale itself, see our [area-by-area guide to Scottsdale golf communities](/blog/golf-communities-in-scottsdale-arizona-area-guide).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Community details, membership rules, age requirements and costs change. Verify everything directly with each community and club and work with a licensed real estate professional before making a decision.*

## The three golf regions of Arizona

Arizona’s golf communities fall into three broad regions, and elevation is the biggest difference between them.

**Greater Phoenix**, including Scottsdale, sits in the low Sonoran Desert. Winters are mild and the golf season peaks from fall through spring. Summers are very hot.

**Tucson and southern Arizona** are also desert, framed by mountain ranges. The climate is similar to Phoenix, with a similar winter golf season.

**The high country**, including Prescott, Sedona and Flagstaff, sits much higher. Summers are far milder, and winters can bring cold and snow, especially in Flagstaff. Golf season here is largely the reverse of the desert.

That is why some Arizona golfers own in two places: a desert home for winter and a high-country home for summer.

## Greater Phoenix beyond Scottsdale

### Gold Canyon: Superstition Mountain Golf and Country Club

East of Phoenix, at the foot of the Superstition Mountains, Superstition Mountain Golf and Country Club is a private club with two courses designed by Jack Nicklaus with his sons:

- **Prospector**, designed with Gary Nicklaus, which opened in 1998
- **Lost Gold**, designed with Jack Nicklaus II, often described as the tougher of the two

The setting, with the Superstition Mountains as a backdrop, is the main draw.

### Wickenburg: Wickenburg Ranch

Northwest of Phoenix, Wickenburg Ranch is a golf community whose courses, Big Wick and Li’l Wick, were designed by Bill Brownlee and Wendell Pickett, the same pair who later designed No. 7 at Desert Mountain. Wickenburg offers a small-town setting at a longer distance from the city.

### Goodyear: PebbleCreek

In the West Valley, PebbleCreek is a 55+ golf community developed by Robson Resort Communities. It is one of several large active-adult communities on the west side of the Phoenix area.

### Buckeye: Verrado

Farther west, Verrado is a master-planned community in Buckeye. It includes a 55+ section, Four Seasons at Victory at Verrado, built by K. Hovnanian.

### Anthem

North of Phoenix, Anthem Golf and Country Club is owned by Invited, which offers golf membership to Anthem property owners and Anthem Parkside residents.

**How they compare with Scottsdale.** These communities often offer more space, newer homes or an active-adult setting at a greater distance from Scottsdale’s dining, shopping and private-club concentration. For many buyers, the trade-off is worth it. For others, Scottsdale’s amenities are the point.

## Tucson and southern Arizona

### Marana: Dove Mountain

Northwest of Tucson, Dove Mountain is home to some of the best-known golf in the Tucson area, including The Gallery. One source describes an 18-hole championship course there by Arthur Hills, and another credits Jack Nicklaus with a design at Dove Mountain. The setting in the Tortolita Mountains is a major part of its appeal.

### SaddleBrooke

North of Tucson, SaddleBrooke is a large active-adult community with two 18-hole courses and mountain views. **The Preserve at SaddleBrooke** is a gated 55+ section with two semi-private 18-hole clubs, The Preserve Golf Club and MountainView Golf Club.

### Oracle: SaddleBrooke Ranch

Farther north, SaddleBrooke Ranch is described as one of the largest active-adult communities in southern Arizona, with its own 18-hole course designed by Tom Schroeder, a driving range and a pro shop.

### Oro Valley: Sun City Oro Valley

Sun City Oro Valley is a 55+ community of about 2,500 attached and single-family homes. Its course is The Views Golf Club at Oro Valley.

**How they compare with Scottsdale.** Southern Arizona golf communities often emphasize mountain views, active-adult living and a quieter pace. Tucson is a smaller city than Phoenix, with a strong university and medical presence, but it is a longer trip to Phoenix Sky Harbor for flights.

## The high country

### Flagstaff: Pine Canyon

At high elevation in Flagstaff, Pine Canyon is a private mountain golf community. Its course, credited to Jay Morrish, opened in 2004 and has ranked near the top of at least one published list of Arizona’s best courses. Summer is the season here, with temperatures far below the desert.

### Sedona: Seven Canyons

In Sedona, Seven Canyons Golf Club sits within the Coconino National Forest, surrounded by red rock. Its course was designed by Tom Weiskopf and opened in 2003.

### Prescott: Talking Rock

North of Phoenix, Talking Rock is a private club in the Prescott area with a Jay Morrish course designed with walkability in mind.

**How they compare with Scottsdale.** The high country offers what the desert cannot: comfortable summer golf, pine forests and red rock. In exchange, winter golf is limited, especially in Flagstaff, and most of these communities are smaller and more remote.

## Golf models you will find across Arizona

The same four golf models found in Scottsdale appear statewide, in different proportions.

**Private clubs inside communities.** Superstition Mountain, Pine Canyon and Talking Rock are examples outside Scottsdale. Expect initiation fees and dues on top of HOA assessments, and ask whether membership is required, optional or limited to property owners.

**Clubs owned by operators.** Anthem Golf and Country Club, owned by Invited, is one example. The owner, not the members, makes the major decisions.

**Active-adult golf.** Communities such as PebbleCreek, SaddleBrooke and Sun City Oro Valley build golf into a 55+ lifestyle. How golf is paid for, whether bundled, by membership or by round, varies by community.

**Public and resort golf.** Across the state, many golf communities sit next to public or resort courses with no club obligation. These often have the lowest fixed costs.

## Questions to ask any Arizona golf community

- Is golf access included, optional or limited to members?
- What are the HOA assessments, and what do they cover?
- If there is a club, who owns it, and what are the current initiation and dues?
- Are there age requirements for the community or any part of it?
- What are the rental rules?
- How far is the nearest hospital, airport and major shopping?
- What is the community like in its off-season?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed real estate professional.

## How to compare Arizona golf communities

1. **Decide on your season.** If you will live here year-round, think about summer. If you are a winter resident, the desert regions make the most sense. If you want summer golf, look north.
2. **Decide on your golf model.** Private club, semi-private club, active-adult club or public golf nearby. Each has a very different cost and social life.
3. **Check age requirements.** Many communities in this guide are 55+. Confirm the rules with each one.
4. **Count your drives.** Time the drive to an airport, medical care and shopping. Distances in Arizona are large.
5. **Get the full cost.** HOA assessments, club initiation and dues where they apply, property taxes and insurance. Ask each community for current figures in writing.
6. **Visit in more than one season.** A desert community in July and a mountain community in January can feel very different from the brochure.

## A note on water and the desert

Golf in Arizona’s desert regions depends on careful water management, and communities increasingly talk about it. If you are buying in a desert golf community, it is reasonable to ask the HOA or club how the course is irrigated, whether turf has been reduced in recent years and whether any changes are planned. The answers can affect how the course looks and what it costs to maintain.

## Two-home strategies

Arizona makes a two-home golf life possible within a few hours’ drive. A common pattern is a desert home in Scottsdale, Gold Canyon or Tucson for winter and a high-country home in Prescott, Sedona or Flagstaff for summer. If you are considering this, think about:

- **Two sets of carrying costs**, including two HOAs and possibly two club memberships
- **Lock-and-leave fit** for whichever home sits empty
- **Reciprocal play**, since some private clubs arrange access with other clubs
- **Tax and residency questions**, which a licensed tax professional can help with

## Where Scottsdale fits

Scottsdale remains Arizona’s deepest concentration of private clubs, resort golf and luxury golf communities, from Desert Mountain and Silverleaf to Troon North and Grayhawk. The rest of the state adds options Scottsdale cannot: summer golf in the pines, red-rock settings, quieter southern Arizona living and large active-adult communities. Many buyers start their search in Scottsdale and end up somewhere else, or own in both.
