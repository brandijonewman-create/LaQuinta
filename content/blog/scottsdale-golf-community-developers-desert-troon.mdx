Every Scottsdale golf community started with a developer: someone who bought ranch land or desert, planned the roads and lots, hired a golf architect and wrote the rules homeowners still live by. A small number of companies and individuals shaped most of what buyers see today.

This guide looks at the developers and owners behind Scottsdale’s best-known golf communities and clubs, including Desert Troon Companies, and explains why that history still matters.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Development histories come from public sources, which sometimes disagree. Verify current details with each community, club and a licensed real estate professional before making any decision.*

## Why the developer still matters

Long after the last lot sells, a developer’s decisions remain:

- **Layout and density:** how lots, roads, open space and golf courses fit together
- **Design rules:** architectural guidelines and approval processes
- **Governing documents:** the CC&Rs and association structure
- **Club structure:** whether the club was built as member-owned, developer-owned or public

Understanding who built a community, and when, helps explain how it works today and what may change.

## Kaiser-Aetna and McCormick Ranch

**McCormick Ranch** began as the cattle and Arabian horse ranch of Anne and Fowler McCormick, who started buying the land in 1943. Kaiser-Aetna bought the property around 1970 for a reported $12.1 million. The property owners association was incorporated in 1972, and the community was built out between 1972 and 1989. McCormick Ranch Golf Club’s Palm and Pine courses, designed by Desmond Muirhead, opened in 1972.

McCormick Ranch was one of Scottsdale’s first large master-planned communities, and its lakes, greenbelts and paths set a pattern others followed.

## Markland Properties and Gainey Ranch

**Gainey Ranch** was the ranch of Daniel C. Gainey, known for raising Arabian horses. Markland Properties bought land from Gainey in 1980, and development began in the mid-1980s. The result was a guard-gated community of individually gated neighborhoods around a 27-hole golf club, now owned by Invited.

Our [Gainey Ranch vs. McCormick Ranch comparison](/blog/gainey-ranch-vs-mccormick-ranch) covers both communities in detail.

## Lyle Anderson: Desert Highlands and Desert Mountain

Developer **Lyle Anderson** shaped the far north more than anyone. In the early 1980s, he hired Jack Nicklaus, with Bob Cupp as senior designer, to build the course at **Desert Highlands**, which opened in 1984. In 1986, he began **Desert Mountain**, which grew into a community of about 8,300 acres with six Nicklaus Signature courses opened between 1987 and 2003.

Anderson also developed Las Campanas in Santa Fe and Loch Lomond Golf Club in Scotland. Desert Mountain’s club describes its history as being “at one with the land” since 1986.

Ownership of Desert Mountain’s club later passed to **Crescent Real Estate Holdings**. In 2011, members bought the club’s six golf courses, related facilities and about 500 acres of developable land from Crescent for a reported $73.5 million. Our [Desert Mountain overview](/blog/desert-mountain-scottsdale-az-overview) covers the story.

## DMB: DC Ranch and Silverleaf

**DMB** was founded in Scottsdale in 1984. It began work on **DC Ranch** in 1995, on land with a long ranching history. Sources differ on its eventual size, from about 3,000 homes to nearly 4,000 lots and units. Architect Vernon D. Swaback, the youngest apprentice of Frank Lloyd Wright, served as architect of DC Ranch.

Within DC Ranch, DMB created **Silverleaf** as a private gated enclave. DMB’s own materials describe about 700 custom homesites and a club, spa and golf course. Silverleaf’s Tom Weiskopf course opened in 2002. The name comes from a historic “Silver Leaf” mining title tied to the ranch.

DMB has also developed master-planned communities elsewhere in the Phoenix area, including Verrado in Buckeye, Eastmark in Mesa and Marley Park.

## Grayhawk Development: Grayhawk

**Grayhawk Development** is the Scottsdale-based firm behind **Grayhawk**, which it describes as more than 1,600 acres with two interconnected areas, The Park and The Retreat. Grayhawk Golf Club, with Tom Fazio’s Raptor and David Graham and Gary Panks’s Talon, opened to the public in 1994. Our [Grayhawk real estate guide](/blog/grayhawk-real-estate-guide-2026) covers the community.

## Desert Troon Companies

**Desert Troon Companies** is a Scottsdale-based real estate development firm. One release describes it as having been based in Scottsdale for more than 30 years. Public reporting covers mainly its commercial work:

- **Perimeter Center:** Desert Troon originally developed a five-building office portfolio in the Perimeter Center business park and sold it for $122 million in 2016.
- **Office development:** a 2007 report described construction on a roughly $139.5 million class A office project near the TPC Desert golf course.
- **Scorpion Bay:** Desert Troon was named concessionaire of the Scorpion Bay marina at Lake Pleasant, under an agreement running until 2040.

**Desert Troon vs. Troon.** Despite the similar name, Desert Troon Companies is a separate business from **Troon**, the golf management company. Troon is described as the world’s largest golf management company, with more than 250 courses in its portfolio, and is associated with Troon North Golf Club. We did not find a public source confirming a business link between Desert Troon and Troon, or confirming Desert Troon’s role in developing Troon North. If that history matters to your decision, ask the community’s associations directly.

## Club owners: Crescent, Invited, Arcis and Bob Parsons

Golf clubs often outlive their developers and change hands.

- **Crescent Real Estate Holdings** owned Desert Mountain’s club before members bought it in 2011.
- **Invited**, formerly ClubCorp, owns Gainey Ranch Golf Club and Anthem Golf and Country Club.
- **Arcis Golf** owns Ancala Country Club and Tatum Ranch Golf Club.
- **Bob Parsons**, founder of GoDaddy and PXG, bought The Golf Club Scottsdale in October 2013 for a reported $600,000 plus its debts, renamed it Scottsdale National and has been reported to have invested about $250 million in it. He and his wife, Renee Parsons, own it.

Who owns a club today matters more to a member than who built it. Our [club-by-club ownership guide](/blog/equity-vs-non-equity-golf-memberships-scottsdale-clubs) covers current ownership.

## How developer models differ

Scottsdale’s developers took different approaches to golf, and those choices still shape communities today:

- **Public golf at the center.** Grayhawk and Troon North built communities around courses open to everyone, so residents never needed a private club.
- **Private clubs inside the community.** Desert Mountain, Silverleaf, Estancia and others built private clubs as part of the community. Some later became member-owned.
- **Resort and club together.** The Boulders paired homes with a resort and an optional club.
- **Clubs without homes.** Whisper Rock and Scottsdale National were built or rebuilt as golf clubs with no conventional residential community.

## The golf architects

Developers hired a remarkable group of golf architects:

- **Jack Nicklaus:** Desert Highlands and Desert Mountain
- **Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish:** Troon Country Club, TPC Scottsdale’s Stadium Course and Troon North’s Monument
- **Tom Weiskopf:** Troon North’s Pinnacle and Silverleaf
- **Tom Fazio:** Estancia, Mirabel, Grayhawk’s Raptor and Whisper Rock’s Upper Course
- **Jay Morrish:** both courses at The Boulders
- **Desmond Muirhead:** McCormick Ranch’s Palm and Pine
- **Phil Mickelson with Gary Stephenson:** Whisper Rock’s Lower Course

## A timeline of Scottsdale golf development

- **1943:** Anne and Fowler McCormick begin buying the land that becomes McCormick Ranch.
- **Around 1970:** Kaiser-Aetna buys McCormick Ranch.
- **1972:** McCormick Ranch Golf Club opens, and the property owners association is incorporated.
- **1980:** Markland Properties buys land from Daniel C. Gainey.
- **1984:** Desert Highlands’ Nicklaus course opens, and DMB is founded in Scottsdale.
- **Mid-1980s:** Development of Gainey Ranch begins. Troon Country Club’s clubhouse opens in 1986.
- **1986:** Lyle Anderson begins Desert Mountain.
- **1990:** Troon North’s Monument course opens.
- **1994:** Grayhawk Golf Club opens.
- **1995:** DMB begins DC Ranch.
- **2002:** Silverleaf’s Tom Weiskopf course opens.
- **2011:** Members buy Desert Mountain Club from Crescent Real Estate Holdings.
- **2013:** Bob Parsons buys The Golf Club Scottsdale and renames it Scottsdale National.
- **2016:** Desert Troon Companies sells its Perimeter Center office portfolio for $122 million.

## Questions to ask about a community’s history

When you are serious about a home, ask:

1. Who developed the community, and when was it completed?
2. Does the original developer still control any land, votes or decisions?
3. Who owns the golf club today, and has ownership changed?
4. When were the CC&Rs and design guidelines last amended?
5. Are there any unfinished phases or planned developments nearby?

## What this means for buyers

1. **Read the original documents.** CC&Rs written by the developer often still govern the community.
2. **Ask who controls the club now.** A club built by a developer may now be member-owned, owner-operated or public.
3. **Understand the design rules.** Many developers set strict architectural standards that still apply.
4. **Check for unfinished business.** In newer communities, ask whether the developer still controls any land or decisions.

*Important: Development histories and ownership details come from public sources, which sometimes disagree, and ownership can change. Confirm current details with each community and club, and consult a licensed real estate professional before making any decision.*
