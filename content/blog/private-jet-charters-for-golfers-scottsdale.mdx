For some golfers, a private jet turns a long weekend into a real golf trip. You can leave Scottsdale in the morning, play a famous course across the country in the afternoon and be home without a single airport line. It is expensive, but for groups splitting the cost, or trips to resorts far from major airports, it can make sense.

This guide explains how private aviation works for golfers, what published sources say it costs, the basics of flying from Scottsdale Airport and how to check safety. It does not recommend specific charter operators or brokers.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax, aviation or financial advice. Prices, availability and policies change. Get written quotes that list all fees, confirm the operator’s certification yourself, and talk to a licensed aviation attorney, tax professional or insurance professional before signing any long-term agreement.*

## Three ways to fly private

**1. On-demand charter.** You book a specific trip on a specific aircraft, often through a broker or directly with an operator. You pay per trip. It is the most flexible way to start, with no long-term commitment.

**2. Jet cards.** You prepay for a block of flight hours or a dollar amount with a provider. In return, you typically get fixed hourly rates and guaranteed availability with limited notice. Terms vary widely, including blackout dates, minimum flight times and fees.

**3. Fractional ownership.** You buy a share of an aircraft, sometimes as small as one-sixteenth, and pay monthly management fees plus an hourly rate when you fly. It suits frequent flyers who want guaranteed access and a consistent fleet.

Full aircraft ownership is a fourth option for very frequent flyers, but it brings the most cost, complexity and tax and legal considerations.

## What published sources say it costs

These are published 2025 U.S. estimates from industry and broker sources. They vary, and they usually exclude taxes and fees.

**Hourly charter rates**

- **Light jets:** roughly $2,500 to $6,000 an hour
- **Midsize jets:** roughly $4,000 to $8,000 an hour
- **Heavy jets:** roughly $8,000 to $18,000 an hour, with ultra-long-range jets higher

**Jet cards:** one industry benchmark reported an average hourly rate of about $11,300 at the end of the second quarter of 2025, averaged across card types and aircraft sizes.

**Extras that add to the price:**

- Federal excise tax
- Landing, parking and handling fees
- Positioning, when the aircraft has to fly empty to pick you up or return
- Crew expenses, including overnight stays
- Catering
- De-icing in winter destinations

Always ask for an itemized quote and ask what is not included.

## Scottsdale Airport basics

Scottsdale Airport (SDL) is a city-owned general aviation airport that serves much of the area’s private aviation.

- **Runway:** a single runway, 03/21, about 8,249 feet long
- **Aircraft size:** the city says it can accept aircraft up to 75,000 pounds, or up to 100,000 pounds with prior permission
- **Activity:** about 164,000 takeoffs and landings in 2025, according to the city
- **Services:** several fixed base operators provide fuel, lounges and ground services
- **Funding:** the city says it operates as an enterprise fund without general fund subsidy

Phoenix Sky Harbor and Phoenix Deer Valley are also options, depending on your aircraft, destination and where you live. Time the drive from your home to each.

## Safety: who is actually flying you?

In the United States, on-demand charter flights for hire are operated under FAA Part 135 rules. A broker may sell you a flight, but a certificated operator flies it. Before you book:

1. **Ask which company operates the flight,** not just who is selling it.
2. **Confirm the operator holds an FAA Part 135 certificate.**
3. **Ask about third-party safety audits or ratings,** which many buyers look for.
4. **Ask about the pilots’** experience and training.
5. **Ask about the aircraft’s** age and maintenance.
6. **Be wary of illegal charter,** where an operator without proper certification sells flights. If a price seems far below the market, ask more questions.

## Flying with golf clubs

Golf bags take up a lot of space, and smaller jets have limited baggage room.

- **Tell the operator exactly** how many passengers, golf bags and suitcases you have.
- **Ask about baggage capacity** for the specific aircraft, including whether bags fit in a heated or pressurized compartment.
- **Consider soft travel bags,** which fit odd spaces better than hard cases, but pad clubs well.
- **Ship clubs ahead** if space is tight.
- **Size up the aircraft** for a group with clubs. A midsize jet may be more practical than a light jet for four golfers.

## A worked group example

Using the published ranges, here is how a group trip might look. Real quotes will differ.

- **Four golfers on a midsize jet,** with about three hours of flying each way, so roughly six flight hours in total
- **At roughly $4,000 to $8,000 an hour,** that is about $24,000 to $48,000 before taxes and fees
- **Split four ways,** about $6,000 to $12,000 per person, before taxes, fees and any positioning

For some groups, that is worth it for the time saved and the convenience. For others, premium commercial seats make more sense. Get quotes for both before you decide.

## Empty legs

When an aircraft has to fly empty to reposition, operators sometimes sell that one-way “empty leg” at a discount. Empty legs can be good value for flexible travelers, but they come with trade-offs: the schedule is set by the operator, and the flight can be canceled or changed if the original trip changes. They rarely suit a golf trip with fixed tee times.

## Semi-private options

Between commercial and fully private flights are semi-private services that sell individual seats on smaller aircraft from private terminals. One example at Scottsdale Airport is JSX, which moved its Phoenix-area service from Sky Harbor to Scottsdale in January 2024 and operates under Part 135 rules. Semi-private service can offer a faster, simpler airport experience at a price closer to premium commercial travel. Check routes, baggage policies and golf-bag rules before booking.

## Understanding jet sizes

- **Light jets** typically seat a small group and suit shorter trips, but baggage space for golf bags can be tight.
- **Midsize jets** offer more cabin and baggage room and longer range, often a good fit for four golfers with clubs.
- **Heavy jets** offer the most space and range, suiting larger groups and long or international trips.

Ask the operator for the passenger and baggage limits for the specific aircraft, not just the category.

## When private makes sense for golf

- **Groups.** Splitting the cost among four to eight golfers can bring the per-person price closer to premium commercial travel.
- **Resort destinations** far from major airports, where private flights land much closer.
- **Tight schedules,** such as playing two destinations in a weekend.
- **Day trips** to courses within a few hours’ flight.

For solo travelers on routes with good airline service, commercial flights are usually far cheaper.

## On the day of the flight

- **Arrive at the FBO** a little before departure. Private terminals are usually quick, but allow time for parking and loading bags.
- **Bring identification.** Private flights still require ID, and international trips require passports and customs procedures.
- **Confirm the tail number** and operator with your booking.
- **Pack medications and valuables** in carry-on bags.
- **Ask about weather.** Summer heat in Arizona can affect aircraft performance and timing, and summer monsoon storms can cause delays.

## Insurance and liability

Charter operators carry aircraft and liability insurance, but coverage terms vary. Ask what the operator’s policy covers for passengers and baggage, including golf equipment. If you are considering ownership or fractional shares, talk to a licensed aviation insurance professional about the coverage you will need.

## Choosing between charter, cards and fractional

- **Fly a few trips a year:** on-demand charter is usually the simplest.
- **Fly regularly and want guaranteed availability:** jet cards are worth comparing.
- **Fly frequently and want a consistent aircraft and service:** fractional ownership may fit.

Before signing any card or fractional agreement, read the contract carefully, including minimum notice, peak-day surcharges, blackout dates, cancellation terms, refunds and what happens if the provider changes ownership. A licensed aviation attorney and tax professional can help.

## Questions to ask before you book

1. Who is the operator, and does it hold an FAA Part 135 certificate?
2. What aircraft will we fly, and what is its baggage capacity?
3. What is included in the quote, and what fees are extra?
4. Is positioning included?
5. What is the cancellation policy?
6. What happens if the aircraft has a mechanical issue?
7. Is catering available, and at what cost?
