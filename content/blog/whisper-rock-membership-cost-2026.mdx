Whisper Rock Golf Club is one of the most talked-about private clubs in Arizona, in part because it is known for the number of touring professionals who belong. It is also one of the least transparent about price. The club is invitation-only and does not publish its fees.

This article focuses on the ongoing cost of being a member: dues, the charges that sit on top of dues, and the housing cost that comes with a club that has no homes of its own. The initiation fee itself, including how it is structured and how it compares with peer clubs, is covered in a separate companion guide.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All figures below are third-party estimates presented as ranges. Verify every figure and term directly with Whisper Rock Golf Club and review them with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before making a decision.*

## What Whisper Rock is

Whisper Rock is a private golf club in North Scottsdale. Most sources date its opening to 2001. It has two 18-hole courses:

- **The Lower Course**, designed by Phil Mickelson with architect Gary Stephenson
- **The Upper Course**, designed by Tom Fazio

The club is described across many sources as a favorite of tour players, and its membership includes a notable number of PGA TOUR professionals. That reputation shapes the culture. Whisper Rock is a golf-first club where the quality of the courses and the practice facilities is the main reason to belong.

What Whisper Rock does not have is a residential community. There are no homes inside the club. That single fact changes how you should think about what membership really costs.

## What published sources say the dues are

The club does not publish a dues schedule. Here is the range of third-party estimates we found:

- **Most commonly cited:** about $15,400 a year, which works out to roughly $1,283 a month
- **Higher single estimate:** about $1,725 a month for an equity golf membership, or roughly $20,700 a year
- **Cost-estimator range:** roughly $18,000 to $25,000 a year

Taken together, a reasonable planning range for dues alone is roughly **$15,000 to $25,000 a year**. The spread reflects different dates, different membership categories and different methods. None of these figures is official, and dues at private clubs usually rise over time.

For comparison, published 2026 estimates for Scottsdale-area private clubs generally put monthly dues at roughly $800 to $3,500. The most-cited Whisper Rock figure sits toward the lower middle of that range, which may surprise buyers who assume the club’s reputation means it charges the highest dues in the market.

## What sits on top of dues

Dues are only one line in the annual budget. Ask the club about each of the following before you estimate your yearly spend.

**Food and beverage minimums.** Many private clubs require a minimum annual spend on dining. Ask whether Whisper Rock has one and what happens to unspent amounts.

**Cart and range fees.** One published estimate says cart fees are generally included with play. Confirm that, and ask about practice facility access.

**Guest fees.** If you plan to host clients, friends or family, guest fees can become a significant cost. Ask how many guest rounds are allowed and what each one costs.

**Assessments.** Clubs fund major projects through assessments. Ask about any current or planned assessments and how they are decided. At a member-owned club, assessments are typically approved by the board or the membership.

**Lockers, storage and caddies.** Ask which of these are included and which are billed separately.

**Taxes and service charges.** Ask whether a service charge is added to dining and whether any charges are subject to tax.

Once you have answers, add them to the dues range above. For many members, these extras add meaningfully to the annual total, especially for those who entertain often.

## The housing cost: the part most estimates skip

Because Whisper Rock has no homes, every member needs housing somewhere else. For a local, full-time resident that may simply be the home they already own. For anyone relocating or buying a second home to use the club, housing is a major part of the true cost of membership.

Members live across North Scottsdale and the surrounding area. If you are buying with Whisper Rock in mind, a few practical points apply:

- **Drive time matters.** If you plan to play several times a week, a short drive to the club makes a real difference in how often you actually go. Time the drive from any home you are considering.
- **A second club may be in the mix.** Some households choose a home in a community with its own club, for dining and social life close to home, and use Whisper Rock for golf. That means paying for two memberships. Others choose a community with no mandatory club and keep Whisper Rock as their only membership.
- **HOA costs still apply.** Whatever community you choose will have its own HOA assessments, separate from any club.

## Building a realistic annual budget

Here is a simple way to estimate what Whisper Rock costs you each year, using published ranges. Replace each line with the club’s actual figures once you have them.

1. **Annual dues:** roughly $15,000 to $25,000, based on published estimates
2. **Food and beverage minimum, if any:** ask the club
3. **Guest fees:** your expected guest rounds times the per-round fee
4. **Assessments:** any current or planned amounts, spread over the years they apply
5. **Other charges:** lockers, caddies, service charges and taxes
6. **Initiation, spread over time:** divide the initiation you pay (less anything refundable) by the number of years you expect to stay
7. **Housing:** the carrying cost of your home in the area, if you are buying or keeping a home mainly to use the club

Line 6 is the one people forget. If you pay an initiation in the $100,000 to $130,000 range most sources cite and stay ten years, that is roughly $10,000 to $13,000 a year before any refund. Stay five years and it doubles.

## How Whisper Rock’s annual cost compares

Published estimates suggest Whisper Rock’s ongoing cost sits below several of its best-known neighbors. All of these are third-party estimates, not club figures.

- **Silverleaf Club:** recent estimates put Golf Membership dues at roughly $2,750 to $3,900 a month, or about $33,000 to $47,000 a year.
- **Scottsdale National Golf Club:** annual dues are most often reported at around $60,000, with some sources citing more than $80,000.
- **Desert Mountain Club:** third-party estimates put monthly dues at roughly $1,300 to $2,250, or about $15,700 to $26,900 a year, for a club with seven courses.

On dues alone, the most-cited Whisper Rock figure is in the same general range as Desert Mountain and well below Silverleaf and Scottsdale National. Keep in mind that the models differ. Desert Mountain and Silverleaf are clubs inside residential communities, so their members are usually also paying HOA assessments in the same community. Whisper Rock members pay for housing separately, wherever they live.

## Is Whisper Rock worth what it costs?

That depends almost entirely on how much you play. Whisper Rock is a golf club first. If you play often, value two courses by well-known designers and want a club culture built around serious golf, published estimates suggest the annual cost is moderate compared with other top Scottsdale clubs.

If you play occasionally, or what you really want is a dining and social club close to home, Whisper Rock is less likely to be a good value. A club inside your own community, with social or lifestyle membership tiers, may serve you better.

A useful test: divide your estimated annual cost (dues plus extras, plus initiation spread over your expected stay) by the number of rounds you realistically expect to play. Compare that cost per round with what you would pay elsewhere.

## Questions to ask before you join

- What are the current dues, and how have they changed over the last several years?
- What do dues include, and what is billed separately?
- Is there a food and beverage minimum?
- Are any assessments planned or being paid now?
- What are the guest policies and fees?
- Who owns the club, and how are decisions about dues and assessments made?
- What happens to the initiation if I resign or move away?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed professional.
