For many Scottsdale golfers, shopping for golf apparel is part of the fun. A shirt from a famous course, a new pair of shoes before the season or a hat from a trip all become part of a golfer’s story. In a city with this much golf, there are plenty of places to shop.

This guide explains the main kinds of shops, what to wear for desert golf, how dress codes work and how to choose golf shoes. It does not recommend specific stores. Stock and stores change, and the best shop depends on what you need.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Stock, prices, return policies and dress codes change. Confirm details directly with each shop and course before you buy or play.*

## Four kinds of places to shop

**1. Pro shops at clubs and courses.** Nearly every course and club has a pro shop with apparel, shoes, balls and accessories. Many carry logo apparel from the course itself, which golfers love to collect. At private clubs, pro shops often carry premium brands and offer member charging. Pro shop staff can also advise on dress codes.

**2. Large golf retailers.** Big-box golf stores offer the widest selection of brands, sizes and shoes in one place, along with clubs, balls and often fitting services.

**3. Resort and specialty boutiques.** Resort shops and specialty golf boutiques carry curated, often premium, apparel and accessories, and are good for gifts.

**4. Online.** Online stores offer selection and convenience, though fit can be harder to judge. Check return policies, especially for shoes.

## Dressing for desert golf

**Warm months.** Scottsdale is hot for much of the year. Look for:

- **Breathable, moisture-wicking fabrics** that dry quickly
- **Sun protection**, including clothing with a UPF rating, long sleeves or sleeves for arms
- **A good hat.** Wide brims protect the face and neck. Caps and visors are standard, too.
- **Sunglasses** that reduce glare without hiding the ball
- **Light colors**, which reflect heat

**Winter mornings.** Desert mornings in winter can be cool, even when the afternoon is warm. Layer:

- **A light base layer**
- **A quarter-zip or vest**
- **A wind layer** for breezy days

You may shed layers by the back nine.

**Summer extremes.** In the hottest months, play early. Many golfers wear cooling towels and carry extra water. Sun protection is essential.

## Dress codes

Many courses, especially private clubs, have dress codes. Common rules include:

- **Collared shirts** or approved mock necks for men
- **No denim**
- **Tailored shorts** of a certain length
- **Hats worn forward**, and removed indoors at some clubs
- **Golf shoes only** on the course, and sometimes rules about shoes in the clubhouse

Women’s dress codes vary by club. If you are a guest, ask your host. If you are visiting a public course, check its website or ask when you book.

## Golf shoes

**Spiked shoes** use replaceable cleats, usually soft or plastic. Many courses do not allow metal spikes. Spiked shoes offer more grip, which some golfers prefer on slopes, wet grass or in a fast swing.

**Spikeless shoes** have molded traction on the sole. They are comfortable and versatile, and many golfers wear them on and off the course.

**Waterproofing** matters less in the desert than elsewhere, but early-morning dew and watered fairways can still make shoes wet.

**Breathability** matters more. In Scottsdale heat, a breathable upper keeps feet cooler.

## Getting fitted for shoes

- **Shop later in the day**, when feet are slightly larger.
- **Wear the socks you play in.**
- **Check length, width and heel hold.** Your heel should not slip.
- **Take practice swings** in the shop if possible, and walk around.
- **Think about walking.** If you walk the course, comfort over 18 holes matters most.
- **Replace worn spikes** regularly to keep traction.

## Accessories that matter in the desert

- **Golf umbrella.** Many Scottsdale golfers carry one for shade, not rain. A light, reflective umbrella makes hot afternoons more bearable.
- **Cooling towels.** Soaked and draped around the neck, they help on hot days.
- **Arm sleeves.** Sun-protective sleeves keep arms covered without a long-sleeve shirt.
- **Gloves.** Heat and sweat wear gloves out quickly. Buy spares, and consider gloves designed for hot or humid conditions.
- **A water bottle.** An insulated bottle keeps water cool through a round.
- **Sunscreen and lip balm.** Reapply during the round.
- **Rain gear.** Rarely needed in winter, but summer monsoon storms can arrive quickly. A light shell is worth keeping in the bag.

## Women’s golf apparel

Selection for women has grown substantially in recent years, with more brands offering sun-protective tops, skorts, dresses and layering pieces. Dress codes for women vary by club. If you are unsure, ask the pro shop or your host. Many women golfers recommend trying shoes in person, since sizing and width vary by brand.

## Juniors and grandchildren

Many Scottsdale golf households include young golfers, whether juniors in the family or visiting grandchildren. Children grow quickly, so prioritize comfortable shoes, sun protection and hats over premium apparel. Check whether your club’s dress code applies to juniors, and ask the pro shop about junior sizes and programs.

## Budgeting

Golf apparel ranges from value to premium. A few tips:

- **Spend on what matters most:** shoes, sun protection and a good hat.
- **Buy logo apparel selectively.** It is a souvenir as much as clothing.
- **Watch for end-of-season sales** in late spring and summer.
- **Check return policies** before buying online.

## Building a season wardrobe

For a full Scottsdale season, a practical wardrobe might include:

- Several breathable, sun-protective polos or tops
- Two or three pairs of shorts or skirts and a pair of lightweight trousers
- A quarter-zip, vest and light wind jacket for winter mornings
- Two pairs of golf shoes, so one can dry between rounds
- A couple of hats and a pair of quality sunglasses
- Golf gloves, including spares, since heat and sweat wear them out quickly

## Dressing for the clubhouse and after the round

Many golfers head straight from the course to lunch, a wine bar or a club dinner. A few tips:

- **Keep a change of shoes** in the car or locker. Many clubhouses and restaurants prefer you not wear golf shoes inside.
- **Pack a fresh shirt** for after a hot round.
- **Check clubhouse dress codes,** which can be stricter than on-course rules, especially in dining rooms in the evening.
- **Bring a light layer** for air-conditioned rooms.

## Getting fitted for more than shoes

Many golf retailers and some pro shops offer fitting services for clubs, balls and gloves as well as shoes. If you are investing in new equipment, a fitting can help you choose gear that suits your swing. Ask what the fitting includes, how long it takes and whether there is a fee or credit toward a purchase.

## Shopping as a visitor

If you are in Scottsdale for a golf trip, a few tips:

- **Pack light and buy local** if you plan to bring home logo apparel.
- **Check airline baggage limits** before you shop, especially if you are also flying with clubs.
- **Ship purchases home** if the shop offers it.

## Caring for gear in the sun

- **Rotate shoes** so they dry between rounds.
- **Do not leave shoes, gloves or clothing in a hot car.** Heat can damage materials and adhesives.
- **Wash technical fabrics** on gentle cycles and air dry.
- **Replace gloves often.** They wear out faster in heat.
- **Clean shoes after rounds** to remove dust and grass.

## Secondhand and donating

Golf apparel and shoes often have plenty of life left when golfers upgrade. Consider donating gently used items or passing them to junior golf programs, and check local resale options for higher-end pieces.

## Logo apparel and souvenirs

Logo apparel is a Scottsdale golf tradition. Shirts and hats from famous courses, private clubs where you played as a guest, and tournaments make great souvenirs and gifts. Pro shops at public courses are open to anyone. At private clubs, guests can often shop when accompanied by a member. Ask your host.

## Shopping around the season

- **Fall** brings new season stock as part-year residents return.
- **Peak season**, from winter through spring, is the busiest time in shops.
- **Summer** often brings sales on seasonal stock, and quieter shops.
- **Tournament weeks**, such as the WM Phoenix Open, bring special merchandise.

## Questions to ask a shop

1. What is your return or exchange policy, especially for shoes?
2. Do you offer shoe or club fitting?
3. Do you carry wide or narrow shoe sizes?
4. Can you order a size or color you do not have in stock?
5. Do you know the dress code at the course I am playing?

*Important: Stock, prices, return policies and dress codes change. Confirm details directly with each shop and course before you buy or play.*
