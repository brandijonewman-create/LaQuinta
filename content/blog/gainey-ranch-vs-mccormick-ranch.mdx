Most of Scottsdale’s famous golf communities sit far to the north, in the high desert. Two of the most established sit right in the middle of the city: Gainey Ranch and McCormick Ranch. They are close to each other, they share a surprising amount of history, and buyers often consider both.

They are also very different places to live. This article compares them side by side. For a full guide to Gainey Ranch on its own, see our [Gainey Ranch, Scottsdale guide](/blog/gainey-ranch-scottsdale-guide-2026).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees are published snapshots shown as ranges, and sources sometimes disagree. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## At a glance

**McCormick Ranch**

- Developed from 1972 by Kaiser-Aetna
- Roughly 4,200 acres, according to one source
- About 67 subdivisions and more than 27,000 residents
- McCormick Ranch Golf Club: Palm and Pine courses, designed by Desmond Muirhead, opened in 1972
- Lakes and greenbelt paths
- Open master-planned community

**Gainey Ranch**

- Developed from the mid-1980s after Markland Properties bought the land in 1980
- Roughly 560 to 640 acres, depending on the source
- Roughly 18 to 19 individually gated neighborhoods
- Gainey Ranch Golf Club: private, 27 holes in three nines (the Lakes, the Dunes and the Arroyo), owned by Invited
- Walled, with staffed gates
- Hyatt Regency Scottsdale Resort and Spa nearby

## Two ranches with a shared history

Both communities began as Arabian horse ranches.

**McCormick Ranch** was owned by Anne and Fowler McCormick, who began buying the land in 1943 and ran it as a cattle and Arabian horse ranch. Kaiser-Aetna bought the property around 1970 for a reported $12.1 million. Sources differ slightly on the year. The property owners association was incorporated in 1972, and the community was built out between 1972 and 1989.

**Gainey Ranch** was the ranch of Daniel C. Gainey, known for raising Arabian horses. Sources also describe cattle and other agricultural operations. Markland Properties bought land from Gainey in 1980, and development of the master-planned community began in the mid-1980s. The original Gainey house later became the community’s Estate Club.

That shared heritage is part of what gives central Scottsdale its character: wide, planned communities built on former ranch land, with lakes and mature landscaping rather than open desert.

## The golf

The two golf clubs work very differently.

**McCormick Ranch Golf Club** has two 18-hole courses, the Palm and the Pine, both designed by Desmond Muirhead and opened in 1972. The club describes the courses as available to golfers, so you do not need to own a home in McCormick Ranch to play. Check the club’s current access policies and rates.

**Gainey Ranch Golf Club** is private, with 27 holes in three nines: the Lakes, the Dunes and the Arroyo. One source dates its opening to 1985. It is owned by Invited, formerly ClubCorp, so it is a non-equity club: members pay to use it but do not own it. Membership is separate from home ownership.

**What this means for you:** if you want to play regularly without joining a private club, McCormick Ranch’s model may suit you better. If you want a private club with a membership roster and club life, Gainey Ranch offers that, for an initiation and dues. Ask Gainey Ranch Golf Club about current categories, fees and availability.

## Security and setting

**Gainey Ranch** is a walled community with staffed gates. Sources describe 24-hour security, roving patrols and cameras, and each neighborhood inside is individually gated. For buyers who put security first, especially part-year owners, this is a major draw.

**McCormick Ranch** is a large, open master-planned community made up of many subdivisions. Some individual subdivisions may be gated, but the community as a whole is not walled in the same way. In exchange, it offers lakes, greenbelts and roughly 25 miles of paths, according to one source. Sources count anywhere from 7 to 11 lakes.

The feel is different as a result. Gainey Ranch is more private and enclosed. McCormick Ranch is more open and connected, with neighborhoods linked by paths and parks.

## Home prices

Here is what published data show. These come from different sources and dates.

**McCormick Ranch**

- Median sale price: about $875,000 in October 2024, according to one source
- Median sale price: about $900,000 in another, undated source
- Median single-family sale price: about $1.2 million in the same source
- Median townhouse sale price: about $800,000 in the same source

**Gainey Ranch**

- Median sale price: about $1.5 million in July 2025, according to one source
- Median home value: about $1.2 million in mid-2025, according to another
- Median single-family sale price: about $1.7 million in a third source
- Median townhouse sale price: about $800,000 in the same source

**The pattern:** Gainey Ranch’s single-family homes tend to sell well above McCormick Ranch’s, while townhouse medians are similar in the sources we found. McCormick Ranch’s size and number of subdivisions give it a wider spread of prices and home types. Compare similar homes, not community-wide medians.

## HOA costs

Both communities have layered associations, so most homes pay more than one assessment.

**Gainey Ranch** published examples include about $215 to $348 a month at Sunset Cove and about $225 to $719 a month at The Pavilions. Staffing gates and security patrols adds to the cost.

**McCormick Ranch** has a property owners association, incorporated in 1972, plus subdivision associations. We did not find a reliable published range for its assessments. Ask for the full disclosure for any home.

In both communities, ask what each association covers, how healthy its reserves are and whether any special assessments are pending.

## Home types

**McCormick Ranch** offers a wide range across its roughly 67 subdivisions, from condos and townhomes to single-family homes, many dating from the 1970s and 1980s. Its size means more choice in every price band.

**Gainey Ranch** offers condos, townhomes, single-family homes and estate homes within its gated neighborhoods, generally built from the mid-1980s onward.

In both, older homes may need updating. Check roofs, plumbing, air conditioning and windows, and price any updates before you negotiate.

## Location

The two communities are close to each other in central Scottsdale, so their locations are broadly similar: close to shopping, dining, medical care and the resort corridor, and a shorter drive to the airport and Old Town Scottsdale than the far-north communities. The differences come down to the specific home and its distance from main roads, the golf course, lakes or the resort.

## For part-year owners

Both communities attract part-year owners, for different reasons.

**Gainey Ranch** offers staffed gates and patrols, which many owners value while they are away, plus condos and townhomes where the association handles more of the exterior.

**McCormick Ranch** offers a wide choice of condos and townhomes at a range of prices, and no private club bill. Without a community-wide staffed gate, plan for regular home checks and consider monitoring.

In either community, confirm what your association maintains while you are away, check rental rules if you plan to rent, and plan for someone to check the home, pool and air conditioning in summer.

## Daily life compared

**A typical morning in McCormick Ranch** might mean a walk or ride along the greenbelt paths, past the lakes, before heading out for errands nearby. The community’s scale means neighbors, parks and paths are a big part of daily life.

**A typical morning in Gainey Ranch** might mean a quiet walk inside the gates, a workout at the Estate Club or a round on one of the three nines, with the resort nearby for lunch. Life tends to feel more private and contained.

Neither is better. They are simply different ways of living in central Scottsdale.

## Who each suits

**McCormick Ranch tends to suit buyers who:**

- Want a wide choice of home types and prices
- Value lakes, paths and an open, connected community
- Want golf nearby without a private club
- Are comfortable without a community-wide staffed gate

**Gainey Ranch tends to suit buyers who:**

- Put guard-gated security first
- Want a private 27-hole club close by, with membership optional
- Like having a resort nearby
- Are comfortable with higher prices and layered HOA costs

## How to decide

1. **Decide how much the gate matters.** If staffed security is essential, Gainey Ranch is the clearer fit.
2. **Decide how you want to golf.** Private club or open access?
3. **Set your budget by home type.** Compare similar homes in each community.
4. **Get the HOA documents** for every association.
5. **Walk both communities.** Spend an evening on McCormick Ranch’s paths and drive through Gainey Ranch’s gates to see which feels right.
