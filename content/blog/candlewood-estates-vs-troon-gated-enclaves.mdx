Candlewood Estates is one of the best-known gated enclaves in North Scottsdale. But a buyer looking for a gated home in the Troon area has more options than Candlewood alone. Nearby Troon Village, built around Troon Mountain, has gated neighborhoods of its own, and Troon North includes many other subdivisions.

This article compares Candlewood Estates with those alternatives. For a full guide to Candlewood itself, see our [Candlewood Estates at Troon North guide](/blog/candlewood-estates-troon-north-guide-2026).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees are published snapshots shown as ranges, and sources sometimes disagree. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## Two communities, two golf models

The first thing to understand is that “Troon” covers two distinct communities in North Scottsdale.

**Troon North** is built around **Troon North Golf Club**, whose two courses are open to the public: Monument (Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish, 1990) and Pinnacle (Weiskopf, 1995). There is no mandatory club. Candlewood Estates is part of Troon North.

**Troon Village** is a separate master-planned community around Troon Mountain, built around the private **Troon Country Club**. The club’s course was designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish, and its clubhouse of roughly 50,000 square feet opened in 1986 and was renovated for almost $8 million in 2017. One source describes Troon Village as about 1,400 acres, and another cites about 1,300 home sites.

So the choice between a Candlewood home and a gated Troon Village home is partly a choice between public golf nearby and a private club at the center of the community.

## The enclaves compared

**Candlewood Estates (Troon North)**

- Guard-gated, with a staffed gate described as operating around the clock
- Custom homes, typically about 3,000 to 7,000 square feet
- Golf and view lots near the public Troon North courses
- Published medians: about $1.8 million (luxury homes) to $2.7 million (homes with views)
- Sources disagree on size, from 81 homesites to 300 homes

**Gated neighborhoods in Troon Village**

- Troon Village is made up of 12 sub-associations, according to the Troon Village Association
- Gated examples include **Windy Walk Estates**, **Ballantrae Ridge** and **Troon Mountain**
- Other sub-associations, such as Troon Fairways, Quail Ridge, Tusayan, Whispering Ridge, Glenn Moor and Troon Ridge, vary in whether they are gated
- Around the private Troon Country Club

**Other Troon North subdivisions**

- Many subdivisions, including Pinnacle Canyon, Pinnacle Views, Skyline Estates, Balancing Rock, Echo Ridge, Boulder Crest and Pinnacle Ridge
- Gating varies by subdivision
- Published HOA examples include Pinnacle Canyon at about $25 to $330 a month and Boulder Crest at about $58 to $200

## Guard-gated vs. gated

Not all gates are the same.

**A guard-gated community** has a staffed gate. Visitors check in with a person, who can verify guests, contractors and deliveries. Candlewood Estates is described this way.

**A gated community** may have an electronic gate with a code, remote or call box, but no staff. It controls traffic but offers less screening.

We did not confirm which Troon Village neighborhoods have staffed gates. If staffed security matters to you, ask the HOA directly for any neighborhood you consider. Staffed gates cost more to run, which usually shows up in higher assessments.

## Golf: public vs. private

**From Candlewood Estates,** you can play Troon North’s Monument and Pinnacle as a paying guest, with no initiation and no dues. That keeps fixed costs lower and gives you two championship courses close by.

**From Troon Village,** the private Troon Country Club is at the center of the community. Sources say living in Troon Village is not required for membership, and membership is not required to own a home there. A 2022 source said memberships were at capacity. Ask the club about current availability, categories and fees.

If you want a private club’s social life and a membership roster, Troon Village is the natural fit. If you want golf without a club bill, Candlewood and the rest of Troon North fit better.

- Median sale price: about $1.1 million in one source, with single-family homes around $1.5 million

**The pattern:** Candlewood sits toward the top of the Troon area market. Troon Village single-family homes generally sit close to Candlewood’s lower range, while its townhouses pull the overall median down. Compare similar homes, not community-wide figures.

## HOA costs

We did not find published HOA fees for Candlewood Estates or for specific gated Troon Village neighborhoods. What we found:

- **Troon Village townhouses:** about $370 to $410 a month, covering cable television and exterior maintenance, according to one source
- **Troon Village fee examples** listed without neighborhood names range from about $30 to $260, which suggests wide variation
- **Troon North examples:** about $25 to $330 a month at Pinnacle Canyon and $58 to $200 at Boulder Crest

Most homes in both communities belong to more than one association. Ask for the full HOA disclosure, including the master association, the sub-association, reserves and any pending special assessments.

## Setting and homes

**Candlewood Estates** is a custom-home enclave in the high desert near Pinnacle Peak, with golf and view lots. Homes vary widely in age, style and finish.

**Troon Village** surrounds Troon Mountain and offers a broader range, including single-family homes and townhouses across its 12 sub-associations, some gated and some not. One listing describes Troon Ridge Estates as having closer-set lots and a more community-focused feel.

**Other Troon North subdivisions** range from townhomes to custom homes, with varied gating and HOA structures.

## For part-year owners

Gated neighborhoods appeal to many part-year owners, but the type of gate and the HOA’s coverage matter.

- **A staffed gate,** as at Candlewood, screens visitors while you are away. It does not replace regular home checks.
- **An electronic gate** limits through traffic but relies on codes and remotes. Ask how codes are managed and changed.
- **HOA coverage** varies widely. Townhouse associations in Troon Village have been reported to cover exterior maintenance, while custom-home enclaves typically leave more to the owner.

Whichever you choose, plan for someone to check the home, pool and air conditioning in summer, and confirm the rental rules in writing if you plan to rent.

## Questions to ask Troon Country Club

If a private club is part of your plan, ask:

- Is membership available now, or is there a waitlist?
- What categories exist, and what are the current initiation and dues?
- Is the club member-owned or owned by a company?
- What happens to my initiation if I resign?
- Are any assessments or capital projects planned?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed professional.

## Questions to ask any gated HOA

1. Is the gate staffed, electronic or both, and during which hours?
2. How are guests, contractors and deliveries handled?
3. What do the assessments cover, and how much goes to the gate?
4. How healthy are the reserves, and when was the last reserve study?
5. Are any special assessments planned, for example for gate equipment?

## Who each suits

**Candlewood Estates tends to suit buyers who:**

- Want a staffed gate and a custom home
- Want public golf nearby without a club bill
- Value golf and mountain views

**Gated Troon Village neighborhoods tend to suit buyers who:**

- Want a private club at the center of the community
- Want a gated neighborhood with a broader range of home types
- Are comfortable with club initiation and dues if they join

**Other Troon North subdivisions tend to suit buyers who:**

- Want Troon North’s setting and public golf at a lower price point
- Do not need a gate

## How to decide

1. **Decide on the golf model:** public golf nearby or a private club.
2. **Decide on the gate:** staffed, electronic or none.
3. **Set your budget** by home type and compare similar homes.
4. **Ask each HOA** for fees, coverage, reserves and gate details.
5. **Visit at different times**, including weekday mornings and evenings, to see how each gate and neighborhood works.

*Important: Prices, HOA fees, gate arrangements and club availability change, and sources sometimes disagree. Confirm current details with ARMLS data, each association, Troon Country Club and a licensed real estate professional before making any decision.*
