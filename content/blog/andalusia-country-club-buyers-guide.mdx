Andalusia Country Club sits at the southern end of La Quinta, just east of Madison Street, on land that was previously open desert and agricultural plots. It opened in the mid-2000s with a Rees Jones routing and a master plan that emphasized newer construction, modern Spanish-Mediterranean architecture, and large view corridors framing the Santa Rosa Mountains.

Among the La Quinta private clubs, Andalusia is the most architecturally consistent newer-construction community: most homes are post-2005, building heights and palettes are tightly controlled, and the streetscape reads as a single design language rather than a patchwork. This guide covers what a serious buyer should understand before a visit, the course, how membership and access work, the housing stock, how Andalusia compares to its neighbors, and the questions worth asking on a tour. La Quinta Golf Lifestyle profiles La Quinta's private golf communities across the valley, and Andalusia is one of the most cohesive in the portfolio.

## The Rees Jones course

Rees Jones is best known nationally for U.S. Open course restorations, and his original work at Andalusia reflects that idiom: classically proportioned green complexes, fairway bunkering placed for the modern tour player rather than the modest amateur, and a relatively conservative green-to-tee routing. The course is more demanding than The Tradition but more forgiving than the Dye courses at The Hideaway. Conditioning is consistent through the season.

### How it plays

The design rewards a player who thinks about position and green complexes rather than one who simply hits it far. That makes Andalusia a satisfying long-term home course: demanding enough to stay interesting, fair enough not to grind down the mid-handicap golfer who makes up most of a club's membership.

## Membership and access at Andalusia

Andalusia is an equity club. The membership has historically been more family-skewed than at Madison Club or Hideaway, with a more active social calendar and stronger junior programming. Like every La Quinta private, it is materially quieter in the summer months, and it is a member-and-guest operation rather than a resort, there is no outside daily-fee play.

Initiation and dues figures are not published on this site; they change over time and should be confirmed directly with the membership office. ## Homes at Andalusia: what buyers should know

Andalusia's defining real-estate quality is consistency: lot orientations, building heights, exterior palettes, and landscape language are all tightly controlled, and the result is a community that reads as a single coherent piece of architecture from the curb. The dominant home style is modern Spanish-Mediterranean, with newer contemporary California influences appearing in the later phases. Lot sizes are mid- to large-scale; most homes are post-2005 custom builds, with a smaller secondary band of fairway villas as the entry product. Specific pricing should be confirmed with a local specialist against current listings.

## How Andalusia compares

Andalusia lands in the middle of the La Quinta private spectrum: newer and more architecturally uniform than the older clubs, more family-oriented and social than the golf-forward Hideaway or the ultra-private Madison Club, and more accessible on entry cost than either. ## Your pre-tour checklist

Every serious buyer should have answers to the following before or during a visit:

- Confirm the phase your lot is in, the architectural language shifts across phases.
- Ask about junior programming and the family calendar if that fits your buyer profile.
- Verify HOA restrictions on rental usage if you plan to lease seasonally.
- Check afternoon sun exposure, south-facing lots read very differently at 4pm than at showing time.
- Confirm current initiation, dues, and transfer terms directly with the membership office.

## Is Andalusia the right fit for you?

Andalusia is a strong fit for a family-oriented buyer who wants newer construction, tight architectural cohesion, and an active social and junior calendar inside a genuine private club. It is less the answer for a buyer set on a historic streetscape, the lowest entry price in the valley, or a purely golf-forward membership.
