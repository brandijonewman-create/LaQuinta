Scottsdale stretches a long way north. Drive far enough up from Old Town and the city gives way to high desert, boulder fields and mountain views, until it meets the town of Carefree and the Tonto National Forest. That far northern edge, Scottsdale’s true north, is where some of the most celebrated golf communities in Arizona are found.

This guide covers those northernmost communities: what each offers and what living this far from the center really involves. For a guide to every part of the city, see our [area-by-area guide to Scottsdale golf communities](/blog/golf-communities-in-scottsdale-arizona-area-guide).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Community details, club rules, fees and school boundaries change. Verify everything directly and work with a licensed real estate professional before making a decision.*

## A note on the name

You may see the phrase “True North” used loosely for far north Scottsdale. The only community we found with that name is **True North Eight Estates**, a gated Bolte Homes development listed at 13601 N 88th Place in the 85250 ZIP code. Despite the name, that address is in central Scottsdale, well south of the communities in this guide. If you are searching for that development specifically, ask the builder for current details.

## Where the far north begins

There is no official line, but most buyers think of far north Scottsdale as the area north of Pinnacle Peak and up to the city’s edge, much of it in the 85262 and 85266 ZIP codes. The defining features are:

- **Higher elevation** than central Scottsdale
- **Large master-planned communities** built around golf
- **A high concentration of private clubs**
- **Native desert** preserved between homes and courses
- **Longer drives** to everything else

## The communities

### Desert Mountain

The largest community in the far north, at about 8,300 acres in the 85262 ZIP code, bordering Carefree and the Tonto National Forest. Developer Lyle Anderson began it in 1986, and members have owned the club since buying it in 2011. It has six Jack Nicklaus Signature courses, opened from 1987 to 2003, plus No. 7, a par-54 course from 2019. Published elevation figures range from roughly 2,500 to 4,500 feet. Our [Desert Mountain overview](/blog/desert-mountain-scottsdale-az-overview) covers it in depth.

### Desert Highlands

Lyle Anderson’s earlier community, with a Jack Nicklaus course that opened in 1984, built with Bob Cupp as senior designer. Nicklaus Design began a renovation of the course in 2025. Public sources describe club membership as tied to owning property in the community.

### Estancia

A smaller private community at the base of Pinnacle Peak, with a member-owned club built around a Tom Fazio course that opened in 1995. It was named Golf Digest’s Best New Private Course of 1996.

### Mirabel

A private community with a member-owned club and a Tom Fazio course that opened on December 1, 2001. Public sources say Mirabel no longer requires property ownership for membership.

### Legend Trail

A community built around a course designed by Arthur Jack Snyder and Forrest Richardson, with the office of Rees Jones brought in during construction.

### Troon North

The far north’s main public-golf community, near Pinnacle Peak, with Troon North Golf Club’s Monument (1990) and Pinnacle (1995) courses and no mandatory club.

### Clubs without homes

Two of the area’s most prominent private clubs, **Whisper Rock** and **Scottsdale National**, are in the far north but have no residential community of their own. Members live in the surrounding communities or elsewhere.

## The communities at a glance

- **Most golf in one place:** Desert Mountain, with seven courses
- **Oldest Nicklaus course here:** Desert Highlands, 1984
- **Single-course member-owned clubs:** Estancia and Mirabel, both Tom Fazio designs
- **Public golf with no club bill:** Troon North
- **Golf-only private clubs:** Whisper Rock and Scottsdale National
- **Resort setting:** The Boulders, just over the line in Carefree and Scottsdale

Each one has a different model, scale and cost. Two homes at similar prices in two of these communities can come with very different club obligations.

## Just beyond the city: Carefree and Cave Creek

The far north’s nearest small towns sit just past Scottsdale’s edge.

**Carefree** was established as a planned community in 1955. It is home to Desert Forest Golf Club, designed by Robert “Red” Lawrence and opened in 1962, and to part of The Boulders, a resort community with two Jay Morrish courses.

**Cave Creek** sits at roughly 2,200 feet and has a western character and a lively strip of restaurants.

For many far-north residents, these towns are where daily errands and casual dinners happen.

## What living this far north involves

### Distance

The far north’s biggest trade-off is distance. From Desert Mountain:

- **Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport** is roughly 30 miles away, with published estimates of about 45 to 50 minutes of driving
- **Phoenix Deer Valley Airport**, popular with private aircraft owners, is roughly 17 to 18 miles away

Most errands mean a drive, and medical care, large shopping centers and central Scottsdale are farther away than they are from communities such as Gainey Ranch or McCormick Ranch. Before you buy, time the drives that matter to you on a weekday morning in season.

### Elevation and climate

The far north sits higher than the Valley floor. Desert Mountain’s published elevation figures range from roughly 2,500 to 4,500 feet, compared with roughly 1,100 feet for Phoenix. That usually means slightly milder temperatures, though summer is still hot, and wide views across the desert.

### Schools

School assignment depends on the address. Listings for Desert Mountain homes typically name Cave Creek Unified School District, often Black Mountain Elementary, Sonoran Trails Middle School and Cactus Shadows High School. The 85262 ZIP code crosses more than one district, so confirm with the district for any specific home.

### Club costs

Because most golf in the far north is private, many households pay club initiation and dues on top of HOA assessments. Published 2026 estimates for Scottsdale-area private clubs put initiation at roughly $35,000 to $350,000 or more and monthly dues at roughly $800 to $3,500, with the most exclusive clubs estimated above that range. Troon North is the main exception, with public golf and no club bill.

## Far north living for part-year owners

Many far-north homeowners live here only part of the year, often from fall through spring. A few things matter more for them:

- **Full-year club dues.** Private clubs usually charge dues year-round. Ask whether seasonal or non-resident categories exist.
- **Lock-and-leave options.** Attached and patio homes, where the association handles more of the exterior, are easier to leave for months.
- **Rental rules.** Some communities restrict short-term rentals. Desert Mountain, for example, has restricted rentals of less than thirty days since a 2018 amendment that a court upheld in 2019.
- **Summer care.** Plan for someone to check the home, pool, air conditioning and irrigation.

## Summer in the far north

Higher elevation helps, but summer in the far north is still hot. Golf moves to early mornings, social calendars thin out as part-year residents leave, and the clubs and communities feel much quieter. Some residents love the calm. Others find it too quiet. Visit in July before you commit to living here year-round.

## Who the far north suits

The far north tends to suit buyers who:

- Want private-club golf at the center of their lives
- Value privacy, space and desert views
- Are comfortable driving for errands, medical care and the airport
- Plan to stay long enough for club initiation to make sense

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want to walk to shops and restaurants
- Need a short daily commute into central Phoenix
- Want golf without a club bill and do not want Troon North’s setting

## Questions to ask in any far-north community

1. Is club membership required, optional or tied to owning property?
2. What are the HOA assessments for every association the home belongs to?
3. What are the rental rules?
4. What school is assigned to the address?
5. How far is the nearest emergency room and urgent care?
6. What is the community like from June through September?

## How to explore the far north

1. **Spend a full day there.** Drive between communities and into Carefree and Cave Creek.
2. **Visit more than one club.** Private clubs differ widely in culture, scale and cost.
3. **Time your drives** to the airport, medical care and the places you go every week.
4. **Visit in summer** as well as in season.
5. **Get the documents** for any home and club you are serious about, and review them with a licensed professional.
