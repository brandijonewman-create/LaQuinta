If you want to live next to championship golf in North Scottsdale without paying for a private club, two names come up again and again: Grayhawk and Troon North. Both are built around public golf clubs, both span a wide range of home types, and both draw full-time residents and part-year owners.

They are not the same, though. This article compares them side by side. For full buyer’s guides to each, see our [Grayhawk real estate guide](/blog/grayhawk-real-estate-guide-2026) and our [Troon North homes for sale guide](/blog/troon-north-homes-for-sale-2026-guide).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees are published snapshots shown as ranges, and they change. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## At a glance

**Grayhawk**

- Master-planned community of more than 1,600 acres, according to its developer, Grayhawk Development
- Two main areas: The Park and The Retreat
- Grayhawk Golf Club, open to the public since 1994: Raptor (Tom Fazio) and Talon (David Graham and Gary Panks)
- Condos, townhomes, single-family homes, gated enclaves and a life-care retirement community

**Troon North**

- Collection of subdivisions near Pinnacle Peak, within a master plan of roughly 1,800 acres, according to one source
- Troon North Golf Club, public: Monument (Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish, 1990) and Pinnacle (Weiskopf, 1995)
- Four Seasons Resort Scottsdale at Troon North nearby
- Townhomes, single-family homes, custom homes and guard-gated enclaves such as Candlewood Estates

## The golf

Both communities offer two public championship courses, and both are popular with visiting golfers.

**Grayhawk Golf Club** opened in 1994. Raptor, designed by Tom Fazio, hosted the PGA TOUR’s Frys.com Open from 2007 to 2009 and is generally described as the tougher of the two. Talon was designed by two-time major champion David Graham and architect Gary Panks.

**Troon North Golf Club** opened Monument in 1990, designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish, and added Pinnacle in 1995, a solo Weiskopf design. Both are high-desert courses framed by granite boulders, with views of Pinnacle Peak. Troon North was one of the first high-end public golf facilities in Scottsdale.

**For residents,** the model is the same: you pay to play, like any other golfer, and there is no initiation or monthly club bill. Ask each club about any resident or frequent-player programs.

## Setting and feel

This is where the two differ most.

**Grayhawk** sits closer to the center of North Scottsdale. It feels like a planned suburban community with golf at its heart: neighborhoods, shopping and services nearby, and a wide mix of housing. Many buyers describe it as an everyday neighborhood.

**Troon North** sits farther north, near Pinnacle Peak. It feels more like the high desert: boulders, saguaros and wide views toward the mountains. The Four Seasons resort adds a resort presence.

If you want convenience, Grayhawk usually wins. If you want the desert, Troon North usually wins.

## Home prices

Published data come from different sources and dates, so treat these as ranges.

**Grayhawk**

- Median sale price: about $805,000 overall, in one source covering the prior twelve months
- Median single-family sale price: about $1.2 million in the same source
- Median townhouse sale price: about $670,000 in the same source
- Subdivision medians: about $474,000 to $1.85 million in published snapshots

**Troon North**

- Median sale price: about $1.1 million in one source
- Median single-family sale price: about $1.5 million in another
- Median townhouse sale price: about $750,000 in the same source
- Listing range: roughly $225,000 to $4.5 million in one snapshot

**The pattern:** Troon North’s single-family homes tend to run higher, and its top end reaches further into custom estates on view lots. Grayhawk offers more choice in the lower and middle price bands, especially condos and townhomes. In both, compare similar homes in specific subdivisions.

## HOA costs

Both communities have layered associations, so most homes pay more than one assessment.

**Grayhawk** published examples:

- Many single-family subdivisions in The Park: about $54 to $125 a month
- Condo and townhome communities: about $250 to $450 a month

**Troon North** published examples:

- Pinnacle Canyon: about $25 to $330 a month
- Boulder Crest: about $58 to $200 a month

Guard-gated enclaves usually cost more because of gate staffing. Ask for the full HOA disclosure for any home.

## Home types

**Grayhawk** has the broader mix: condos, townhomes, single-family homes on compact and larger lots, gated enclaves such as Tesoro and Encore at Grayhawk, and a life-care retirement community. That makes it a strong choice for first-time Scottsdale buyers, downsizers and families.

**Troon North** has townhomes and attached homes at the entry level, a large middle market of single-family homes, and a strong custom-home segment, including guard-gated Candlewood Estates. It suits buyers who want a custom home or a view lot.

## Location and commute

Grayhawk’s more central position generally means shorter drives to shopping, medical care, Old Town Scottsdale and the airport. Troon North’s position near Pinnacle Peak adds time to most errands but puts you closer to the high desert and the far-north communities. Time the drives that matter to you from any specific home.

## Schools

School assignment depends on the address in both communities. Confirm with the district for any home you are considering.

## For part-year owners

Both communities are popular with part-year owners, because neither has a mandatory club and both offer attached homes.

**In Grayhawk,** condo and townhome communities with higher HOA fees often cover more of the exterior, which suits owners who are away for months. Gated enclaves add security.

**In Troon North,** townhomes and attached homes offer lock-and-leave options, while guard-gated enclaves such as Candlewood Estates add staffed security for single-family owners.

In either, confirm what the HOA maintains while you are away, check rental rules in writing, and plan for someone to check the home, pool and air conditioning in summer.

## Golf-course lots compared

Both communities have homes along their courses. The trade-offs are similar in both: open views and a sense of space, balanced against golf traffic, carts, maintenance crews and the occasional stray ball. What differs is the setting around the course. Grayhawk’s courses run through a planned suburban community. Troon North’s run through boulders and high desert with views toward Pinnacle Peak. Golf-course homes in both usually sell at a premium over similar homes away from the course, so compare the two before you decide the view is worth it.

Walk the holes near any home you are considering, and stand in the backyard during busy tee times.

## Questions to ask in either community

1. Which subdivision is the home in, and what associations does it belong to?
2. What do the assessments cover, and how healthy are the reserves?
3. What are the rental rules?
4. What approvals are needed for exterior changes?
5. How have similar homes in the same subdivision sold recently?
6. Is the home on the golf course, and which hole?
7. What school is assigned to the address?

## Resale considerations

Grayhawk’s wide range of home types means a broad buyer pool, from first-time Scottsdale buyers to families and downsizers. Troon North’s strength at the custom-home and view-lot end appeals to a narrower but often well-funded buyer pool. In both, homes that are updated, well-positioned and priced in line with recent subdivision sales tend to sell most smoothly. Ask your agent how long similar homes have taken to sell.

## Who each suits

**Grayhawk tends to suit buyers who:**

- Want golf nearby and a central North Scottsdale location
- Want the widest choice of home types and prices
- Want condo or townhome options for part-year ownership
- Value an everyday neighborhood feel

**Troon North tends to suit buyers who:**

- Want a high-desert setting near Pinnacle Peak
- Want custom homes or view lots
- Like having a resort nearby
- Do not mind longer drives

## The cost of skipping a private club

For many buyers, the biggest financial advantage of both communities is what they do not cost. Published 2026 estimates for Scottsdale-area private clubs put initiation at roughly $35,000 to $350,000 or more and monthly dues at roughly $800 to $3,500. A household in Grayhawk or Troon North pays none of that and simply pays green fees when it plays. For occasional golfers, that difference can be substantial over a decade.

## Owning in both?

Some golfers simply play both clubs. Living in either community puts the other within a reasonable drive, so you can enjoy four public championship courses without any private club. That is one of the strongest arguments for public-golf living in North Scottsdale.

## How to decide

1. **Pick the setting first:** suburban convenience or high desert.
2. **Set your budget by home type** and compare similar homes in specific subdivisions.
3. **Play both golf clubs** before you buy.
4. **Drive the commute** at the times you would actually travel.
5. **Get the HOA documents** for every association the home belongs to.

*Important: Prices, HOA fees and community details are published snapshots and change often. Confirm current figures with ARMLS data, each association and a licensed real estate professional before making any decision.*
