Scottsdale is one of the best golf destinations in the world, so why leave? For many residents, the answer is the calendar. When Arizona summer arrives, the best golf in the Northern Hemisphere is just getting started, from the links of Scotland and Ireland to mountain courses and ocean-side layouts reached by sea.

This guide covers how Scottsdale golfers plan trips farther afield, including golf-focused cruises. It does not recommend specific tour operators or cruise lines. Offerings change every season.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Flight schedules, cruise itineraries, prices and policies change. Confirm everything directly with airlines, cruise lines, courses and tour operators, and consider working with a licensed travel advisor and insurance professional.*

## Why summer is golf-travel season

Scottsdale’s golf calendar runs roughly opposite to much of the world’s. The low desert is at its best from fall through spring, and very hot in summer. Northern golf destinations, including Scotland, Ireland, England, Scandinavia, Canada and the U.S. Northeast and Pacific Northwest, are at their best in summer.

That makes summer the natural time for Scottsdale golfers to travel. It also means your home club may be quieter while you are away, which is a good time to be somewhere else.

## Getting there from Phoenix

Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport is the main gateway for Scottsdale residents. Current nonstop international service includes:

- **London,** with British Airways, which marked about 30 years of Phoenix service in 2026
- **Paris,** with Air France
- **Taipei,** with STARLUX, which began service in January 2026
- **Several cities in Canada and Mexico**

**Scotland and Ireland.** We did not find a nonstop route from Phoenix to Edinburgh or Dublin in current sources. A common approach is a nonstop to London followed by a short connection, or a connection through a U.S. hub. Check airline schedules, since routes and seasons change.

**Private aviation.** Some golfers fly private from Scottsdale Airport, a city-owned general aviation airport with a single 8,249-foot runway. Our guide to private jet charters for golfers covers that option.

## Planning a links trip

Scotland and Ireland are the dream trips for many golfers. A few planning points:

- **Book famous courses early.** Many of the best-known links courses have limited visitor tee times, and some use ballots or advance booking windows. Plan well ahead.
- **Check handicap requirements.** Some courses ask visitors to show a handicap certificate. Keep your handicap current through your Arizona association.
- **Plan for weather.** Even in summer, wind and rain are part of the experience. Pack waterproofs.
- **Consider a caddie.** Local caddies add a great deal to links golf.
- **Use a driver or tour.** Distances and narrow roads can make driving yourself tiring after a round.

## Golf cruises

A golf cruise combines time at sea with golf at courses near ports of call. Common formats include:

- **Shore excursions to golf courses,** arranged by the cruise line or an independent operator
- **Group golf cruises,** where a golf travel company books a block of cabins and organizes tee times at each port
- **Onboard golf features,** such as simulators, putting greens or instruction on some ships

**Ports near Scottsdale.** Arizona has no ocean cruise port. The closest major ports are on the West Coast, in San Diego, Los Angeles and Long Beach, which serve itineraries to Mexico, Hawaii, Alaska and beyond. Many golfers also fly to ports in Florida for Caribbean cruises, or to Europe for Mediterranean and Northern European itineraries.

**Questions to ask about a golf cruise:**

1. Which courses are included at each port, and are tee times guaranteed?
2. What is included: green fees, carts, transfers, club rental?
3. How much time is there between the ship’s arrival and departure at each port?
4. What happens if weather or a schedule change cancels a round?
5. Can you bring your own clubs on board, and how are they stored?

## Destination ideas by season

**Summer (Scottsdale’s off-season):** Scotland, Ireland, England and Wales; Scandinavia; Canada; the U.S. Northeast, Upper Midwest, Rocky Mountains and Pacific Northwest. These are at their best when Arizona is at its hottest.

**Arizona high country:** for a shorter trip, Prescott, Sedona and Flagstaff offer cooler summer golf within a few hours’ drive.

**Shoulder seasons:** late spring and early fall can be good times for destinations that are too hot in midsummer, such as parts of the Mediterranean.

**Winter:** most Scottsdale golfers stay home in winter, when the desert is at its best. Some travel to the Southern Hemisphere, where it is summer, but that usually means giving up the best months of Arizona golf.

## Taking a group abroad

Traveling with your regular foursome or a larger group adds planning:

- **Agree on the budget and style** before booking: luxury, mid-range, walking or carts.
- **Book tee times as a block** so the group plays together.
- **Plan rest days.** Back-to-back rounds on firm, windy links courses can be tiring.
- **Assign roles:** one person for bookings, one for transport, one for dinners.
- **Collect deposits early** to lock in commitments.

## A golf travel packing list

- Waterproof jacket and trousers, and a warm layer
- Two pairs of golf shoes, so one can dry
- Rain gloves and plenty of towels
- A copy of your handicap record
- Travel adapters and chargers
- Medications in your carry-on
- Copies of your travel insurance and booking confirmations

## Traveling with clubs

**Checking clubs.** Most airlines accept golf bags as checked baggage, often counted as a standard bag, sometimes with extra fees. A hard case or well-padded travel bag protects clubs. Remove the driver head if it is adjustable, and pack towels around club heads.

**Shipping clubs.** Club-shipping services pick up from your home and deliver to your hotel, course or ship. It costs more but avoids lugging bags through airports. Book with enough lead time and confirm the delivery address will accept it.

**Renting.** Many destination courses rent quality clubs, which is the lightest option, though you lose the familiarity of your own set.

## Travel insurance

Golf trips are often expensive and prepaid. Travel insurance can help. Look at:

- **Trip cancellation and interruption**
- **Medical coverage abroad,** since your regular health plan may not cover you overseas
- **Medical evacuation**
- **Baggage and equipment coverage,** including golf clubs
- **Exclusions,** such as pre-existing conditions or cancellations for weather

Read the policy carefully, and talk to a licensed insurance professional about what fits your trip.

## Passports, visas and health

- **Check your passport** well before an international trip. Many countries require at least several months of validity beyond your travel dates.
- **Check entry requirements** for each country, including any electronic travel authorization. Rules change, so check official government sources.
- **Bring prescriptions** in original containers and enough for the whole trip.
- **Plan for jet lag.** Schedule an easy first day before your first big round.

## Domestic golf trips

Not every trip needs a passport. Many Scottsdale golfers plan summer trips to cooler U.S. destinations, from mountain resorts to coastal courses. Domestic trips avoid passport and long-haul travel hassles, and short nonstop flights from Phoenix reach many of them. The same planning principles apply: book tee times early, plan for weather and consider travel insurance.

## Working with a travel advisor

For complex trips, such as several links courses in Scotland, a golf cruise or a group of eight, a travel advisor who specializes in golf can save time and secure tee times you might not get on your own. Ask:

- How many golf trips have you planned to this destination?
- How do you secure tee times at the best-known courses?
- What is included in your fee or package?
- What happens if something goes wrong while we are traveling?

## Timing around your Scottsdale life

- **Home club dues:** most private clubs charge dues year-round. Summer travel does not reduce your bill, so factor it in.
- **Home care while away:** arrange home checks, pool care and air conditioning settings before you leave.
- **Car storage:** protect cars stored in the garage during summer.
- **Return before the season:** many residents plan to be back by fall, when Scottsdale golf comes alive again.

## Staying connected to your home club

Some private clubs arrange reciprocal play with clubs elsewhere, which can open doors to courses that are otherwise hard to book. Ask your club whether it has reciprocal arrangements in the places you plan to visit, and how to request a tee time through them.

## Budgeting a golf trip

Costs vary enormously with destination, season and style. Build a budget that includes:

1. Flights or cruise fare
2. Lodging
3. Green fees and caddies
4. Ground transportation
5. Club shipping or baggage fees
6. Travel insurance
7. Meals and extras

Get written quotes for packaged trips, and check what is included before comparing prices.
