Scottsdale National Golf Club is often described as one of the most exclusive and most expensive golf clubs in Arizona. It is also unusual in structure. It is owned by one family rather than by its members, it has no residential community in the conventional sense, and its admission process is unlike that of most clubs.

This guide covers what published sources say about Scottsdale National membership cost, how the admission process has been reported to work, who owns the club and what that ownership means for a member’s money.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Scottsdale National does not publish its fees. All figures below are third-party estimates shown as ranges. Verify every figure and term directly with the club and review any agreement with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before you make a decision.*

## What Scottsdale National is

Scottsdale National Golf Club is a private club in North Scottsdale. It was previously known as The Golf Club Scottsdale. In October 2013, Bob Parsons, the founder of GoDaddy and PXG, bought the club and its outstanding debts for a reported $600,000 and renamed it Scottsdale National. He and his wife, Renee Parsons, own it.

Since then, the club has expanded significantly. Parsons has been reported to have invested about $250 million in the property, including a second clubhouse and member villas. The club’s own history notes that the land was once part of a ranching operation that grew to 44,000 acres.

## The courses

Scottsdale National has two 18-hole courses and a nine-hole course:

- **The Mineshaft:** the original course, dating from the club’s years as The Golf Club Scottsdale
- **The Other Course:** a second 18-hole course by Jackson Kahn Design, the firm of Tim Jackson and David Kahn, which opened in 2016
- **The Bad Little Nine:** a nine-hole par-3 course, also by Jackson Kahn Design, which opened in 2016 and is widely described as one of the most demanding short courses anywhere

That gives members three distinct kinds of golf on one property, from a championship round to a short but punishing par-3 loop.

## What published sources say membership costs

The club does not publish its fees. Here is what third-party sources report.

**Initiation**

- **Most commonly reported:** around $300,000
- **Broader range in one guide:** $300,000 to more than $1 million
- **Highest single report:** at or around $1 million

**Annual dues**

- **Most commonly reported:** around $60,000 a year, or about $5,000 a month
- **Highest single report:** more than $80,000 a year

**Admission program**

- **The Xperience:** Golf.com reported that prospective members apply to take part in a program called the Xperience, which costs $30,000

Taken together, a reasonable planning range is roughly **$300,000 to $1 million or more for initiation** and roughly **$60,000 to $80,000 or more a year for dues**. These figures are well above the general range for Scottsdale-area private clubs, where published 2026 estimates put initiation at roughly $35,000 to $350,000 or more and monthly dues at roughly $800 to $3,500.

Golf.com also reported a membership of about 145 members across 45 holes of golf. Membership numbers change, and the club does not publish them, but a small roster spread across that much golf is part of what members are paying for.

## How admission has been reported to work

At most private clubs, you apply, are reviewed and, if accepted, pay the initiation. Scottsdale National has been reported to work differently.

According to Golf.com, prospective members apply to take part in the Xperience, an admission program that itself carries a cost. A course directory describes playing the club as possible only as a member, as a guest of a member, or through the Xperience, which it says is available by application and invitation only.

Because the club does not publish its process, ask directly:

- How do I apply for the Xperience, and what does it include?
- What does it cost today?
- Is any part of the Xperience cost credited toward initiation if I join?
- What happens after the Xperience, and how are membership offers made?

## What a non-equity membership means for your money

Scottsdale National is owned by the Parsons, not by its members. That has practical consequences.

**Your initiation buys use, not ownership.** At a member-owned club, your initiation usually buys a share of the club. At an owner-operated club like Scottsdale National, it buys the right to use the club. You do not own any part of it.

**Refunds depend on the agreement.** Non-equity memberships commonly return little or nothing on resignation. We could not confirm Scottsdale National’s terms from public sources. Ask what happens to your initiation if you resign, and get the answer in writing.

**The owner makes the big decisions.** Decisions about dues, new facilities and the club’s future rest with the owner, not a member-elected board. Some members value that, because a well-funded owner can invest heavily without assessing members. Others prefer a say in how the club is run.

**The owner’s plans matter.** At an owner-operated club, it is reasonable to ask what would happen to memberships if ownership ever changed. Ask whether the membership agreement addresses that.

## What you actually pay in year one

Using the published ranges, a first-year budget looks like this. Replace each line with the club’s figures once you have them.

1. **Xperience:** reported at $30,000
2. **Initiation:** roughly $300,000 to $1 million or more
3. **Annual dues:** roughly $60,000 to $80,000 or more
4. **Food and beverage, guests and other charges:** ask the club
5. **Housing:** wherever you live, since the club has no conventional residential neighborhood
6. **Travel:** if you live outside Arizona, the cost of getting here to play

Spread the initiation over the years you expect to stay to see the true annual cost. A $300,000 initiation over ten years is about $30,000 a year before any refund. Added to around $60,000 in dues, that is about $90,000 a year before other charges.

## Where members live

Because Scottsdale National has no conventional residential community, members live elsewhere, whether in Scottsdale or out of state. If you are buying a home with the club in mind, time the drive from any community you are considering and decide whether you also want a club close to home for dining and social life, which would mean a second membership.

## How Scottsdale National compares

On published numbers, Scottsdale National is at the top of the Scottsdale market on annual cost. For comparison, using third-party estimates:

- **Silverleaf Club:** Golf initiation of roughly $400,000 to $500,000, with dues of roughly $2,750 to $3,900 a month
- **Whisper Rock Golf Club:** initiation most often cited at roughly $100,000 to $130,000, with dues around $15,400 a year
- **Desert Mountain Club:** initiation estimates of roughly $100,000 to $200,000 or more depending on category, with dues of roughly $1,300 to $2,250 a month

Silverleaf’s estimated initiation can exceed Scottsdale National’s most-cited figure, but Scottsdale National’s reported dues are far higher than any other club on this list. The models also differ: Silverleaf and Desert Mountain are clubs inside residential communities, Whisper Rock is a golf club with no homes, and Scottsdale National is an owner-operated club with a small membership.

## Questions to ask before you join

Get written answers to these questions and review them with a licensed professional:

1. What does the Xperience cost today, and is any of it credited toward initiation?
2. What is the current initiation fee, and does it cover an individual or a household?
3. What are the current annual dues, and how have they changed in recent years?
4. Is there a food and beverage minimum or any other required spending?
5. What are the guest policies and fees?
6. What happens to my initiation if I resign?
7. Can my membership be transferred, sold or passed to family?
8. What does the membership agreement say about a future change in ownership?
9. How do member villas work, and who can use them?

## Who Scottsdale National suits

Scottsdale National tends to suit members who want a very small, very private club with a great deal of golf, value an owner who invests heavily in the property, and are comfortable with a non-equity structure and the highest reported dues in the area. It is a poor fit for anyone who wants a share of ownership, a say in how the club is run or a club close to a residential community with family amenities.
