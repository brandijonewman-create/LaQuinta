The Hideaway is a 36-hole private community on Hideaway Way in central La Quinta, and its defining feature is rare in the Coachella Valley: two complete 18-hole routings behind a single gate, one by Pete Dye and one by Clive Clark. Members move freely between the two courses, which gives the club an unusual breadth of golf for its size and lets two architectural personalities sit side by side in productive contrast — Dye's visual edge against Clark's more open, member-friendly lines.

The real-estate footprint is mid-scale, the architecture is cohesive, and the membership feels notably tighter than at the larger resort-scale Coachella Valley clubs despite the comparable course count. This guide covers what a serious buyer should understand before scheduling a visit — the two courses, how membership and access work, what the housing stock looks like, how The Hideaway compares to its neighbors, and the questions worth asking on a tour. La Quinta Golf Lifestyle profiles La Quinta's private golf communities across the valley, and The Hideaway is one of the most distinctive in the portfolio.

## The two courses behind one gate

The Pete Dye course at The Hideaway is the more visually intimidating of the two — bunkers cut hard against fairway lines, water in play on the closing holes, and the visual misdirection Dye built his reputation on. The Clive Clark course, completed slightly later, is widely regarded as the friendlier daily-play option: wider landing areas, more conservative green complexes, and an emphasis on playability. Members typically rotate, but most settle into a personal preference quickly. The conditioning standard across both courses is consistent year-round.

### Dye versus Clark, day to day

The two designs ask different things of the same player. Dye plays visually intimidating from any tee, with hard-cut bunker edges and water in play on the closers; Clark plays as a member-friendly rotation option with wider corridors and more open green fronts. That contrast is the whole point of The Hideaway, and it is worth experiencing both before you decide which side of the property you want to live on — because the lot you buy determines which course you look at every morning.

## Membership and access at The Hideaway

The Hideaway is an equity club with a defined cap that keeps tee-sheet pressure manageable across 36 holes. Like Madison Club, it is not a resort or daily-fee operation, and outside play is limited. The membership skews seasonal, with a higher proportion of active golfers than at clubs known more for the lifestyle than the play. Family programming exists, but the dominant rhythm here is the morning golf round.

## Homes at The Hideaway: what buyers should know

Hideaway homes are predominantly custom-built single-family estates, with a smaller secondary band of fairway homes on more modest lots. The architectural language leans contemporary California with Spanish-Mediterranean accents. Lot sizes vary widely — some of the original Dye-side estate lots are substantial, while the Clark-side homes are more uniformly mid-scale. Inventory turns slowly; a typical year sees a single-digit number of resales across the community, and there is no condo stock. Specific pricing should always be confirmed with a local specialist against current listings rather than assumed.

## How The Hideaway compares

Among the La Quinta privates, The Hideaway occupies a distinct spot: more golf variety than the single-course clubs, but a tighter, more golf-forward membership than the larger resort-scale communities. Its Dye course plays harder than The Tradition and Andalusia; its Clark course gives members a gentler daily option that many single-course clubs can't offer. ## Your pre-tour checklist

Every serious buyer should have answers to the following before or during a visit:

- Ask whether the lot backs onto the Dye or the Clark course — the daily-play character is materially different.
- Verify irrigation and turf policy; conditioning cost is a real line item at 36 holes.
- Confirm access rights to both courses under the specific membership tier you're considering.
- Check remodel restrictions early — the architectural code is enforced.
- Confirm current initiation, dues, transfer terms, and any waitlist directly with the membership office.

## Is The Hideaway the right fit for you?

The Hideaway is a compelling fit for an active-golf buyer who wants genuine breadth of play — two full 18s — inside a club that still feels intimate rather than resort-scale. It is less obviously the answer for a buyer who wants a large social scene, hotel-style amenities, or the lowest entry price in the valley; those buyers may be better matched at PGA West or elsewhere.
