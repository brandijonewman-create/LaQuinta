Our [Desert Mountain buyer’s guide](/blog/desert-mountain-homes-for-sale-2026-guide) covers the golf, the neighborhoods and who tends to buy here. This article covers what happens after you buy: who governs the community, what the rules say, what you will pay every year and what daily life looks like at the northern edge of Scottsdale.

Everything below comes from public sources: Desert Mountain’s own website and published materials, court records, and real estate listing data. Where we could not find a current, reliable figure, we say so rather than estimate one.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. HOA rules, assessments, club fees and school boundaries change. Verify every figure and rule for the specific home with the association, the club, the school district and a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before you make a decision.*

## Three organizations, one address

Owning a home in Desert Mountain means dealing with up to three separate organizations. Buyers who treat them as one often misread their costs and their rights.

**The Desert Mountain Master Association.** This is the community-wide homeowners association. According to community materials, master assessments pay for administration of the master and village associations, plus maintenance and reserves for Desert Mountain Parkway, Desert Hills Drive and the common areas. The Master Declaration is also where community-wide rules, such as the rental restriction, are recorded.

**Your village association.** Desert Mountain is divided into many separate subdivisions, often called villages. Each one has its own association and its own assessment. Some cover front-yard landscaping or exterior upkeep, which matters a great deal for part-year owners. Others cover very little. Two homes at the same price in different villages can have very different monthly costs and very different rules.

**Desert Mountain Club.** The club owns and runs the golf courses, clubhouses, restaurants and recreation facilities. Members bought the club in 2011, and it is now member-owned. It is a separate organization from the HOA, with its own board, budget and membership rules. You can own a home without joining, but you cannot use the club without a membership.

Why this matters: an HOA assessment is tied to the property and must be paid by whoever owns it. Club dues are tied to your membership. When you compare two homes, compare the master assessment, the village assessment and the club cost as three separate numbers.

## What you will actually pay each year

We are not going to give you a single “total cost of living” figure for Desert Mountain, because there isn’t one. The real number depends on the village, the size of the home, whether you join the club and which category you choose. What we can do is tell you which costs to collect and where to get each one.

- **Master Association assessment.** Set by the Desert Mountain Master Association. Get it from the HOA disclosure and statement in escrow.
- **Village association assessment.** Set by your village’s association. Get it from the village association disclosure in escrow.
- **Pending or recent special assessments.** Set by the master and village boards. Get them from board minutes and the HOA disclosure statement.
- **Club initiation fee.** Set by Desert Mountain Club. Get it from the club’s membership office.
- **Club monthly dues and minimums.** Set by Desert Mountain Club. Get them from the club’s membership office.
- **Property tax.** Set through Maricopa County. Get it from the County Assessor and Treasurer records for the parcel.
- **Homeowners insurance.** Set by your insurer. Get a quote for the specific address before you remove contingencies.
- **Utilities, landscaping, pool and security.** Set by the providers you hire. Ask for the seller’s last twelve months of bills, if the seller will share them.

**On HOA timing.** Community materials describe Master Association assessments as invoiced twice a year, due January 1 and July 1. Budget for those two dates, not a smooth monthly figure. Village associations set their own schedules.

**On club costs.** The most recent official fee schedule we found is the club’s 2021 FAQ. It listed initiation fees of $70,000 for Full Golf, $33,750 for Seven Golf and $22,250 for Lifestyle, and noted that fees could change. Third-party websites now cite initiation figures of $100,000 to $200,000 or more for some categories, but they disagree with each other and most don’t give a date. Use them as a reason to ask, not as a budget. The club also notes that wait lists can apply. ## The rules that surprise new owners

### Short-term rentals are restricted

In 2018, the Master Association recorded an amendment to its Master Declaration that generally bars rentals of less than thirty days. An owner challenged it in court. In July 2019, a Maricopa County Superior Court judge granted the association summary judgment, ruling that it had used the correct voting process to adopt the restriction.

The practical result: do not buy a Desert Mountain home on the assumption that it will earn vacation-rental income. If rental income matters to you, ask the association in writing what rental terms are allowed today and what the village documents add, and have a real estate attorney review the answers.

### Plan for design review before you remodel

In master-planned communities like this one, exterior changes such as additions, paint colors, landscaping, solar panels and lighting usually need association approval. Request the current design guidelines and approval process for the village before you buy, especially if you plan to remodel. A renovation plan that ignores them can cost months.

### Club access follows membership, not the deed

Buying a home does not come with golf, dining or fitness access. Membership is applied for separately, comes in different categories and can have wait lists. The club has also promoted listings that include a membership, and memberships for homes in the newer Seven Desert Mountain enclave have been reserved for approved buyers. If the club matters to you, settle the membership question before you remove your contingencies, not after closing.

## Daily life this far north

### Getting around

Desert Mountain sits at the northern edge of Scottsdale, in the 85262 ZIP code, near Carefree and the Tonto National Forest. Phoenix Sky Harbor International Airport is roughly 30 miles away, and published drive-time estimates run about 45 to 50 minutes. Phoenix Deer Valley Airport, which many private aircraft owners use, is roughly 17 to 18 miles away.

Most errands mean a drive. That is part of the appeal for many owners and a real drawback for others. Before you buy, drive from the gate to your usual errands, a grocery store, a pharmacy and the nearest emergency room. Do it on a weekday morning in season.

### Schools

Real estate listings for Desert Mountain homes typically name Cave Creek Unified School District, often Black Mountain Elementary, Sonoran Trails Middle School and Cactus Shadows High School. The 85262 ZIP code crosses more than one school district, so confirm the assignment for the exact address with the district before you rely on it. Families who are considering private schools should time the daily drive as well.

### What the club offers

For members, the club describes a campus with seven clubhouses, ten restaurants and grills, a racquet complex with nine tennis courts and eight pickleball courts, and more than 25 miles of private hiking trails. The golf is the main draw: six Jack Nicklaus Signature courses (Renegade, Cochise, Geronimo, Apache, Chiricahua and Outlaw) plus No. 7 at Desert Mountain, a par-54 course designed by club members Bill Brownlee and Wendell Pickett that opened in 2019. Facility counts change as the club renovates, so confirm what is open and what your category includes.

### The seasons

Desert Mountain sits higher than central Scottsdale, but summer is still hot. Many owners spend summers elsewhere, and the social calendar is busiest from fall through spring. If you plan to live here year-round, visit in July before you commit. If you plan to be away for months at a time, ask your village association exactly what it maintains while you are gone.

## Who Desert Mountain living fits

Desert Mountain tends to suit owners who:

- Want golf, dining and social life inside one gate, and plan to use the club enough to justify the cost
- Value quiet, space and desert views over walking to shops and restaurants
- Plan to stay several years, so initiation and transaction costs have time to make sense
- Are comfortable with HOA design review and a no-short-term-rental rule

It tends to frustrate owners who:

- Want to walk to coffee, dining and shopping (Old Town Scottsdale, Kierland or Gainey Ranch fit better)
- Expect to rent the home short-term to cover carrying costs
- Need a short daily commute to central Phoenix or to schools outside the assigned district
- Plan to join the club only occasionally and are not prepared for full-year dues

## Your Desert Mountain due diligence checklist

Collect these during escrow, in writing, for the specific home:

1. The Master Association and village association disclosure statements, including current assessments and any pending special assessments
2. The last twelve months of board minutes for both associations
3. The current Master Declaration, village documents and design guidelines
4. Written confirmation of the current rental rules for that home
5. A current membership quote, category options and wait-list status from Desert Mountain Club
6. Property tax history for the parcel from Maricopa County records
7. An insurance quote for the address
8. School assignment confirmed with the district for the address

None of this is unusual for a luxury golf community. Desert Mountain simply has more layers than most, and the buyers who are happiest here are the ones who collected all of it before closing.
