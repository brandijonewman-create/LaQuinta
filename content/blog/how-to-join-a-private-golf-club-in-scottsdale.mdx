Scottsdale has some of the most sought-after private golf clubs in the country. Joining one is rarely as simple as filling in a form and writing a check. Each club has its own rules about who can join, how long you might wait and what your money buys.

This guide walks through the process step by step. For a look at the courses themselves, see our [course-by-course guide to private golf in Scottsdale](/blog/private-golf-courses-in-scottsdale-guide-2026). For how clubs are owned, see our [club-by-club ownership guide](/blog/equity-vs-non-equity-golf-memberships-scottsdale-clubs).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Membership rules, fees and availability change. Get every term in writing and review it with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before you join.*

## Step 1: Decide what you actually want

Before you contact any club, answer three questions.

**How will you use the club?** Count your realistic rounds per year, how often you would eat there, and whether anyone else in your household will use the fitness, tennis, pickleball or social programs.

**Where will you live?** Some clubs sit inside residential communities. Some require you to own property there. Others have no homes at all. Your housing plan and your club plan are linked.

**How long will you stay?** Initiation fees are large and often only partly refundable, if at all. A club that makes sense over ten years may not make sense over three.

## Step 2: Understand the membership categories

Most Scottsdale private clubs offer more than one category. Common types include:

- **Full golf**, with unrestricted golf and all facilities
- **Limited or alternative golf**, such as Desert Mountain’s Seven Golf category, built around its par-54 No. 7 course
- **Social, clubhouse or lifestyle**, with dining, fitness and social access but no regular golf, such as Silverleaf’s Clubhouse Membership or Desert Mountain’s Lifestyle category
- **Seasonal or non-resident**, at some clubs, for members who live elsewhere part of the year

The category you choose drives your cost. It is worth asking how easy it is to change categories later, and what that costs.

## Step 3: Check the entry rules

Scottsdale clubs use very different entry rules. Here are the main types, with examples from public sources:

**Invitation and sponsorship.** Whisper Rock membership is by invitation and generally requires sponsorship from existing members. At clubs like this, relationships come first.

**Property ownership.** Desert Highlands ties membership to owning property in its community. At clubs like this, you buy the home and the membership together.

**Open application.** Some clubs accept applications from qualified candidates without requiring a home purchase. Mirabel, for example, no longer requires property ownership.

**Application-based programs.** Scottsdale National has been reported to use an application-based program called the Xperience as part of its admissions process. Golf.com reported that it costs $30,000.

Ask each club which rules apply today. They change.

## Step 4: Apply

The application process usually includes some combination of:

- A written application with personal and financial information
- Letters or forms from sponsoring members, where required
- Meetings or interviews with the membership committee or staff
- A review by a committee or the board

Be prepared for the process to take time. At clubs with sponsorship requirements, the review can be thorough.

## Step 5: Wait, or find a faster route

Popular clubs often have waitlists. Reporting from 2024 described a multi-year waitlist for Golf Membership at Silverleaf. Desert Mountain notes that wait lists may apply to all categories.

Ask every club:

- How long is the current wait for the category I want?
- How is the list ordered?
- Is a deposit required to hold my place, and is it refundable?
- Is the initiation locked when I join the list, or set when I am admitted?
- Can I join a social category while I wait?

**Faster routes exist at some clubs.** Desert Mountain has reserved memberships in all three categories for approved buyers of homes in its Seven enclave, without the wait list, and has promoted home listings that include a membership. If you are buying a home anyway, ask whether a home you are considering comes with one.

## Step 6: Read the documents before you pay

Before you pay an initiation, ask for and read:

1. **The membership plan or bylaws**, which set your rights and obligations
2. **The current fee schedule**, including initiation, dues, food minimums and other charges
3. **The dues history** for the last several years
4. **Any information on planned assessments** or capital projects
5. **The rules and regulations**, including guest policies

Pay closest attention to the sections on resignation, refunds and transfers. At some clubs, part of the initiation is returned when a membership is resold. At others, nothing is returned. Our [guide to how equity memberships work](/blog/what-is-an-equity-golf-membership) explains the common models.

Have a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional review the documents with you.

## Step 7: Pay and join

Once you are admitted, you pay the initiation, sign the membership agreement and begin paying dues. Ask:

- Whether the initiation can be paid in installments, and at what total cost
- When dues begin
- Whether there are new-member orientation or handicap requirements

## A realistic timeline

The time from first inquiry to first round as a member varies widely:

- **At a club with open availability**, it can be a matter of weeks once your application is approved.
- **At a club with a waitlist**, it can be months or years.
- **At an invitation-only club**, it depends first on building relationships with members who will sponsor you.
- **When membership comes with a home purchase**, it can align with your closing date.

If timing matters, raise it with each club at the start.

## How much it costs

Published 2026 estimates for Scottsdale-area private clubs put initiation at roughly $35,000 to $350,000 or more and monthly dues at roughly $800 to $3,500, with the most exclusive clubs estimated above that range. These are third-party estimates, not club figures. ## What to look for on a club visit

If the club lets you visit or play as a prospective member, use the time well:

- **Watch the pace of play** on a busy day in season.
- **Ask about tee times.** How far ahead can members book, and how hard is it to get a morning time in February?
- **Look at the practice facilities.** For serious players, the range and short-game areas matter.
- **Eat in the dining room.** It tells you a lot about the club’s culture and how members use it.
- **Talk to members**, not just staff. Ask what they wish they had known before joining.
- **Ask about the calendar.** Leagues, member events and tournaments shape club life as much as the course does.

## Comparing two clubs side by side

When you are choosing between clubs, put the same questions to each and compare the answers on one page:

1. Who owns the club, and how are decisions made?
2. What is the initiation for my category, and is any of it refundable?
3. What are the dues, and how have they changed over the last several years?
4. Are there food minimums, capital charges or planned assessments?
5. How long is the wait, and is there a faster route?
6. What are the guest policies and fees?
7. How many courses, and how hard is it to get a tee time?
8. Does membership require or come with a home?
9. What happens to my membership if I move or sell my home?

Two clubs with similar initiation fees can turn out very differently once you compare dues history, assessments and refund terms.

## If you are relocating to Scottsdale

Buyers moving from out of state often try to choose a home and a club at the same time. A few practical tips:

- **Start the club conversation early**, ideally before you make an offer on a home.
- **Rent first if you can.** A season in Scottsdale lets you play as a guest, meet members and see which club fits.
- **Ask about non-resident or seasonal categories** if you will split time between Arizona and another home.
- **Keep your current club** until your new membership is confirmed, if your current club’s terms allow it.

Our guide to [moving to Scottsdale for golf](/blog/moving-to-scottsdale-for-golf-2026) covers relocation in more detail.

## Common mistakes to avoid

- **Choosing a home before checking the club.** If membership matters, confirm availability before you remove your purchase contingencies.
- **Relying on old fee figures.** Fees at popular clubs have risen sharply. Always check the date.
- **Ignoring refund terms.** Know what you would get back, and when, if you leave.
- **Choosing the wrong category.** A cheaper category that leaves you paying guest fees every time you play can cost more than the one above it.
- **Skipping the documents.** The membership plan, not the sales conversation, controls your rights.

*Important: Membership rules, fees and availability change. Confirm every term directly with the club and consult a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before you join.*
