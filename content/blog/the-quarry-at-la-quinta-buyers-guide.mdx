The Quarry at La Quinta opened in the mid-1990s on a property that had been a working rock quarry. Tom Fazio routed the course through and around the existing topography, using the dramatic rock walls and elevation changes as the visual signature of the design, a different approach from his more polished, view-framed work at The Madison Club. The community is intentionally small, the membership is one of the tightest in the Coachella Valley, and the real-estate footprint is correspondingly modest in absolute terms but high in per-lot value.

This guide covers what a serious buyer should understand before a visit, the course, how membership and access work, the small estate real-estate pool, how The Quarry compares to its neighbors, and the questions worth asking on a tour. La Quinta Golf Lifestyle profiles La Quinta's private golf communities across the valley, and The Quarry is one of the most visually distinctive.

## The Tom Fazio course

Where Fazio's Madison routing is about wide fairways and framed views, his Quarry routing is about topography. The course uses the surviving quarry walls as visual backstops, and several holes play through narrow corridors created by the original excavation. The result is one of the more vertical and visually distinctive courses in the Coachella Valley, not as long as Madison from the tips, but more variable in shot demands.

## Membership and access at The Quarry

The Quarry has historically maintained one of the tightest membership caps among La Quinta private clubs, which keeps the daily-play feel notably more like a small club than any of the larger La Quinta operations. There is no resort access; this is a member-and-guest operation.

## Homes at The Quarry: what buyers should know

Quarry real estate is modest in unit count but skews high in per-lot value. The dominant architectural language is Spanish-Mediterranean with custom California influences; most homes are 1990s-to-2000s vintage with a strong recent renovation wave, and the product is custom single-family only. Inventory turns slowly, year-on-year resale counts are routinely in the single digits, so buyers with a specific brief often work with a licensed partner who can surface off-market opportunities. Specific pricing should be confirmed with a local specialist against current listings.

## How The Quarry compares

The Quarry's closest comparison is The Madison Club, same architect, deliberately different idiom, and, on scale and intimacy, The Hideaway. It sits high on exclusivity and price, with a small-club feel that the larger resort-scale communities can't replicate. ## Your pre-tour checklist

Every serious buyer should have answers to the following before or during a visit:

- Ask about the visual context of each hole from your lot, the quarry walls are a defining element and exposure varies dramatically.
- Confirm recent renovation scope; many homes have been substantially updated.
- Verify the architectural code for any planned addition.
- Ask about member-guest policy timing if you plan to host frequently.
- Confirm current initiation, dues, and member status directly with the club.

## Is The Quarry the right fit for you?

The Quarry is the best fit for a buyer who prizes a small-club feel and a visually distinctive course over broader amenities and community scale. It is less the answer for a buyer who wants a large social calendar, resort amenities, or a deep pool of ready inventory.
