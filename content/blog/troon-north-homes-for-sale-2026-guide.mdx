Troon North is one of the most recognizable names in North Scottsdale, thanks to a golf club whose two courses have drawn visiting golfers for decades. For buyers, its appeal is a little different: desert scenery near Pinnacle Peak, a wide range of home types and prices, and no mandatory club membership.

This guide covers what buyers should know about Troon North homes for sale in 2026: how the community is organized, what published data says about prices and HOA fees, the golf, and what to check before you make an offer. If you are weighing Troon North against a private-club community, our [Troon North vs. Desert Mountain comparison](/blog/troon-north-vs-desert-mountain-2026) covers that decision in depth.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees below are published snapshots shown as ranges, and they change. Verify current figures with ARMLS data and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## What Troon North is

Troon North is a North Scottsdale area near Pinnacle Peak, built around Troon North Golf Club. The club’s two 18-hole courses are open to the public:

- **Monument**, which opened in 1990, designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish
- **Pinnacle**, which opened in 1995, designed by Tom Weiskopf

The golf club is operated by Troon, the golf management company. The Four Seasons Resort Scottsdale at Troon North is also nearby, which adds a resort presence to the area.

Unlike private-club communities such as Desert Mountain or Silverleaf, Troon North has no mandatory club. You can buy a home, never join anything, and still play the courses as a paying guest. For many buyers, that is the deciding factor.

## How the community is organized

Troon North is not one neighborhood with one HOA. It is a collection of separate subdivisions, each with its own homes, price range, rules and association. Subdivisions include:

- **Pinnacle Canyon**
- **Pinnacle Views**
- **Skyline Estates**
- **Balancing Rock**
- **Echo Ridge**
- **Boulder Crest**
- **Candlewood Estates**
- **Pinnacle Ridge**

This structure matters for two reasons. First, the home you buy is governed by its own subdivision’s HOA, not a single community-wide set of rules. Second, two homes at the same price in different subdivisions can have very different HOA costs, rules on rentals and exterior changes, and neighborhood character. Read the documents for the specific subdivision.

## What Troon North homes cost

Prices in Troon North vary widely by home type, size, lot and location. Here is what published snapshots show. These come from listing and data sites at different times, so treat them as ranges, not current prices.

**Across the community**

- **Listing range:** roughly $225,000 to $4.5 million in one snapshot
- **Median sale price:** about $1.1 million in one source
- **Median single-family sale price:** about $1.5 million in another source
- **Median townhouse sale price:** about $750,000 in the same source
- **Average single-family list price:** roughly $2.5 million in recent snapshots, with townhouses averaging roughly $650,000

**By subdivision (published medians and averages)**

- **Boulder Crest:** a median of about $1.85 million, among the highest in the community
- **Pinnacle Canyon:** recent average list prices of roughly $2.1 million to $3.2 million in different snapshots
- **Pinnacle Views and Skyline Estates:** medians of roughly $1.0 million
- **Balancing Rock and Echo Ridge:** medians of roughly $850,000 to $900,000

The pattern is consistent: attached homes and townhouses offer the lowest entry point, mid-size single-family homes cluster around $1 million to $1.5 million, and larger custom homes on view lots reach into the multiple millions. Check current ARMLS data for the subdivision you are considering before you rely on any of these figures.

## The home types you will find

Troon North offers a wider range of home types than most North Scottsdale golf areas.

**Townhomes and attached homes.** The lowest entry point, with published median sale prices for townhouses around $750,000 and some smaller units listed for less. These suit part-year owners and buyers who want less upkeep. Ask what the HOA covers on the exterior.

**Single-family homes on standard lots.** The core of the market, with many sales clustered around $1 million to $1.5 million in published data. Expect a mix of desert contemporary and Southwestern styles.

**Custom homes on view and golf lots.** The top of the market, reaching into the multiple millions. Lot position, views toward Pinnacle Peak and privacy from neighboring homes drive much of the price difference.

**Homesites.** Undeveloped lots come up from time to time. If you are considering building, ask about the subdivision’s design rules, approval process and any time limits on construction.

## HOA fees

HOA fees in Troon North vary by subdivision. Published examples include:

- **Pinnacle Canyon:** roughly $25 to $330 a month
- **Boulder Crest:** roughly $58 to $200 a month

Some homes may also belong to more than one association. Ask for the full HOA disclosure for any home you are considering, including all associations, current assessments, reserves and any pending special assessments.

Because there is no mandatory club, the total annual carrying cost of a Troon North home is often lower than a comparable home in a private-club community. That gap can be significant once you add club dues, which at Scottsdale private clubs commonly run into the thousands of dollars a month.

## The golf

Troon North Golf Club is the reason many people first hear about the area. Both courses are desert layouts routed through boulders, saguaros and washes, with views of Pinnacle Peak.

**Monument** opened first, in 1990, as a collaboration between Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish. It is known for dramatic desert holes framed by granite boulders.

**Pinnacle** followed in 1995, a solo Weiskopf design that climbs toward Pinnacle Peak with elevated tees and wide views.

Because the courses are public, residents pay to play like any other golfer, though the club has promoted a “member for a day” style of experience for visitors. Ask the club about current resident or frequent-player options if you plan to play often.

## Troon North for part-year owners

Many Troon North buyers are not full-time residents. A few points matter more for them:

- **Lock-and-leave fit.** Attached homes and smaller lots usually need less care while you are away. Ask your HOA exactly what it maintains.
- **Rental rules.** If you plan to rent the home while you are away, confirm the subdivision’s rules in writing. They vary across Troon North.
- **Home monitoring.** Plan for someone to check the home regularly in summer, including the pool, air conditioning and irrigation.
- **No dues while away.** Without a mandatory club, you are not paying full-year club dues for months you are not here. For part-year owners, that is often the biggest financial advantage of Troon North over a private-club community.

## Who Troon North suits

Troon North tends to suit buyers who:

- Want desert golf nearby without committing to a private club
- Want a wide range of home types, from lock-and-leave townhomes to custom estates
- Plan to own part-year and want lower fixed costs
- Value desert scenery and a quieter setting in North Scottsdale

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want a private club’s social life and amenities built into the community
- Want a single community-wide set of rules and amenities
- Want to walk to shops and restaurants

## How Troon North compares with nearby options

Buyers looking at Troon North often compare it with two other kinds of North Scottsdale communities.

**Private-club communities**, such as Desert Mountain, Estancia or Silverleaf, build a members-only club into the community. You get a full social calendar and private golf, but you also take on initiation fees and monthly club dues on top of HOA assessments.

**Communities with no golf on site**, such as many neighborhoods around Pinnacle Peak, may offer similar desert settings and lower prices, but you drive to play.

Troon North sits between the two: public championship golf on your doorstep, no club bill, and a wide range of home prices. For a side-by-side look at the private-club alternative, see our [Troon North vs. Desert Mountain comparison](/blog/troon-north-vs-desert-mountain-2026).

## What to check before you make an offer

1. **The subdivision’s HOA documents.** Rules, fees, reserves and any pending assessments.
2. **Rental rules.** These vary by subdivision. Confirm them in writing if rental income matters to you.
3. **Exterior and design rules.** Ask what approvals are needed for changes such as additions, landscaping or solar.
4. **Comparable sales.** Ask your agent for recent sales in the same subdivision and home type.
5. **Views and lot position.** In Troon North, views and privacy can vary sharply from one lot to the next. Visit at different times of day.
6. **Golf course proximity.** Homes along the courses offer views but also see golf traffic. Decide which you prefer.
7. **Schools.** If schools matter, confirm the assignment for the exact address with the district.
8. **Insurance and inspections.** Get an insurance quote and a full inspection, including the roof, pool and any desert landscaping and drainage.
