Troon Mountain is one of the landmarks of North Scottsdale, a rocky peak that rises above the surrounding desert. Around it sits **Troon Village**, a master-planned golf community built around the private **Troon Country Club**. It is often confused with Troon North, its better-known neighbor to the north, but it is a distinct community with its own club, neighborhoods and character.

This guide covers what buyers should know about Troon Village and the area around Troon Mountain.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees are published snapshots shown as ranges, and sources sometimes disagree. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations, Troon Country Club and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## Troon Village vs. Troon North

The two names cause a lot of confusion, so it helps to separate them first.

**Troon Village** surrounds Troon Mountain. It is built around **Troon Country Club**, a private club. One source describes it as a roughly 1,400-acre single-family master-planned golf community, and another cites about 1,300 home sites.

**Troon North** sits to the north, near Pinnacle Peak. It is built around **Troon North Golf Club**, whose Monument and Pinnacle courses are open to the public. It has no mandatory club.

Even some listing sites mix the two up. When you look at a home, confirm which community, and which golf club, it is actually tied to.

## Troon Country Club

Troon Country Club is the heart of Troon Village.

- **The course** was designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish, the same pair behind Troon North’s Monument course and TPC Scottsdale’s Stadium Course.
- **The clubhouse**, of roughly 50,000 square feet, opened in 1986.
- **A renovation** of almost $8 million was completed in December 2017.

**Membership.** Sources say living in Troon Village is not required to join the club, and joining the club is not required to own a home. A 2022 source said memberships were at capacity. That may have changed, so ask the club about current availability, categories, initiation and dues. Our [guide to joining a Scottsdale private club](/blog/how-to-join-a-private-golf-club-in-scottsdale) walks through the questions to ask.

## Questions to ask Troon Country Club

If the club is part of your plan, ask before you buy:

1. Is membership available now, or is there a waitlist?
2. What membership categories exist, and what does each include?
3. What are the current initiation and dues?
4. Who owns the club, and how are dues and assessments decided?
5. Is any part of the initiation refundable if I resign?
6. Are any capital projects or assessments planned?
7. How are tee times allocated in peak season?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional.

## The neighborhoods

The Troon Village Association lists 12 sub-associations. They include:

- **Ballantrae Ridge** (gated)
- **Glenn Moor**
- **Quail Ridge**
- **Troon Fairways**
- **Troon Mountain** (gated)
- **Troon Ridge**, also called Troon Ridge Estates
- **Tusayan**
- **Whispering Ridge**
- **Windy Walk Estates** (gated)

Some neighborhoods are gated and some are not. One listing describes Troon Ridge Estates as sitting in the heart of Troon Village, close to the club, with closer-set lots and a more community-focused feel.

Because each sub-association has its own rules and assessments, two homes in Troon Village can come with very different costs and obligations. Always read the documents for the specific neighborhood.

## What Troon Village homes cost

Published sources give a range of figures from different dates.

- **Median sale price:** about $1.5 million in October 2025, up about 27% from a year earlier, according to one source
- **Median sale price:** about $1.65 million in another source, with single-family homes around $1.77 million and townhouses around $704,000
- **Median sold price:** about $1.9 million in February 2026, according to a third source
- **Median sale price:** about $1.3 million in a fourth source

The spread reflects different dates, different definitions of the community and the mix of townhouses and single-family homes. Ask your agent for recent closed sales in the specific neighborhood and home type.

## HOA fees

Troon Village has a master association plus 12 sub-associations, so most homes pay more than one assessment.

- **Townhouses:** about $370 to $410 a month, covering cable television and exterior maintenance, according to one source
- **Other published fee examples**, not tied to specific neighborhoods, range widely, from under $50 to more than $250

Gated neighborhoods usually carry the cost of their gates. Ask for the full disclosure for every association the home belongs to, including reserves and any pending special assessments.

## Living around Troon Mountain

Troon Village sits in North Scottsdale, south of Troon North. Daily life centers on the club for members and on the surrounding desert for everyone. Troon Mountain itself is the backdrop for many homes, and views of it and of the desert beyond are a large part of the area’s appeal.

For shopping, dining and services, residents drive into the surrounding North Scottsdale area. Time the drives that matter to you from any home you are considering.

## Weiskopf and Morrish in Scottsdale

Troon Country Club is part of a remarkable run of work by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish in the Scottsdale area. Together they designed:

- **Troon Country Club**, with its clubhouse opening in 1986
- **TPC Scottsdale’s Stadium Course**, built to host the Phoenix Open from 1987
- **Troon North’s Monument course**, opened in 1990

Weiskopf later designed Troon North’s Pinnacle course (1995) and Silverleaf (2002) on his own. Morrish went on to design both courses at The Boulders and, farther north, Pine Canyon in Flagstaff. For golfers, Troon Village offers a chance to belong to a private club with a course from one of the most important design partnerships in desert golf.

## Mountain-view and golf-course lots

Within Troon Village, a home’s position often matters as much as its size.

- **Views of Troon Mountain** and the surrounding desert are a major draw. Check what a home actually looks at, and whether future construction nearby could change it.
- **Golf-course homes** along the Troon Country Club course offer open views but also see golf traffic, carts and maintenance crews.
- **Orientation** affects summer heat and afternoon glare on patios and windows.

Visit at different times of day, including late afternoon in summer.

## Troon Village vs. nearby options

**Compared with Troon North,** Troon Village offers a private club at its center instead of public golf. Choose Troon Village if you want club life and Troon North if you want golf without a club bill.

**Compared with Grayhawk,** Troon Village offers a private club and mountain views, while Grayhawk offers public golf and more condos and townhomes.

**Compared with far-north communities such as Desert Mountain,** Troon Village is a single-course club in a smaller community, closer to central North Scottsdale.

## Who Troon Village suits

Troon Village tends to suit buyers who:

- Want a private club at the center of the community
- Value mountain and desert views in North Scottsdale
- Want a choice of gated and ungated neighborhoods
- Want townhouse options as well as single-family homes

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want public golf without a club, which Troon North offers
- Want a single, uniform set of rules across the whole community
- Want a walkable neighborhood close to shops

## Buyer due diligence

1. **Confirm the community.** Make sure the home is in Troon Village, not Troon North, and which club it is near.
2. **Get every HOA document.** Master and sub-association fees, coverage, reserves, rules and pending assessments.
3. **Ask about club membership** before you buy, if the club matters to you.
4. **Check gate arrangements** if security matters: staffed, electronic or none.
5. **Compare recent sales** in the same neighborhood and home type.
6. **Inspect carefully.** Development dates to the mid-1980s, so many homes are decades old. Check the roof, air conditioning, plumbing and pool.
7. **Confirm schools** for the exact address with the district.
8. **Get an insurance quote** for the address before you remove contingencies.

## Townhouse or single-family?

Troon Village offers both, and the choice shapes your costs. Townhouses, with a published median around $704,000 in one source, typically come with HOA fees that cover more, such as exterior maintenance, and suit owners who want less upkeep. Single-family homes, with a published median around $1.77 million in the same source, give more space and privacy but leave more maintenance to you. Compare the total yearly cost of each, not just the price.

## For part-year owners

Townhouses with associations that cover exterior maintenance suit part-year owners well. Gated neighborhoods add security while you are away. Confirm rental rules in writing if you plan to rent, and plan for someone to check the home, pool and air conditioning in summer.

*Important: Prices, HOA fees, gate arrangements and club availability change, and sources sometimes disagree. Confirm current details with ARMLS data, each association, Troon Country Club and a licensed real estate professional before making any decision.*
