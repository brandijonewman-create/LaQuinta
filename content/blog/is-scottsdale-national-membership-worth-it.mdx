Our [Scottsdale National membership cost guide](/blog/scottsdale-national-golf-club-membership-cost-2026) covers what published sources say the club costs and how admission has been reported to work. This article answers the next question: is it worth it, and for whom?

There is no single answer. A membership that makes sense for one golfer is a poor fit for another. What we can do is put the published numbers into practical terms, lay out the trade-offs and show the alternatives.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Scottsdale National does not publish its fees. All figures are third-party estimates shown as ranges. Confirm current terms with the club and review any decision with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional.*

## What you are paying for

Scottsdale National is a private club in North Scottsdale owned by Bob Parsons, founder of GoDaddy and PXG, and his wife, Renee Parsons. Parsons bought the club, then called The Golf Club Scottsdale, in 2013 and has been reported to have invested about $250 million in the property.

Members get:

- **Three courses:** the Mineshaft, the original course; The Other Course, by Jackson Kahn Design, opened in 2016; and the Bad Little Nine, a par-3 course also by Jackson Kahn, opened in 2016
- **A very small membership**, reported by Golf.com at about 145 members across 45 holes of golf
- **Two clubhouses and member villas**, according to reports on the club’s expansion

The small roster relative to the amount of golf is a large part of what members are paying for: access, privacy and little pressure on tee times.

## The cost in practical terms

Using the most commonly reported figures, about $300,000 for initiation and about $60,000 a year in dues, here is what membership costs over time. These are estimates, not club figures, and they exclude food, guest fees and other charges.

**Spread over ten years**

- Initiation: about $30,000 a year
- Dues: about $60,000 a year
- **Total: roughly $90,000 a year**

**Spread over five years**

- Initiation: about $60,000 a year
- Dues: about $60,000 a year
- **Total: roughly $120,000 a year**

**Per round, over ten years at $90,000 a year**

- 50 rounds a year: roughly $1,800 a round
- 100 rounds a year: roughly $900 a round
- 200 rounds a year: roughly $450 a round

Some sources report initiation well above $300,000 and dues above $80,000. If those figures apply, the costs above rise accordingly. The admission program, the Xperience, which Golf.com reported costs $30,000, would add to the first-year cost.

For comparison, one source put peak-season green fees at TPC Scottsdale’s Stadium Course at roughly $339 to $579 in 2025. A golfer who plays 50 rounds a year at that course would spend far less than a Scottsdale National member, though without the privacy, access or amenities.

## Costs the headline numbers leave out

The initiation and dues are only part of the picture. When you estimate value, add:

- **Food and beverage**, including any required minimum
- **Guest fees**, which add up quickly for members who host clients or friends
- **Caddies, lockers and storage**, if billed separately
- **Travel**, for members who live outside Arizona and fly in to play
- **Housing**, since the club has no conventional neighborhood
- **A second club**, if you also want a dining and social club close to home

For an out-of-state member, travel and lodging alone can change the cost per round significantly.

## The non-equity trade-off

Scottsdale National is owned by the Parsons, not by its members. That shapes the value question in three ways.

**You do not build ownership.** At a member-owned club, your initiation typically buys a share of the club, and some clubs return part of it when you leave. At an owner-operated club, the initiation buys the right to use the club. Ask the club what happens to your initiation if you resign, and get the answer in writing.

**The owner decides.** Decisions on dues, facilities and the club’s future rest with the owner. Many members value that: a well-funded owner can invest heavily without asking members for assessments. Others prefer a vote.

**Ownership risk.** At any owner-operated club, it is reasonable to ask what would happen to memberships if ownership changed. Ask whether the membership agreement addresses it.

Our [guide to how equity memberships work](/blog/what-is-an-equity-golf-membership) explains the member-owned alternative in detail.

## Who Scottsdale National suits

Based on its model and reported costs, Scottsdale National tends to suit golfers who:

- **Play a lot.** The cost per round falls sharply for members who play several times a week.
- **Value privacy and access.** A small roster across 45 holes means little competition for tee times.
- **Want a demanding, distinctive golf experience**, including one of the most talked-about par-3 courses in the country.
- **Value an owner who invests.** The club has been reported to have received heavy investment since 2013.
- **Do not need a residential club.** The club has no conventional neighborhood, so members live elsewhere.

## A worked comparison

Consider two golfers, each planning to stay ten years, using published estimates only:

- **Golfer A plays 150 rounds a year.** At Scottsdale National, roughly $90,000 a year works out to about $600 a round. At Whisper Rock, with published estimates of a $100,000 to $130,000 initiation and dues around $15,400, the annual cost is roughly $25,000 to $28,000, or about $170 to $190 a round.
- **Golfer B plays 40 rounds a year.** At Scottsdale National, the same $90,000 works out to about $2,250 a round. Public golf at peak-season rates would cost a fraction of that.

The numbers do not capture everything a member values, such as privacy, access and the experience itself. But they show how much the decision depends on how often you play.

## Who it does not suit

Scottsdale National is a poor fit for golfers who:

- **Play occasionally.** At 50 rounds a year or fewer, the cost per round is very high.
- **Want ownership or a vote.** The club is not member-owned.
- **Want a family or social club close to home.** Clubs inside residential communities offer more of that.
- **Are cost-sensitive.** Reported dues are far above most Scottsdale clubs.

## The alternatives

If Scottsdale National does not fit, Scottsdale has alternatives at a range of published price points. All figures are third-party estimates except where noted.

**Whisper Rock Golf Club.** A golf-first club with two courses, the Lower by Phil Mickelson with Gary Stephenson and the Upper by Tom Fazio. Published estimates most often put initiation at roughly $100,000 to $130,000 and annual dues around $15,400. Membership is by invitation, generally with sponsorship. Like Scottsdale National, it has no homes.

**Desert Mountain Club.** Member-owned since 2011, with six Jack Nicklaus Signature courses and the par-54 No. 7. Third-party estimates put initiation at roughly $100,000 to $200,000 or more by category, with dues of roughly $1,300 to $2,250 a month. It sits inside a residential community.

**Silverleaf Club.** A single Tom Weiskopf course inside a residential community. Recent estimates put Golf initiation at roughly $400,000 to $500,000, with dues of roughly $2,750 to $3,900 a month, well below Scottsdale National’s reported dues.

**Estancia and Mirabel.** Member-owned clubs built around Tom Fazio courses, each inside its own residential community.

**Public golf.** TPC Scottsdale, Troon North, Grayhawk, Talking Stick and We-Ko-Pa offer championship golf with no initiation and no dues. For occasional players, public golf is almost always the lowest-cost choice.

## Getting a feel before you commit

Before you commit to any club at this level, try to experience it first. At Scottsdale National, the reported route is the Xperience, an application-based program that Golf.com reported costs $30,000. At other clubs, playing as a member’s guest is the usual way in.

When you do visit, talk to members about:

- How often they actually play, and whether they feel they get their money’s worth
- How dues and policies have changed since they joined
- What they would do differently if they were joining today
- What the club is like in summer, when many members are away

Members’ honest answers are often more useful than any published figure.

## How to decide

1. **Count your rounds honestly.** Use last year’s rounds, not your ideal year.
2. **Pick your time horizon.** Spread the initiation over the years you realistically expect to stay.
3. **Run the cost per round.** Compare it with what you would pay at public courses or other clubs.
4. **Decide how much ownership matters.** If you want a share and a vote, look at member-owned clubs.
5. **Consider where you live.** A club inside your community may serve your household better than one across town.
6. **Get the documents.** Ask for the membership agreement and fee schedule, and review them with a licensed professional.
