Scottsdale is one of the most popular golf destinations in the country for groups: buddy trips, bachelor weekends, corporate outings, charity tournaments and league rounds. It has a deep supply of public and resort golf within a short drive, reliable winter weather and plenty of places to stay.

Planning golf for a group is different from booking a round for yourself, though. Tee times, pricing, pace of play and logistics all get harder as the group grows. This guide covers how to plan it well.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Green fees, group policies and course details change. Confirm current rates and terms directly with each course. If your trip is tied to a home or club decision, consult a licensed real estate or financial professional.*

## Start with the group

Before you look at courses, answer four questions:

1. **How many players?** Two foursomes and twenty foursomes are very different problems.
2. **What is the skill range?** A mix of scratch players and beginners needs at least one forgiving course.
3. **What is the budget?** Scottsdale green fees range widely by course and season.
4. **What is the format?** A casual buddy trip, a competitive event with prizes, or a corporate outing with food and awards?

Your answers point you toward the right courses and the right booking approach.

## Public courses that work well for groups

Facilities with two courses on site are especially useful for groups, because a larger group can split across both and meet afterward. Here are well-known options, all open to the public.

**TPC Scottsdale.** Home of the WM Phoenix Open. The Stadium Course was designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish and is famous for its 16th hole during tournament week. The Champions Course, credited to Randy Heckenkemper, is usually the less expensive option. TPC Scottsdale says it hosts corporate outings, business meetings, fundraising tournaments and other events each year, and groups can play both courses in one day.

**Grayhawk Golf Club.** Open to the public since 1994, Grayhawk has two par-72 courses: Raptor, designed by Tom Fazio, which hosted the PGA TOUR’s Frys.com Open from 2007 to 2009, and Talon, designed by David Graham and Gary Panks. Raptor is generally described as the tougher of the two.

**Troon North Golf Club.** Two public courses near Pinnacle Peak: Monument, designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish and opened in 1990, and Pinnacle, designed by Weiskopf and opened in 1995. Both are classic high-desert golf.

**Talking Stick Golf Club.** Two courses, O’odham and Piipaash. The O’odham course is a Coore and Crenshaw design. The facility is next to Talking Stick Resort, which is set up to host groups for both lodging and events. One operator says it books back-to-back tee times for multi-foursome groups.

**We-Ko-Pa Golf Club.** East of Scottsdale, We-Ko-Pa is a two-course facility popular with golf travelers.

For a broader list of public options, especially for mixed-ability groups, see our [guide to Scottsdale golf courses for beginners](/blog/best-scottsdale-golf-courses-for-beginners-2026).

## When to go, and what it costs

Season is the single biggest factor in what a Scottsdale golf trip costs.

**Peak season (roughly January through April).** The best weather and the highest prices. One source put TPC Scottsdale Stadium Course green fees at roughly $339 to $579 from February through May 2025. We-Ko-Pa’s published 2026 rates for 36 holes peaked at about $579 from February to April. Tee times fill quickly, especially on weekends.

**Tournament week.** The WM Phoenix Open at TPC Scottsdale draws enormous crowds in early February. Hotels and tee times across the area are in high demand that week. Unless the tournament is part of the trip, plan around it.

**Fall and late spring.** Good weather, more availability and often lower rates than peak season. Some courses close for a few weeks in the fall to overseed their grass, so check before you book.

**Summer.** The lowest rates. We-Ko-Pa’s published 2026 36-hole rates started at about $179 in summer, and one source notes summer Stadium Course rounds can cost less than half of peak season. The trade-off is serious heat. Plan very early tee times.

All of these figures come from published sources at a point in time. Confirm current rates directly with each course.

## How to book for a group

**Up to three or four foursomes.** You can usually book consecutive tee times directly with the course. Book as early as the booking window allows.

**Larger groups.** Contact the course’s group or events team. Ask about:

- Back-to-back tee times versus a shotgun start
- Group rates and minimum player counts
- Deposits, cancellation terms and how many players you can drop without penalty
- Carts, range balls, forecaddies and rental clubs
- Food and beverage packages, scoring, prizes and an awards space

**Using a tour operator.** Several companies package Scottsdale golf, lodging and transportation for groups. They can save time, especially for large groups. Compare their package price with booking directly.

## Sample itineraries

**Three days, two courses a day for the keen.** Day one: TPC Scottsdale, Stadium in the morning and Champions in the afternoon. Day two: Grayhawk’s Raptor and Talon. Day three: Troon North’s Monument and Pinnacle.

**Three days, one round a day for a mixed group.** Pick one facility per day, play in the morning and leave afternoons free for the pool, dinner or a spa.

**East and north split.** One guide suggests spending the first half of the trip near Talking Stick and We-Ko-Pa and the second half up north near Troon North, Grayhawk and TPC Scottsdale, to cut driving time.

## Building a group budget

A simple per-person budget for a Scottsdale golf trip has five lines:

1. **Green fees**, which vary most by season and course. Use the published ranges above as a starting point and get quotes.
2. **Lodging**, which rises sharply in peak season and tournament week.
3. **Transportation**, including airport transfers and getting between courses. For a large group, a van or shuttle can be cheaper and easier than several rental cars.
4. **Food and drink**, including on-course spending and group dinners.
5. **Extras**, such as range balls, rental clubs, caddies or forecaddies, prizes and gratuities.

Collect a deposit from each player early, especially if the course or hotel requires one. It keeps commitments firm.

## Matching courses to a mixed group

Most groups include players of different abilities. A few ways to keep everyone happy:

- **Use the right tees.** Encourage players to choose tees that match their game. Desert courses punish long misses, and forward tees speed up play.
- **Mix the difficulty.** Pair a demanding course such as TPC Scottsdale’s Stadium or Grayhawk’s Raptor with a more forgiving option.
- **Use formats that help.** Scrambles and best-ball games keep weaker players involved and keep pace moving.
- **Plan one lighter day.** A shorter course, a practice session or a day off avoids fatigue on a long trip.

## Where to stay

For a golf group, location matters more than a resort’s brochure. Staying near the courses you will play cuts driving time. Talking Stick Resort sits next to Talking Stick Golf Club and is set up for groups. Resorts and hotels near TPC Scottsdale, Grayhawk and Troon North work well for trips focused on north Scottsdale. For larger groups, rental homes can be cheaper per person and give everyone a common space, but check the rental rules and minimum stays.

## Keeping a group on schedule

- **Set tee times and pairings the night before.** Share them in writing.
- **Build in travel time.** Scottsdale is spread out, and courses can be 30 minutes or more apart.
- **Play ready golf.** Large groups slow down fast. Agree on pace expectations up front.
- **Pick a captain for each foursome** to handle scorecards and keep the group moving.
- **Plan meals.** Book dinner for large groups well in advance, especially in peak season.

## Heat and safety

Outside winter, the Arizona sun is a real risk. For every group round:

- Book early tee times from late spring through early fall
- Carry more water than you think you need, and use the water stations on the course
- Wear sun protection: hats, sunscreen and breathable clothing
- Watch for signs of heat illness and take breaks in the shade
- Make sure everyone knows the cart rules and where to find help on the course

## Golf groups for Scottsdale residents

If you live in Scottsdale and want a regular group, many clubs run men’s, women’s and mixed golf groups, and public courses often host leagues. Ask the golf shop at your nearest course what groups and leagues it runs, and how to join.
