Golf and wine go together in Scottsdale. After a morning round, a relaxed glass in the shade is part of the routine for many golfers, and a special bottle often marks the end of a good trip, a club championship or a milestone birthday.

This guide is not a ranking of wine bars. Venues open, close and change often, and the right spot depends on the moment. Instead, it explains how to choose, what Arizona’s own wine scene offers and how to plan a great wine night for a group of golfers.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Venue hours, policies, prices and menus change. Confirm details directly with each venue, drink responsibly and plan safe transportation.*

## Match the venue to the moment

Golfers tend to want three different kinds of wine experiences.

**The post-round glass.** Something relaxed, close to the course, with shade, a good by-the-glass list and food that works in the afternoon. Dress is casual, and nobody minds if you have just come off the eighteenth green.

**The group night.** Space for a foursome or more, a staff happy to suggest bottles to share, and a menu that works for a mix of tastes. Reservations matter here.

**The celebration.** A deep list, knowledgeable sommeliers and a setting that feels special. This is where you open the bottle you have been saving or try something you would never order on a Tuesday.

The same venue rarely does all three equally well. Decide which one you want before you choose.

## What to look for in a wine bar

**A strong by-the-glass list.** It lets everyone try different wines without committing to a bottle, and it is a good sign that the venue rotates and cares about its list.

**Staff who can guide you.** The best wine bars make it easy to try something new. Tell them what you like and ask for something similar but different.

**Flights.** A tasting flight of three or more small pours is a great way to compare wines, regions or grapes.

**Food that fits.** After a round, many golfers want something more than a cheese plate. Check the menu.

**Setting and comfort.** Shaded patios are valuable in Scottsdale for much of the year. Check noise levels if you want to talk.

**Policies.** Ask about reservations for groups, any corkage fee if you want to bring a special bottle, and dress codes.

## Arizona’s own wine regions

Many visitors are surprised to learn Arizona makes wine. It has three federally recognized American Viticultural Areas:

- **Sonoita,** in southern Arizona, became the state’s first AVA in 1984.
- **Willcox,** in southeastern Arizona, was designated in 2016.
- **Verde Valley,** in central Arizona near Sedona, was designated in 2021.

Many Scottsdale wine lists include Arizona bottles. Ask your server what is local. Trying Arizona wine is one of the easiest ways to make a Scottsdale wine night feel distinctive.

For golfers who like to travel, the Verde Valley sits close to Sedona, which is also home to Seven Canyons Golf Club. Our [guide to Arizona golf communities by season and elevation](/blog/arizona-golf-communities-by-season-and-elevation) covers the high country.

## Ordering with confidence

You do not need to know much about wine to order well. A few simple approaches work almost everywhere:

- **Describe what you like.** “Something dry and crisp,” “a red that is not too heavy” or “like the one I had last time” gives staff plenty to work with.
- **Name a price.** Pointing to a price on the list is a polite, common way to set a budget without saying it out loud.
- **Ask what they are excited about.** Staff often have a favorite bottle that is not the obvious choice.
- **Taste before you commit.** Many wine bars will pour a small taste of a by-the-glass wine before you order.
- **Try a flight** if you are not sure. Comparing side by side is the fastest way to learn what you like.

## Pairing wine with golf-day food

After a round, appetites tend to run toward heartier food. Some easy pairings:

- **Burgers and grilled meats** with medium to full-bodied reds
- **Salads and seafood** with crisp, dry whites or rosé
- **Spicy dishes** with wines that are lower in alcohol and a little fruity
- **Charcuterie and cheese** with almost anything, which is why they are a wine-bar staple

On a hot afternoon, lighter and chilled wines are often more refreshing than big reds.

## Wine trips from Scottsdale

For golfers who want to make a day or weekend of it, Arizona’s wine regions are within driving distance:

- **Verde Valley,** in central Arizona near Sedona, pairs well with a high-country golf trip.
- **Sonoita and Willcox,** in southern and southeastern Arizona, make a longer trip, often combined with Tucson-area golf.

Plan a driver or a tour rather than driving yourself between tasting rooms, and check tasting-room hours before you go, since many are seasonal or open only on certain days.

## What the Michelin Guide recognized

In August 2026, Michelin revealed its first Guide Southwest, covering Arizona, Nevada, New Mexico and Utah. No Arizona restaurant received a Michelin star in that first edition, but several Arizona restaurants were recognized, including several in Scottsdale.

Most relevant for wine lovers, **John Germain of RESERVE in Scottsdale received the Michelin Sommelier Award.** Other Scottsdale restaurants selected for the guide included FnB, which received a Bib Gourmand, and Hush Public House, RESERVE and ShinBay, which were listed as Recommended.

Michelin recognition is one signal among many. Plenty of excellent wine programs are not in the guide.

## Planning a wine night for a golf group

1. **Reserve ahead.** Groups of six or more should always book, especially in peak season.
2. **Decide the format.** A shared flight for everyone, bottles chosen by the table, or a guided tasting arranged with the venue.
3. **Set a budget.** Agree before the night on a rough per-person spend, so nobody is surprised by the bill.
4. **Agree on the bill.** Splitting evenly, by person or by bottle. Decide in advance.
5. **Arrange transportation first.** Book rides or a driver before the first pour.
6. **Mind the next tee time.** If you play early the next day, plan accordingly.

## Budgeting

Wine prices vary widely by venue and bottle. A glass at a casual spot and a bottle from a deep cellar can be very different numbers. A few tips:

- **Ask for a recommendation in your price range.** Good staff are happy to help.
- **Try flights** for value and variety.
- **Ask about corkage** if you want to bring a special bottle, and whether there is a limit.
- **Remember service and tax** when you budget.

## Wine at home after golf

Many golf households entertain at home, especially in communities where the clubhouse is the social center. For home entertaining:

- **Store wine properly.** Arizona heat is hard on wine. A wine refrigerator or climate-controlled storage protects bottles, especially in a garage or casita.
- **Plan for the patio.** Serve whites and rosés well chilled, and keep reds out of the sun.
- **Consider a private chef** for a special dinner, and ask about pairings.

## Wine at the club

At private clubs, the bar and dining room are often the center of social life, and many clubs host wine dinners, tastings and member events. If you belong to a club, ask about its wine program, whether members can store bottles, and whether there are corkage policies for special bottles. For many members, the club wine dinner is the easiest way to try new wines with friends.

## Wine gifts for golfers

A good bottle is a classic gift for a golf partner, a host or a winning team. A few ideas:

- **An Arizona bottle** from Sonoita, Willcox or the Verde Valley, for a local touch
- **A bottle from the year someone joined their club** or won a championship
- **A tasting experience** at a wine bar rather than a bottle

## Wine and the golf season

Scottsdale’s busiest wine months often line up with the golf season, from fall through spring, when part-year residents return and visitors arrive. Weekends and big events book up quickly. In summer, many venues are quieter, and patios give way to cool indoor bars.

## Drinking responsibly

Golf, sun and wine can be a demanding mix. Hydrate between glasses, especially after a hot round, eat something, and arrange safe transportation every time.

## Questions to ask a wine bar before a group visit

- Can you take a reservation for our group size?
- Do you offer flights or guided tastings?
- Is there a corkage fee, and is there a bottle limit?
- Is there a dress code?
- Do you have shaded or quiet seating?
- What Arizona wines do you pour?

*Important: Venue hours, policies, prices and menus change. Confirm details directly with each venue before you go, drink responsibly and plan safe transportation.*
