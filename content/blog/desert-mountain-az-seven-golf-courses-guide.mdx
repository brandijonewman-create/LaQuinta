Desert Mountain is best known for one thing: golf, and a great deal of it. In the high desert at the northern edge of Scottsdale, Arizona, the club has seven courses, six of them designed by Jack Nicklaus. No other private club in the area comes close in sheer quantity.

Our other Desert Mountain guides cover the [community overview](/blog/desert-mountain-scottsdale-az-overview), [membership costs](/blog/desert-mountain-membership-cost-2026) and [buying a home](/blog/desert-mountain-homes-for-sale-2026-guide). This one is about the courses themselves: when each opened, what it is known for and how they work together.

## How the golf grew

Desert Mountain was founded in 1986 by developer Lyle Anderson, who had already worked with Jack Nicklaus at Desert Highlands, whose Nicklaus course opened in 1984. At Desert Mountain, the partnership produced six courses over sixteen years:

The six opened in this order:

1. **Renegade**, 1987
2. **Cochise**, 1988
3. **Geronimo**, 1989 or 1990, depending on the source
4. **Apache**, 1996
5. **Chiricahua**, 1999
6. **Outlaw**, 2003

Then, in 2019, the club added **No. 7 at Desert Mountain**, a par-54 course designed by club members Bill Brownlee and Wendell Pickett.

The club describes its six Nicklaus courses as the largest private collection of Jack Nicklaus Signature designs anywhere. Members have owned the club since 2011, when they bought it from Crescent Real Estate Holdings for a reported $73.5 million.

## Renegade (1987)

Renegade was the first course to open, and it was built with an unusual feature: two greens on each hole. That design gives the club flexibility in how the course is set up from day to day and lets the same hole play quite differently depending on which green is in use.

For a member, Renegade is a good example of why Desert Mountain’s golf does not get repetitive. The same course can feel different across visits.

## Cochise (1988)

Cochise opened the following year and quickly became the club’s tournament course. It was the home of **The Tradition**, which became a major championship on the senior tour, and it later hosted the PGA TOUR Champions’ **Charles Schwab Cup Championship** in 2012 and from 2014 through 2016. The Schwab Cup has been held at Phoenix Country Club since 2017.

That tournament history makes Cochise the course many visitors know best, even if they have never been inside the gates.

## Geronimo (1989 or 1990)

Geronimo followed Cochise. Sources disagree on whether it opened in 1989 or 1990. One published listing puts its length at about 7,100 yards from the back tees.

Geronimo completed the first wave of Desert Mountain golf. Three courses in roughly three years gave the young community a full golf program early on.

## Apache (1996)

After a pause of several years, Apache opened in 1996. Nicklaus Design lists it as opening that year. The gap between Geronimo and Apache is a reminder that Desert Mountain’s golf, like its neighborhoods, was built in stages over decades rather than all at once.

## Chiricahua (1999)

Chiricahua opened in 1999, three years after Apache, when Nicklaus completed it as the fifth of the club’s championship courses. Like the others, it is routed through the boulders, washes and native desert of Desert Mountain’s land, which sits well above the Valley floor.

## Outlaw (2003)

Outlaw, which opened in 2003, is the newest of the six Nicklaus courses. One published listing puts its length at about 7,100 yards. It completed the Nicklaus collection.

## No. 7 at Desert Mountain (2019)

No. 7 is the club’s newest course and the most different. It is a par-54 course of about 3,100 yards, made up entirely of par 3s, designed by club members Bill Brownlee and Wendell Pickett, who also designed the courses at Wickenburg Ranch. It opened in 2019.

No. 7 was built for a different kind of golf: shorter rounds, more social play, and an easier way for beginners, families and time-pressed members to get on the course. The club also built its membership structure around it. The **Seven Golf** category gives members access built around No. 7 at a lower cost than Full Golf.

## The Nicklaus connection

Desert Mountain is part of a longer story of Jack Nicklaus in Arizona. Developer Lyle Anderson first hired Nicklaus in the early 1980s to build the course at Desert Highlands in Scottsdale, with Bob Cupp as senior designer. That course opened in 1984, and Nicklaus Design began a renovation there in 2025.

Anderson then brought Nicklaus to Desert Mountain, where the partnership produced six courses. Elsewhere in the Phoenix area, Nicklaus later designed the two courses at Superstition Mountain in Gold Canyon with his sons. Few places have as much Nicklaus golf as the Scottsdale area, and Desert Mountain holds the largest share of it.

## What desert golf here is like

If you are coming from parkland or coastal golf, Desert Mountain’s courses will feel different:

- **Target golf.** Fairways and greens are surrounded by native desert. A missed shot often means a lost ball rather than a recovery from the rough.
- **Elevation and views.** The land rises well above the Valley floor, and many holes offer long views across the Sonoran Desert.
- **Carts.** Distances between greens and tees on desert courses can be long, so carts are common.
- **Seasons.** The best golf weather runs from fall through spring. Summer play is best early in the morning.
- **Overseeding.** Many Arizona courses overseed their grass in the fall, which can mean temporary closures or changes in conditions. Ask the club about its schedule.

## Tips for a first season as a member

1. **Play every course early.** Most members develop favorites quickly, but it helps to know all seven.
2. **Learn the booking system.** Ask how far ahead tee times open and how they are allocated across the courses.
3. **Use No. 7.** It is a good way to meet other members, practice your short game and fit in golf on busy days.
4. **Join a group or event.** Club events and regular games are the fastest way to find playing partners in a large club.
5. **Choose tees honestly.** Desert courses reward accuracy, and the right tees make the golf far more enjoyable.

## How members use seven courses

Having seven courses changes how golf works at a club.

**Variety.** Members can rotate among courses and rarely play the same layout two days in a row.

**Tee times.** With so much golf, tee-time pressure is spread across seven courses rather than concentrated on one, which can make it easier to get the time you want, even in peak season.

**Different moods.** A member can play a championship round on Cochise one day and a quick, social round on No. 7 the next.

**Maintenance.** With several courses, the club can close one for maintenance or renovation while others stay open.

The trade-off is scale. A club with this much golf has a large membership and a less concentrated social culture than a single-course club such as Estancia or Mirabel. For some members, that is a plus. For others, it is a reason to look elsewhere.

## Access by membership category

All seven courses are private. Desert Mountain Club currently lists three categories:

- **Full Golf**, which includes the six Nicklaus courses
- **Seven Golf**, built around No. 7
- **Lifestyle**, for dining, fitness, racquet sports and social access without full golf

Ask the club exactly which courses each category includes, how tee times are allocated and whether wait lists apply. Membership is separate from owning a home in the community.

## Visiting the courses

There is no public golf at Desert Mountain. Non-members play only as the guest of a member. Prospective members and home buyers can ask the club or a real estate agent who works in the community about seeing the courses. If you get the chance, try to play at least one of the original courses, such as Renegade or Cochise, and No. 7, to see the range of golf on offer.

- **Outlaw:** 2003, newest Nicklaus course
