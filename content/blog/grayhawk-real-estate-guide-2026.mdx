Grayhawk is one of North Scottsdale’s best-known master-planned communities, and one of the most varied. Within its boundaries you can find condos, townhomes, single-family homes, gated enclaves and a life-care retirement community, all within reach of two public championship golf courses.

This guide is for buyers looking at Grayhawk on its own. If you are deciding between Grayhawk and its neighbor, our [Grayhawk vs. DC Ranch comparison](/blog/grayhawk-vs-dc-ranch) covers that choice.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees below are published snapshots shown as ranges, and they change. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## What Grayhawk is

Grayhawk was developed by Grayhawk Development, a Scottsdale-based firm. The developer describes the community as covering more than 1,600 acres, with two distinct but interconnected areas:

- **The Park**
- **The Retreat**

Sources differ on the exact size of the community. The developer describes more than 4,000 residential units, while one broker page cites more than 3,700 homes. Sources also differ on timing, with first homes generally dated to the mid-1990s and build-out to the 2000s.

At the center is **Grayhawk Golf Club**, which opened in 1994 and has been open to the public since then.

## The golf

Grayhawk Golf Club has two par-72 courses:

- **Raptor**, designed by Tom Fazio. It hosted the PGA TOUR’s Frys.com Open from 2007 to 2009 and is generally described as the tougher of the two.
- **Talon**, designed by two-time major champion David Graham and architect Gary Panks.

Because the courses are public, there is no mandatory club and no initiation fee to live in Grayhawk. Residents pay to play like any other golfer. That keeps the fixed cost of living in Grayhawk well below that of private-club communities nearby.

## The home types

Grayhawk’s range of homes is one of its main strengths.

**Condos.** The lowest entry point, with several condo communities in the area. HOA fees are higher than for single-family homes, because the association covers more.

**Townhomes.** A step up in space, with published median sale prices for townhouses around $670,000 in one source.

**Single-family homes.** The core of the market, from smaller homes on compact lots to larger homes on golf and view lots. One source put the single-family median at about $1.2 million.

**Gated enclaves.** Several subdivisions are gated, with their own amenities. Tesoro, for example, is described with a community pool and spas, a fitness facility and a community room. Encore at Grayhawk is another gated enclave.

**Life-care retirement community.** Grayhawk also includes a life-care retirement community, which is unusual for a golf community and appeals to buyers planning for later life.

## What Grayhawk homes cost

Published sources give a range of figures. They differ in date and method, so treat them as indicators, not current prices.

**Community-wide**

- **Median sale price:** about $805,000 overall in one source covering the prior twelve months
- **Median single-family sale price:** about $1.2 million in the same source
- **Median townhouse sale price:** about $670,000 in the same source
- **Other estimates:** one data service put a much higher median of about $1.3 million, while another reported about $785,000 for March 2024

**By subdivision**

Published snapshots show subdivision medians ranging from about $474,000 at the lower end to about $1.85 million at Pinnacle, in The Park at Grayhawk.

The spread reflects the range of home types. A buyer looking for a condo and a buyer looking for a golf-course home are shopping in very different markets inside the same community. For context on what different budgets buy across Scottsdale, see [what $1M, $3M and $10M buy in Scottsdale](/blog/what-1m-3m-10m-buys-in-scottsdale).

## HOA fees

Grayhawk has a community association plus subdivision associations, so many homes pay more than one assessment. Published examples:

- **Single-family subdivisions in The Park:** roughly $54 to $125 a month, such as Featherwind at about $54 to $90 and Coventry at about $64 to $90
- **Condo and townhome communities:** roughly $250 to $450 a month, such as Village at Grayhawk at about $250 to $433
- **Some subdivisions bill quarterly**, such as Pinnacle at Grayhawk at about $155 to $285 a quarter

We did not find a published figure for the master community assessment on its own. Ask for the full HOA disclosure, covering every association the home belongs to.

## Location and daily life

Grayhawk sits in North Scottsdale, closer to shopping, dining and services than the far-north private-club communities. That location is a large part of its appeal for full-time residents and families. Drive times to Old Town Scottsdale, the airport and major employers are shorter than from communities such as Desert Mountain, though still a drive.

With public golf, a wide range of home types and shopping and services close by, Grayhawk tends to feel more like an everyday neighborhood than many luxury golf communities. Visit on a weekday and a weekend to get a sense of traffic and activity.

## Buying a condo or townhome in Grayhawk

Condos and townhomes are the most affordable way into Grayhawk, and they suit part-year owners, first-time buyers in Scottsdale and downsizers. A few points matter more for attached homes:

- **HOA coverage.** Higher fees usually mean the association covers more, such as roofs, exterior paint and landscaping. Ask exactly what is included.
- **Reserves.** In attached communities, owners share the cost of major repairs. A healthy reserve fund reduces the risk of a large special assessment.
- **Rental rules.** Some condo communities limit rentals or set minimum lease terms. Confirm the rules in writing.
- **Financing.** Lenders sometimes review a condo association’s finances and owner-occupancy rate before approving a loan. Ask your lender early.

## Buying on the golf course

Homes along Raptor or Talon offer open views and a sense of space. They also come with trade-offs:

- **Golf traffic.** Carts, maintenance crews and early tee times are part of daily life.
- **Stray balls.** Some lots are more exposed than others, depending on the hole.
- **Privacy.** Golfers can see into some yards. Check the sightlines from the course.
- **Price.** Golf-course homes usually sell at a premium. Compare with similar homes away from the course.

Walk the course near any home you are considering, and stand in the backyard during busy tee times.

## Grayhawk for part-year owners

Part-year owners often like Grayhawk because there is no private club bill and condos and townhomes offer lock-and-leave options. Before you buy, confirm what the HOA maintains while you are away, check the rental rules if you plan to rent the home, and plan for someone to check the home, pool and air conditioning during summer.

## How Grayhawk compares

Compared with far-north private-club communities such as Desert Mountain or Silverleaf, Grayhawk offers lower fixed costs, a wider range of home prices and a shorter drive to everyday services, but no private club built into the community. Compared with Troon North, another public-golf community, Grayhawk sits closer to central North Scottsdale and offers more condo and townhome options, while Troon North offers a more remote high-desert setting near Pinnacle Peak.

## Who Grayhawk suits

Grayhawk tends to suit buyers who:

- Want golf nearby without a private club bill
- Want a wide choice of home types and prices in one community
- Live in Scottsdale full-time and value a central North Scottsdale location
- Want a gated enclave option without moving to the far north

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want a private club’s social life and amenities
- Want large estate lots and the remote, high-desert feel of the far north
- Want a single, uniform set of rules across the whole community

## Questions to ask your agent

- Which subdivisions fit my budget and home type?
- How long have similar homes taken to sell recently?
- Which subdivisions are gated, and what amenities do they include?
- Are there known HOA issues or special assessments in any subdivision I am considering?
- How do prices in The Park and The Retreat compare for the home I want?

## Buyer due diligence

1. **All HOA documents.** Master and subdivision associations, fees, reserves and pending assessments.
2. **Rental rules.** These vary by subdivision. Confirm them in writing if rental income matters to you.
3. **Comparable sales.** Ask for recent sales in the same subdivision and home type, not community-wide medians.
4. **Golf course position.** Golf-course homes offer views but see golf traffic. Decide which you prefer.
5. **Schools.** Confirm school assignment for the exact address with the district.
6. **Condition.** Many Grayhawk homes date from the mid-1990s onward. Check the roof, air conditioning, windows and pool equipment.
7. **Insurance.** Get a quote for the address before you remove contingencies.
