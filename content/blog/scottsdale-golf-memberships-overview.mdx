Scottsdale has one of the deepest collections of private golf clubs in the country, and choosing a membership can feel complicated. Clubs are owned differently, offer different categories, set different entry rules and rarely publish their prices.

This overview brings it all together in one place. It explains the basics briefly and points you to our in-depth guides for each topic and club.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Club fees, rules and availability change, and most clubs do not publish their fees. All figures are third-party estimates shown as ranges. Verify every figure and term with the club and review them with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional.*

## The two ownership models

**Equity clubs** are owned by their members, usually through a member corporation. Members elect a board that sets dues, approves capital projects and decides on assessments. Your initiation typically buys a share of the club, and some clubs return part of it when you leave.

**Non-equity clubs** are owned by a company, developer or individual. Your initiation buys the right to use the club. The owner makes the major decisions.

Public sources describe these Scottsdale-area clubs as member-owned: Desert Mountain Club, Mirabel, The Estancia Club, The Country Club at DC Ranch and Tonto Verde. Owner-operated examples include Gainey Ranch Golf Club (Invited), Ancala Country Club (Arcis Golf) and Scottsdale National Golf Club (Bob and Renee Parsons).

## The membership categories

Most private clubs offer more than one category. The common types are:

- **Full golf:** unrestricted golf and all facilities
- **Limited or alternative golf:** restricted golf access or golf on a specific course, such as Desert Mountain’s Seven Golf category built around its par-54 No. 7 course
- **Social, clubhouse or lifestyle:** dining, fitness, pools, racquet sports and social events without regular golf, such as Silverleaf’s Clubhouse Membership or Desert Mountain’s Lifestyle category
- **Seasonal or non-resident:** at some clubs, for members who live elsewhere part of the year

Your category drives your cost more than anything else. Choose based on how your household will actually use the club, and ask how easy it is to change categories later.

## What memberships cost

Clubs rarely publish their fees. Published 2026 estimates for Scottsdale-area private clubs put:

- **Initiation** at roughly $35,000 to $350,000 or more
- **Monthly dues** at roughly $800 to $3,500
- **Extras**, such as food minimums and assessments, at roughly $10,000 to $30,000 a year

## How joining works

Entry rules vary widely by club. The main types are:

- **Invitation and sponsorship.** Whisper Rock membership is by invitation, generally with sponsorship from existing members.
- **Property ownership.** Desert Highlands ties membership to owning property in its community.
- **Open application.** Some clubs accept applications without a home purchase. Mirabel, for example, no longer requires property ownership.
- **Application-based programs.** Scottsdale National has been reported to use a program called the Xperience as part of admission.

Waitlists are common. Reporting from 2024 described a multi-year wait for Golf membership at Silverleaf, and Desert Mountain notes that wait lists may apply to all categories. Some routes can be faster, such as homes sold with a membership included.

## The no-membership alternative

You do not need a membership to play great golf in Scottsdale. Public and resort courses include TPC Scottsdale (Stadium and Champions), Troon North (Monument and Pinnacle), Grayhawk (Raptor and Talon), Talking Stick and We-Ko-Pa. Communities such as Grayhawk and Troon North are built around public golf, so residents pay only when they play.

For occasional golfers, public golf is almost always the lower-cost choice. A simple test: divide a club’s annual cost, including initiation spread over your expected stay, by the rounds you realistically play, and compare it with green fees.

## Memberships and buying a home

In Scottsdale, the club and the home are often linked, so plan them together.

- **Club inside your community:** at Desert Mountain, Silverleaf, Estancia, Mirabel, DC Ranch, Desert Highlands and others, the club sits inside a residential community. Membership may be optional, required or tied to owning property, depending on the club.
- **Club without homes:** Whisper Rock and Scottsdale National have no conventional neighborhood, so you choose your home separately.
- **No club at all:** Grayhawk and Troon North are built around public golf.

If membership matters, confirm availability and terms before you remove your purchase contingencies. Ask whether a home comes with a membership, whether a membership can transfer when you sell, and whether the community’s CC&Rs say anything about the club.

## Memberships for part-year residents

Many Scottsdale golfers live here only part of the year. Private clubs usually charge dues year-round, so a part-year member pays for months away. Ask each club whether it offers seasonal or non-resident categories, how they work and whether they come with restrictions on tee times or guests. For some part-year residents, public golf or a social category is the better value.

## Questions to ask every club

1. Who owns the club, and how are decisions made?
2. What categories are available, and what does each include?
3. What are the current initiation, dues and minimums?
4. Is any part of the initiation refundable, and on what terms?
5. Are any assessments planned?
6. Is there a waitlist, and how long is it?
7. Does membership require, include or transfer with a home?

## A short glossary

- **Initiation:** the one-time fee to join
- **Dues:** the recurring monthly or annual charge
- **Assessment:** an extra charge to fund a project or shortfall
- **Food and beverage minimum:** a required minimum spend on dining
- **Equity contribution:** at an equity club, the part of the initiation that buys ownership
- **Resale queue:** resigned members waiting for their memberships to be resold
- **Transfer fee:** the share a club keeps when a membership changes hands
- **Reciprocal play:** access to other clubs through your home club

## How to choose

1. **Start with how you will use the club.** Rounds per year, dining, fitness and family use.
2. **Decide on the ownership model.** Do you want a share and a vote, or are you comfortable with an owner?
3. **Choose your category.** Full golf, limited golf or social.
4. **Check entry rules and waitlists** before you buy a home.
5. **Get the documents** and review refund, transfer and assessment terms with a licensed professional.
6. **Compare the full cost** with public golf.

*Important: Club ownership, rules, fees and availability change, and most clubs do not publish their fees. Confirm every figure and term with the club and consult a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before making any decision.*
