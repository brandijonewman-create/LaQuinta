The Boulders is one of the most distinctive settings in the Scottsdale area. Its name comes from the giant granite boulder formations that rise throughout the property, and its homes, resort and golf courses are built around them rather than over them. For buyers, it offers something unusual: a resort community with private-club golf available, but no requirement to join.

This guide covers what buyers should know about The Boulders homes for sale in 2026: how the community works, what published data shows about prices and HOA fees, the golf and the resort, and what to check before you make an offer.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees below are published snapshots shown as ranges, and they change. Verify current figures with ARMLS data and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## What The Boulders is

The Boulders is a golf and resort community that spans parts of Scottsdale and Carefree, in the far north of the Phoenix area. At its center is **Boulders Resort and Spa Scottsdale, Curio Collection by Hilton**, a resort known for adobe-style casitas set among the boulder formations. One directory lists the resort as built in 1985 and renovated in 2012.

Around the resort are the community’s homes, ranging from casitas and patio homes to large custom estates, and **The Boulders Club**, with two 18-hole golf courses.

Nearby, the town of Carefree was established as a planned community in 1955, and it remains the closest place for small-town dining and shopping. Carefree is also home to Desert Forest Golf Club, designed by Robert “Red” Lawrence and opened in 1962, often described as one of the first desert golf courses in the Southwest.

## The golf

The Boulders Club has two 18-hole courses, both designed by Jay Morrish:

- **North Course:** about 6,811 yards, par 72
- **South Course:** about 6,726 yards, par 71

Both are desert courses routed through the boulder formations, washes and native vegetation. Morrish also co-designed the Monument course at nearby Troon North with Tom Weiskopf, so buyers comparing the two areas will recognize a similar design sensibility.

The Boulders Club offers **golf and social memberships**. Membership is optional for homeowners. You can buy a home, never join, and still enjoy the setting. Ask the club about current membership categories, availability, fees and whether resort guests and members share the courses, since tee-time access can be an important factor for members who play often.

## How the community is organized

The Boulders is made up of many separate subdivisions, each with its own homes, rules and HOA. Subdivisions include:

- **Boulders Estates**
- **Adobes de la Tierra**
- **Club Villas**
- **Palo Brea**
- **The Reserve at The Boulders**
- **Villas at The Boulders**
- **The Crossing at The Boulders**

Because each subdivision has its own association, rules on rentals, exterior changes and maintenance can differ significantly. Some homes may also belong to more than one association. Always read the documents for the specific subdivision.

## The home types you will find

**Casitas.** Smaller homes near the resort, some of which have historically taken part in a resort rental program, according to listing reports. These can offer the lowest entry point. Ask whether a rental program exists today and what its terms are.

**Patio homes and townhomes.** Often described as lock and leave, these suit part-year owners who want an HOA to handle more of the exterior upkeep.

**Single-family homes.** Detached homes on desert lots, often in Southwestern or desert contemporary styles that blend with the boulder setting.

**Custom estates.** Larger homes on larger lots, some with guest casitas or separate villas, at the top of the market.

## What Boulders homes cost

Here is what published data shows. These figures come from different sources and different years, so treat them as ranges, not current prices.

- **2022 average sale price:** about $1.7 million, from one area report
- **Recent single-family snapshot:** an average of about $2.4 million across a small number of listings
- **Subdivision median in one recent snapshot:** about $1.3 million
- **Older ranges:** a 2018 report described prices from about $595,000 for a casita of roughly 1,600 square feet to about $2.2 million for a custom home of roughly 7,300 square feet

The pattern is consistent: casitas and townhomes offer the lowest entry, single-family homes commonly sell in the $1 million to $2.5 million range, and larger custom homes reach higher. Because the community is small, a handful of large or small sales can move the averages a lot. Ask your agent for recent sales in the specific subdivision and home type.

## HOA fees

Published examples show how widely HOA fees vary:

- **Boulders Estates:** roughly $135 to $219 a month
- **Adobes de la Tierra:** roughly $140 to $167 a month
- **Club Villas:** roughly $405 to $500 a month

Higher fees often reflect more services, such as exterior maintenance or landscaping. Ask exactly what each association covers, the state of its reserves and whether any special assessments are pending.

## Living at The Boulders

Daily life here is shaped by the resort and the desert. Many owners value the resort’s restaurants and spa close to home, the quiet and the hiking and scenery around the boulder formations. Carefree is minutes away for coffee, dining and small shops, and North Scottsdale is the place for larger shopping and services.

The trade-offs are distance and seasonality. The Boulders is a long drive from central Phoenix and Sky Harbor Airport, and summers are hot. Many owners spend part of the year elsewhere, and the community is at its busiest from fall through spring.

## The Boulders for part-year owners

The Boulders has long appealed to buyers who live here only part of the year. A few points matter more for them:

- **Lock-and-leave homes.** Patio homes, townhomes and casitas usually need less care while you are away. Ask your HOA exactly what it maintains.
- **Optional club.** Because membership is not required, you are not committed to full-year club dues for months you are not here. If you do join, ask whether any seasonal options exist.
- **Rental possibilities.** Some owners rent their homes while they are away. Confirm the subdivision’s rules and any resort rental program terms in writing.
- **Summer care.** Plan for someone to check the home, pool, air conditioning and irrigation during the hottest months.

## How The Boulders compares with nearby options

Buyers looking at The Boulders often compare it with two kinds of nearby communities.

**Large private-club communities**, such as Desert Mountain, offer more courses, more clubhouses and a full social calendar, but come with larger club costs and, at some, wait lists.

**Public-golf communities**, such as Troon North, offer championship golf with no club at all, and a wider range of home prices.

The Boulders sits between them: a resort setting, an optional private club with two courses and a smaller, quieter community. For many buyers, the deciding factor is whether they want the resort close to home.

## Who The Boulders suits

The Boulders tends to suit buyers who:

- Want a resort setting with golf available but not required
- Value dramatic desert scenery and a quieter, lower-density community
- Plan to own part-year and want lock-and-leave options
- Like having a small town such as Carefree nearby

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want a single, large private club with many courses and a full-time social calendar
- Need a short commute into central Phoenix
- Want a walkable, urban neighborhood

## Questions to ask The Boulders Club

If golf is part of your plan, ask the club these questions before you buy:

- What membership categories are available today, and what does each include?
- What are the current initiation and dues for each category?
- Is there a wait list?
- How do members and resort guests share the courses, and how are tee times allocated?
- Is there a food and beverage minimum, and are any assessments planned?
- Does a membership transfer with a home sale, or does a buyer apply separately?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed professional.

## What to check before you make an offer

1. **The subdivision’s HOA documents.** Rules, fees, reserves and any pending special assessments, for every association the home belongs to.
2. **Rental rules and programs.** If rental income matters, confirm in writing what is allowed and whether any resort rental program applies.
3. **Club access.** If golf matters, ask the club about current membership categories, availability and how members and resort guests share the courses.
4. **Address and jurisdiction.** Confirm whether the home is in Scottsdale or Carefree, since that affects services and some local rules.
5. **Comparable sales.** Ask for recent sales in the same subdivision and home type.
6. **Inspection.** Get a full inspection, including the roof, drainage around boulders and washes, and any pool or desert landscaping.
7. **Insurance.** Get a quote for the specific address before you remove contingencies.
