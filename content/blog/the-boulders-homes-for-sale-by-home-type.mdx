Our [guide to The Boulders homes for sale](/blog/the-boulders-scottsdale-homes-for-sale-2026) covers the community as a whole: the resort, the two Jay Morrish courses, the subdivisions and the overall market. This article takes a narrower view. It compares the main home types at The Boulders side by side, because the right type of home affects your budget, your upkeep and how you will use the property as much as the price does.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees are published snapshots shown as ranges, and they change. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## The four home types at a glance

The Boulders, which spans parts of Scottsdale and Carefree, has a wider range of home types than many golf communities its size:

1. **Casitas**, smaller homes near Boulders Resort and Spa
2. **Patio homes and townhomes**, attached or semi-attached homes, often described as lock and leave
3. **Single-family homes** on desert lots
4. **Custom estates** on larger lots, sometimes with guest casitas or separate villas

Each sits in its own subdivision with its own HOA. Known subdivisions include Boulders Estates, Adobes de la Tierra, Club Villas, Palo Brea, The Reserve at The Boulders, Villas at The Boulders and The Crossing at The Boulders.

## Casitas

**What they are.** Smaller homes, typically near the resort, in the adobe-inspired style the property is known for.

**Price.** Casitas have historically been among the lowest-priced homes at The Boulders. A 2018 report cited a casita of about 1,600 square feet at about $595,000. Prices have changed since, so treat this as a reference point, not a current price.

**Upkeep.** Usually low. Associations often handle more of the exterior.

**Rental potential.** Listing reports say some casitas have taken part in a resort rental program. If rental income matters to you, ask:

- Whether a program exists today and who runs it
- How revenue is split and what fees apply
- How many nights the owner can use the home
- What furnishing and maintenance standards are required
- What the HOA allows outside the program

**Who they suit.** Part-year owners, buyers who want to be close to the resort, and buyers looking for the lowest entry point.

**Watch for.** Smaller living space, shared walls in some cases, and rental program obligations that may limit when you can use the home.

## Patio homes and townhomes

**What they are.** Attached or semi-attached homes, usually with small private outdoor spaces and shared walls or zero-lot-line designs.

**Price.** An older report cited an updated townhome at about $419,000. Current prices are likely higher. Check recent sales in the specific subdivision.

**Upkeep.** Usually low to moderate. This is the classic lock-and-leave category. Ask the HOA whether it covers roofs, exterior paint, landscaping and pest control.

**HOA costs.** Often higher than detached homes because the association does more. Club Villas, for example, has published HOA fees of roughly $405 to $500 a month.

**Who they suit.** Part-year owners, downsizers and buyers who want minimal maintenance.

**Watch for.** Rules on exterior changes, noise and pets, which tend to be stricter in attached communities. Also check the reserve fund, since attached communities share the cost of major repairs.

## Single-family homes

**What they are.** Detached homes on desert lots, often in Southwestern or desert contemporary styles that blend with the boulder setting.

**Price.** The middle of the market. Published snapshots put recent single-family averages around $2.4 million across a small number of listings, and one subdivision median at about $1.3 million. The 2022 average sale price across The Boulders was about $1.7 million.

**Upkeep.** Moderate. You are typically responsible for your own exterior, yard, pool and roof.

**HOA costs.** Often lower than attached homes. Boulders Estates has published fees of roughly $135 to $219 a month, and Adobes de la Tierra roughly $140 to $167 a month.

**Who they suit.** Full-time residents, families and buyers who want privacy and outdoor space.

**Watch for.** Drainage around boulders and washes, the age of the roof and mechanical systems, and how close the home sits to the golf course or resort traffic.

## Custom estates

**What they are.** Larger homes on larger lots, built to individual designs, sometimes with guest casitas or separate villas.

**Price.** The top of the market. A 2018 report cited a custom home of about 7,300 square feet at about $2.2 million, and an older report cited an estate home with a separate villa at about $2.5 million. Current prices for the best estates are likely well above those figures.

**Upkeep.** High. Larger homes and lots mean higher costs for landscaping, pools, roofs and systems.

**Who they suit.** Full-time residents and buyers who want space, privacy and room for guests.

**Watch for.** Design review rules if you plan to renovate or add on, and the condition of older custom construction.

## Comparing the true cost of ownership

The purchase price is only the start. For each home type, add these annual costs:

- **HOA assessments** for every association the home belongs to
- **Property taxes**, from Maricopa County records for the parcel
- **Insurance**, quoted for the specific address
- **Utilities**, especially summer cooling, which varies a lot with home size
- **Landscaping and pool care**, if not covered by the HOA
- **Club dues**, if you join The Boulders Club
- **Home monitoring** while you are away, for part-year owners

As a rule, attached homes have higher HOA fees but lower upkeep, while detached homes have lower HOA fees but more of everything else. Ask the seller for the last twelve months of utility and maintenance bills to see the real numbers.

## What about the club?

No home type at The Boulders comes with a club membership. The Boulders Club offers golf and social memberships separately, and membership is optional. If golf or the club’s social life matters to you, ask about current categories, availability, initiation and dues before you choose a home, and add any club costs to the ownership comparison above.

## Choosing the right home type

A simple way to decide:

- **You will be here less than half the year:** start with patio homes, townhomes or casitas.
- **You want rental income:** look at casitas with a confirmed rental program, and confirm the rules in writing.
- **You live here full time with family or frequent guests:** look at single-family homes or custom estates.
- **You want to renovate or build:** look at single-family homes or estates, and read the design rules first.
- **You want the lowest entry point:** start with townhomes and casitas, and check recent sales.

## Location within The Boulders

Within any home type, location matters. Three questions to ask about every home:

**How close is it to the resort?** Homes near Boulders Resort and Spa are convenient for dining and the spa but may see more guest traffic and activity. Homes farther away tend to be quieter.

**Is it on the golf course?** Golf-course homes offer views across the North or South course, but also see golf traffic, carts and occasional stray balls. Some buyers love it. Others prefer a desert lot.

**What are the views?** The boulder formations shape views from one lot to the next. A home with a clear view across the desert can feel very different from a similar home facing a neighbor. Visit at different times of day.

## Questions to ask the HOA

Ask each association, in writing:

1. What exactly does the HOA maintain: roofs, exterior paint, landscaping, pools, pest control?
2. What are the current assessments, and how have they changed over the last few years?
3. How healthy is the reserve fund, and when was the last reserve study?
4. Are any special assessments pending or planned?
5. What are the rules on rentals, including minimum stays?
6. What approvals are needed for exterior changes?
7. Does the home belong to more than one association?

## Thinking about resale

Each home type sells to a different buyer pool. Casitas and townhomes tend to appeal to part-year owners and buyers looking for the lowest entry point. Single-family homes and estates tend to appeal to full-time residents and buyers who want space. Because The Boulders is a relatively small community, a few sales can move the averages a lot, so ask your agent how long homes of your type have taken to sell recently.

## Inspection checklist by home type

For every home, get a full inspection. Add these checks by type:

- **Casitas and townhomes:** shared roof and wall responsibilities, the association’s reserve study, and any rental program wear and tear
- **Single-family homes:** roof age, drainage around boulders and washes, pool equipment and air conditioning age
- **Custom estates:** all of the above, plus specialty construction, guest casita systems and any unpermitted additions

*Important: Prices, HOA fees, rental program terms and rules change. Confirm current figures with ARMLS data, the relevant associations and a licensed real estate professional before making any decision.*
