Troon North is made up of many separate subdivisions, and Candlewood Estates is one of its most distinctive. It is a guard-gated enclave of custom homes, many on golf or view lots, in the high desert near Pinnacle Peak.

This guide covers what buyers should know about Candlewood Estates: how it fits within Troon North, what published data show about prices and homes, what makes one lot more valuable than another, and what to check before you buy. For the wider Troon North market, see our [Troon North homes for sale guide](/blog/troon-north-homes-for-sale-2026-guide).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and details below are published snapshots shown as ranges, and sources sometimes disagree. Verify everything with ARMLS data, the HOA and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## What Candlewood Estates is

Candlewood Estates is a subdivision within the Troon North area of North Scottsdale. One source describes it as a 24-hour guard-gated community within the roughly 1,800-acre Troon North master plan. Listings describe private pool and common landscaped areas for residents.

Its defining features are:

- **A staffed gate**, described by one source as operating around the clock
- **Custom homes**, rather than production or tract homes
- **Golf and view lots**, near Troon North Golf Club’s Monument and Pinnacle courses

**How big is it?** Sources disagree. One describes 81 homesites, while another describes 300 custom homes. The difference may reflect different phases or boundaries. If size matters to your decision, ask the HOA for the current number of lots and homes.

## The setting: Troon North

Troon North is built around Troon North Golf Club, whose two courses are open to the public:

- **Monument**, designed by Tom Weiskopf and Jay Morrish, opened in 1990
- **Pinnacle**, designed by Tom Weiskopf, opened in 1995

There is no mandatory club. You can own in Candlewood Estates and play the courses as a paying guest, or not play at all. The Four Seasons Resort Scottsdale at Troon North is also nearby.

## The homes

Candlewood Estates is a custom-home enclave, so no two homes are quite alike. Published sources describe:

- **Sizes** of roughly 3,000 to 7,000 square feet
- **Lots** that vary, with some listings describing premium lots of half an acre or more and elevated sites with views
- **Styles** typical of North Scottsdale custom homes, from Southwestern to desert contemporary
- **Construction dates** that vary by home. One listing, in Unit 3, showed a home built in 2005
- **Builders** that vary by home. One listing named Sever Design Group as its builder

Because homes are custom, condition and updating vary widely. Two homes of similar size can differ by hundreds of thousands of dollars in value depending on age, finishes and lot.

## What Candlewood Estates homes cost

Here is what published data show. These come from listing sites at different times, so treat them as ranges.

- **Median listing price for luxury homes:** about $1.8 million, in one snapshot of eight listings
- **Median listing price for homes with views:** about $2.7 million, in one snapshot of six listings
- **Individual listings:** roughly $1.8 million for a three-bedroom home to about $4.8 million for a five-bedroom home
- **Older summary median:** a little above $1.5 million

The pattern: Candlewood Estates sits toward the upper end of the Troon North market, above the community-wide medians but below the most expensive custom estates in North Scottsdale. Because the enclave is small, a few sales can move the median a lot. Ask your agent for recent closed sales, not just listings.

## What makes one lot worth more than another

In a custom-home enclave like Candlewood Estates, the lot often matters as much as the house. When comparing homes, look at:

**Golf frontage.** Homes on the golf course offer open views and a sense of space, but also see golf traffic, carts and the occasional stray ball. Some buyers prefer a desert lot for privacy.

**Views.** Views toward Pinnacle Peak, the surrounding mountains or the city lights at night can add significantly to value. Elevated lots tend to offer the widest views.

**Privacy.** Check how close the neighboring homes are, which windows face which way and whether future construction on a vacant lot could change the view.

**Orientation.** In the desert, the direction a home faces affects summer heat, afternoon glare and how usable the patio is at different times of day.

**Lot size.** Larger lots give more space and privacy, but also mean more desert landscaping to maintain.

Visit any home you are serious about at different times of day, including late afternoon in summer.

## HOA and gate costs

We did not find a published HOA fee for Candlewood Estates. Guard-gated communities generally carry higher HOA costs than ungated ones, because the association pays for staffing the gate around the clock. Other Troon North subdivisions have published fees ranging from under $100 to a few hundred dollars a month, which gives a sense of the wider market but not of Candlewood specifically.

Ask for the full HOA disclosure, including:

- Current assessments for every association the home belongs to
- What the assessments cover, including gate staffing, common areas and the community pool
- Reserve fund health and the date of the last reserve study
- Any pending or planned special assessments
- Rules on rentals, exterior changes and construction

## Living behind a staffed gate

A guard-gated enclave changes daily life in small ways that are worth understanding before you buy.

**Guests and deliveries.** Visitors, contractors and deliveries typically check in at the gate. Ask how the guest list works, whether there is an app or call-ahead system, and how ride-share and food deliveries are handled.

**Contractors.** If you plan to renovate, ask about working hours, contractor registration and any rules on construction parking or noise.

**Security while away.** For part-year owners, a staffed gate can add peace of mind. It does not replace home monitoring, so plan for someone to check the home regularly, especially in summer.

**Cost.** Staffing a gate around the clock is a significant expense for an HOA. It is one reason guard-gated enclaves usually carry higher assessments than ungated neighborhoods.

## Buying a custom home: what to know

Custom homes reward careful inspection more than production homes do, because every one was built differently.

- **Ask for the plans** and any records of past renovations or additions.
- **Hire inspectors with custom-home experience**, and consider specialists for the roof, pool and air conditioning.
- **Check materials.** Some custom homes use specialty materials, such as natural stone, custom windows or unusual roofing, that are expensive to repair or replace.
- **Look at systems.** Large homes may have several air conditioning units, water heaters and pool systems. Check the age of each.
- **Price the updates.** If a home needs a kitchen, bathrooms or systems updated, get estimates before you negotiate.

## Renovating or rebuilding

Some buyers in custom-home enclaves plan to renovate heavily or even rebuild. If that is your plan, read the design guidelines and approval process before you make an offer, ask how long approvals typically take, and talk to an architect or builder who has worked in Troon North. Design review can affect what you are allowed to build, how tall it can be and what materials you can use.

## Who Candlewood Estates suits

Candlewood Estates tends to suit buyers who:

- Want a guard-gated, custom-home setting without a mandatory private club
- Value golf and mountain views
- Plan to own full-time or for long seasons and want security while away
- Like having public championship golf and a resort nearby

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want a private club’s social life built into the community
- Want the lowest price per square foot in Troon North
- Want a walkable neighborhood close to shops and restaurants

## Buyer due diligence

1. **HOA documents.** Fees, coverage, reserves, rules and pending assessments.
2. **Recent closed sales.** Ask for sales within the enclave over the last year or two.
3. **Lot position.** Golf frontage, views, orientation and what could be built nearby.
4. **Age and condition.** For older custom homes, check the roof, windows, air conditioning, pool equipment and any past additions.
5. **Permits.** Confirm that additions and major renovations were permitted.
6. **Insurance.** Get a quote for the specific address before you remove contingencies.
7. **Design rules.** If you plan to renovate, read the subdivision’s design guidelines and approval process.
8. **Schools.** If schools matter, confirm the assignment for the address with the district.
