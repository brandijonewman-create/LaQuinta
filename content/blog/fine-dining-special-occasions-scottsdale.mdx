Scottsdale is a city of celebrations. Anniversaries, milestone birthdays, retirement dinners, club championships, member-guest wins and the last night of a golf trip all call for something special. With a deep and growing restaurant scene, the challenge is not finding a good restaurant. It is choosing the right one for the moment and planning well.

This guide explains how to plan a special-occasion dinner in Scottsdale. It does not rank restaurants. Menus and chefs change often, and the right choice depends on the occasion. Where we mention specific restaurants, it is to report recognition from the Michelin Guide, not our own endorsement.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Menus, prices, hours and policies change. Confirm details directly with each restaurant, including deposits, cancellation terms and dress codes.*

## Match the restaurant to the occasion

**The intimate dinner for two.** An anniversary or proposal calls for a quiet room, attentive service and a setting that feels special. Ask about the best tables when you book.

**The golf group celebration.** After a club championship or the final night of a golf trip, a lively room or a private dining space works better than a hushed dining room. Look for restaurants with group menus and space for everyone to sit together.

**The client or business dinner.** A quieter room, reliable service and a wine list with options at a range of prices.

**The family milestone.** A space that works across ages, with a menu that offers something for everyone.

**The tasting-menu experience.** For food lovers, a multi-course tasting menu with optional wine pairings is the main event of the evening.

## What the Michelin Guide recognized

In August 2026, Michelin revealed its first Guide Southwest, covering Arizona, Nevada, New Mexico and Utah. No Arizona restaurant received a Michelin star in this first edition, but several Scottsdale restaurants were recognized:

- **FnB** received a Bib Gourmand, Michelin’s designation for good food at a moderate price.
- **Hush Public House, RESERVE and ShinBay** were listed as Recommended.
- **John Germain of RESERVE** received the Michelin Sommelier Award.

Nearby, **INDIBAR** in Paradise Valley was also Recommended, along with several Phoenix restaurants.

Michelin recognition is one useful signal, but it is not the only one. Many excellent special-occasion restaurants are not in the guide.

## Private dining for golf groups

For groups of eight or more, a private dining room often works best. When you contact the events team, ask:

1. **Capacity** and how the room can be arranged
2. **Minimum spend** or room fee
3. **Set menus** versus ordering from the regular menu
4. **Wine options**, including pairings, corkage and bringing your own special bottles
5. **Deposits and cancellation terms**
6. **Audio-visual equipment** for toasts, slideshows or presentations
7. **Timing**, including how long you can keep the room
8. **Dietary needs**, so the kitchen can plan ahead

Put the details in writing, and confirm the final guest count by the restaurant’s deadline.

## Reservations in peak season

Scottsdale’s restaurant calendar follows the golf season. From fall through spring, part-year residents return and visitors arrive, and popular restaurants fill up quickly. A few tips:

- **Book as early as the restaurant allows**, especially for weekends and holidays.
- **Plan around major events.** The WM Phoenix Open week at TPC Scottsdale and other big weekends bring heavy demand.
- **Ask about a waitlist** if your first choice is full.
- **Confirm the day before.** Many restaurants require confirmation.
- **Check cancellation policies.** Some charge for late cancellations or no-shows, especially for groups.

In summer, reservations are usually much easier, and some restaurants offer seasonal menus.

## Tasting menus and wine

**Tasting menus** let the kitchen show its range. Ask when you book:

- How many courses, and how long the meal usually takes
- The price, and whether tax and service are included
- Whether the menu can accommodate allergies and dietary needs
- Whether wine or non-alcoholic pairings are offered

**Wine.** For a special occasion, ask the sommelier for help. Name your preferences and a price, and let them guide you. Our [guide to Scottsdale wine for golfers](/blog/scottsdale-wine-bars-for-golfers-guide) covers ordering with confidence and Arizona’s own wine regions.

## Golf milestones worth celebrating

Golf households have occasions other people do not. A few ideas for marking them:

- **A first hole-in-one:** tradition in many clubs says the golfer buys a round. A dinner with your playing partners is a more memorable way to mark it.
- **A club championship or member-guest win:** a private dining room for your team and their partners.
- **A first round at a dream course:** dinner at a restaurant that reflects the destination, or a celebration back home.
- **Joining a club:** a dinner with your sponsors, if your club used sponsorship.
- **Retirement:** many Scottsdale residents celebrate retirement by finally playing every day. A dinner with family and golf friends marks the start.

## Patios, views and the seasons

Scottsdale dining is shaped by the weather. From fall through spring, patio tables with mountain or sunset views are in high demand, so ask for them when you book. In summer, many diners prefer cool indoor rooms, and some restaurants offer seasonal menus or special pricing. If a view matters for your occasion, ask what time the sun sets and book accordingly.

## Resort dining

Scottsdale’s resorts often include some of the area’s most polished dining rooms, with the advantage of grounds to walk, bars before and after dinner, and, for out-of-town guests, rooms upstairs. For a celebration that includes visitors, a resort dinner can turn an evening into a full experience.

## Hosting with ease

- **Choose the restaurant with your guests in mind,** not just your own tastes.
- **Settle the bill quietly.** Arrange payment with the restaurant in advance so it does not arrive at the table.
- **Confirm accessibility** if any guests need step-free access or special seating.
- **Plan the end of the night,** including rides home or a nightcap nearby.

## Budgeting

Special-occasion dining costs vary widely with the restaurant, the menu and the wine. Build a budget that includes:

- Food, whether à la carte or a tasting menu
- Wine or pairings
- Tax and service, which some restaurants add automatically for groups
- Private room fees or minimum spend
- Transportation

Ask for current pricing when you book, especially for groups, so there are no surprises.

## Dining at your club

For members of private clubs, the club dining room is often a natural choice for celebrations. Clubs commonly host special dinners, wine events and holiday menus, and many offer private rooms for member functions. Ask your club’s events team about menus, room fees and how to book. A club dinner can be especially fitting for golf milestones, since the people who shared the round are often members too.

## Non-alcoholic options

More restaurants now offer thoughtful non-alcoholic pairings and cocktails. If some guests are not drinking, ask when you book what the restaurant offers, so everyone can share in the toasts.

## Making it special

- **Mention the occasion** when you book.
- **Share allergies and dietary needs** in advance.
- **Ask about the best table** for a view or a quiet corner.
- **Arrange a cake or dessert** ahead of time if you want one.
- **Plan the toast.** For groups, decide who will speak and when.
- **Arrange transportation** so everyone gets home safely.

## Choosing between restaurants

If you are deciding between two or three options, compare them on what matters for your occasion: the room and atmosphere, how they handle groups, the menu style, the wine list, the location relative to where guests are staying, and their policies. Read the restaurant’s own website for current menus and hours, and call ahead with any questions.

## Coming from the golf course

Many Scottsdale celebrations start on the course. A few practical points:

- **Check the dress code.** Some restaurants are relaxed, others expect smart attire. Change out of golf shoes.
- **Allow time** between the end of a round or event and the dinner reservation.
- **Book for the right time.** A late afternoon tournament finish may call for a later reservation.

## Dining at home instead

For some occasions, a private chef at home is a better fit, especially for larger groups or when you want a relaxed evening without leaving the house. Our [private chef guide](/blog/private-chefs-for-golf-households-scottsdale) covers costs and how to hire.

## Questions to ask when booking

1. Can you accommodate our group size, and is there a private room?
2. Is there a set menu or minimum spend for groups?
3. What are the deposit and cancellation terms?
4. Is there a dress code?
5. Can you accommodate allergies and dietary needs?
6. Is service added automatically for groups?
7. What is the corkage policy?

*Important: Menus, prices, hours and policies change, and Michelin selections are updated in future editions. Confirm details directly with each restaurant before you book.*
