Scottsdale is known for public golf, from TPC Scottsdale to Troon North and Grayhawk. But some of the most celebrated golf in the area is private, played on courses most visitors never see. This guide walks through those private courses one by one: who designed them, when they opened, what kind of golf they offer and how someone who is not a member might get a game.

This is a guide to the courses. If you want to know how the clubs are owned, see our [equity vs. non-equity club-by-club guide](/blog/equity-vs-non-equity-golf-memberships-scottsdale-clubs). If you want to know where to live, see our [area guide to Scottsdale golf communities](/blog/golf-communities-in-scottsdale-arizona-area-guide).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Course details, guest policies and membership rules change. Confirm current details with each club and consult a licensed real estate or financial professional before making any membership or home decision.*

## How to read this guide

For each club, we list the course or courses, the designers and opening years where public sources agree, and anything notable about the golf or the setting. Where sources disagree, we say so. We do not rank the courses. Every one of them has members who would argue it is the best in town.

## Desert Mountain Club

**Courses:** seven. **Designers:** Jack Nicklaus (six courses); Bill Brownlee and Wendell Pickett (No. 7).

Desert Mountain has the largest concentration of private golf in the area. Its six Jack Nicklaus Signature courses opened over sixteen years:

- **Renegade** (1987), the first course, built with two greens on each hole
- **Cochise** (1988), the original home of The Tradition, a senior major, and later host of the PGA TOUR Champions’ Charles Schwab Cup Championship in 2012 and from 2014 through 2016
- **Geronimo** (1989 or 1990, depending on the source)
- **Apache** (1996)
- **Chiricahua** (1999)
- **Outlaw** (2003)

No. 7 at Desert Mountain, a par-54 course of about 3,100 yards, opened in 2019. The club is member-owned and sits inside a gated residential community at the northern edge of Scottsdale.

## Desert Highlands

**Course:** one. **Designer:** Jack Nicklaus. **Opened:** 1984.

Developer Lyle Anderson brought Nicklaus to Desert Highlands in the early 1980s, with Bob Cupp as senior designer, before the two went on to build Desert Mountain. The course was the start of a long relationship between Nicklaus and the Scottsdale area. In 2025, the club began a renovation led by Nicklaus Design. Public sources describe membership as tied to owning property in the community.

## The Estancia Club

**Course:** one. **Designer:** Tom Fazio. **Opened:** 1995 (some sources say 1996).

Estancia sits at the base of Pinnacle Peak in North Scottsdale. The course was named Golf Digest’s Best New Private Course of 1996, which may explain why sources differ on the opening year. The club is member-owned and built around this single course.

## Mirabel Golf Club

**Course:** one. **Designer:** Tom Fazio. **Opened:** December 1, 2001.

Mirabel is a member-owned club in far North Scottsdale, built around a single Fazio course and set within the Mirabel residential community. Public sources say Mirabel no longer requires property ownership for membership.

## Silverleaf Club

**Course:** one. **Designer:** Tom Weiskopf. **Opened:** 2002.

Silverleaf’s par-72 course plays a little over 7,300 yards from the back tees. It sits within the Silverleaf community in the DC Ranch area, next to the McDowell Sonoran Preserve. The club offers Golf and Clubhouse memberships.

## The Country Club at DC Ranch

**Course:** one. **Designers:** Tom Lehman and John Fought.

DC Ranch’s club pairs a major champion, Tom Lehman, with architect John Fought. Public sources describe the club as member-owned. It is part of the larger DC Ranch master-planned community in North Scottsdale.

## Whisper Rock Golf Club

**Courses:** two. **Designers:** Phil Mickelson with Gary Stephenson (Lower); Tom Fazio (Upper). **Opened:** most sources say 2001.

Whisper Rock is known for the number of touring professionals among its members. It is a golf-only club with no residential community, and membership is by invitation, generally with sponsorship from existing members.

## Scottsdale National Golf Club

**Courses:** two 18-hole courses and a nine-hole course.

- **The Mineshaft**, the original course from the club’s years as The Golf Club Scottsdale
- **The Other Course**, by Jackson Kahn Design, opened in 2016
- **The Bad Little Nine**, a nine-hole par-3 course by Jackson Kahn Design, opened in 2016

Bob Parsons bought the club in 2013. He and his wife, Renee Parsons, own it. The club has no conventional residential community, though member villas have been built on the property.

## Gainey Ranch Golf Club

**Course:** 27 holes in three nines: the Lakes, the Dunes and the Arroyo. **Opened:** 1985, according to one source.

Gainey Ranch is the main private option in central Scottsdale, inside the guard-gated Gainey Ranch community. The club is owned by Invited, formerly known as ClubCorp, and membership is separate from home ownership.

## Desert Forest Golf Club (Carefree)

**Course:** one. **Designer:** Robert “Red” Lawrence. **Opened:** 1962.

Just north of Scottsdale in Carefree, Desert Forest is often described as one of the first desert golf courses in the Southwest. A 2013 renovation was led by Dave Zinkand. It is worth knowing for its place in the history of desert golf design.

## Other private and club options nearby

**The Boulders Club**, spanning Scottsdale and Carefree, has two Jay Morrish courses and offers golf and social memberships alongside Boulders Resort and Spa. **Ancala Country Club** is a Scottsdale club owned by Arcis Golf. These clubs have different models from the member-owned clubs above, so ask each about access and membership.

## A short timeline of private golf here

- **1962:** Desert Forest opens in Carefree, an early example of desert golf design.
- **1984:** Desert Highlands opens its Jack Nicklaus course.
- **1985:** Gainey Ranch Golf Club opens in central Scottsdale, according to one source.
- **1987 to 2003:** Desert Mountain opens its six Nicklaus courses, from Renegade to Outlaw.
- **1995:** The Estancia Club opens its Tom Fazio course.
- **2001:** Mirabel opens its Fazio course in December, and Whisper Rock opens, according to most sources.
- **2002:** Silverleaf opens its Tom Weiskopf course.
- **2013:** Bob Parsons buys The Golf Club Scottsdale and renames it Scottsdale National.
- **2016:** The Other Course and the Bad Little Nine open at Scottsdale National.
- **2019:** No. 7 opens at Desert Mountain.

The pattern is clear. Private golf in Scottsdale moved steadily north over four decades, from the central city to the high desert near Carefree.

## How non-members can play

Private courses are, by design, hard to play if you are not a member. The usual routes are:

**As a member’s guest.** This is the most common way. Most private clubs allow members to bring guests, with guest fees and limits on how often the same guest can play. If you know a member, ask how the club’s guest policy works.

**Through reciprocal play.** Some private clubs arrange reciprocal access with other private clubs, so members of one can play the other. Ask your home club whether it has any reciprocal arrangements in Scottsdale.

**Through a club program.** Scottsdale National has been reported to offer an application-based program called the Xperience, which Golf.com reported costs $30,000 and forms part of its admissions process.

**Through charity and event play.** Private clubs sometimes host charity events or tournaments that include non-member players. These are occasional and vary by club.

**As a prospective member or buyer.** Clubs inside residential communities sometimes arrange golf for serious prospective members or home buyers. Ask the club or a real estate agent who works in the community.

If none of these is available to you, Scottsdale’s public courses, including TPC Scottsdale, Troon North and Grayhawk, offer championship golf to anyone with a tee time.

## If you are invited as a guest

A few basics make a guest round go smoothly:

- **Ask your host about the dress code and arrival time.** Private clubs often have specific rules on attire and on where guests check in.
- **Expect a guest fee.** Many clubs charge one. Ask your host how it is handled.
- **Check phone and photo rules.** Some clubs limit phone use on the course or in the clubhouse.
- **Respect the pace of play.** Members play these courses often, and a steady pace matters.

## Choosing between private courses

If you are deciding which private club to join, the courses are only part of the decision. A few things to weigh:

- **One course or many.** Desert Mountain’s seven courses give variety and spread out tee-time pressure. Single-course clubs like Estancia, Mirabel and Silverleaf offer a more concentrated club culture.
- **Walkability.** If you like to walk, ask about it. Some desert courses are much easier to walk than others.
- **Practice facilities.** For serious players, the range, short-game area and putting greens can matter as much as the course.
- **Where you live.** Some clubs require or encourage owning property in their community. Others have no homes at all.
- **Ownership and cost.** Member-owned and privately owned clubs work very differently. Our [equity membership guide](/blog/what-is-an-equity-golf-membership) explains what that means for your money.
