Gainey Ranch is one of central Scottsdale’s most established golf communities. Where most of Scottsdale’s luxury golf communities have pushed north into the high desert, Gainey Ranch sits closer to the heart of the city, with lakes, mature landscaping and guard-gated neighborhoods around a 27-hole private club.

This guide covers what buyers should know: the community’s history, how it is organized, the golf club, what published data show about prices and HOA fees, and what to check before you buy.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. All prices and fees below are published snapshots shown as ranges, and sources sometimes disagree. Verify current figures with ARMLS data, the community association and a licensed real estate professional before you make a decision.*

## A short history

Gainey Ranch takes its name from Daniel C. Gainey, whose ranch here was known for raising Arabian horses. Sources also describe Hereford cattle and other agricultural operations on the land before it was developed.

In 1980, Markland Properties bought land from Gainey, and development of the master-planned community began in the mid-1980s. Sources differ on the size, citing about 560 acres or about 640 acres. One source dates the opening of the golf club to 1985.

The original Gainey family house later became the **Estate Club**, a community amenity with a pool, fitness center and tennis.

## How the community is organized

Gainey Ranch is a walled community with staffed gates. Sources describe 24-hour security, roving patrols and cameras. Inside, the community is divided into roughly 18 to 19 individually gated neighborhoods, depending on the source, with a mix of:

- **Condos**
- **Townhomes**
- **Single-family homes**, up to larger estate homes

Each neighborhood has its own association, in addition to the master community association. That means most homes pay more than one assessment, and rules can differ from one neighborhood to the next. Known neighborhoods include Sunset Cove, The Pavilions, Arroyo Vista and The Greens at Gainey Ranch.

Listings in Gainey Ranch typically carry the 85258 ZIP code.

## The golf

**Gainey Ranch Golf Club** is a private club with 27 holes in three nines:

- **The Lakes**
- **The Dunes**
- **The Arroyo**

Three nines give members flexibility, with three different 18-hole combinations. The club is owned by Invited, the club operator formerly known as ClubCorp. That makes it a non-equity club: members pay to use it but do not own it. Membership is separate from owning a home in Gainey Ranch.

Ask the club about current membership categories, initiation, dues and availability. Our [equity vs. non-equity club-by-club guide](/blog/equity-vs-non-equity-golf-memberships-scottsdale-clubs) explains what an owner-operated club means for members.

## The resort nearby

The **Hyatt Regency Scottsdale Resort and Spa** sits nearby. For residents, that adds restaurants, a spa and a resort atmosphere close to home. For buyers, it is worth considering how close a home is to the resort, since that can bring both convenience and more activity.

## What Gainey Ranch homes cost

Here is what published data show. These figures come from different sources and dates.

- **Median sale price, July 2025:** about $1.5 million, up about 15% from the year before, according to one source
- **Median home value, mid-2025:** about $1.2 million, according to another
- **Median single-family sale price:** about $1.7 million, in a third source
- **Median townhouse sale price:** about $800,000, in the same source

The spread reflects Gainey Ranch’s mix of home types. Condos and townhomes offer the lowest entry point, while single-family and estate homes sit well above the community-wide median. Ask your agent for recent sales in the specific neighborhood and home type.

## HOA fees

HOA fees vary widely by neighborhood. Published examples:

- **Sunset Cove** (single-family): about $215 to $348 a month
- **The Pavilions** (condos and townhomes): about $225 to $719 a month
- **One single-family listing:** about $300 a month

Higher fees often reflect more services, such as exterior maintenance, landscaping or neighborhood amenities. Guard-gated communities also carry the cost of staffing gates and security patrols. Ask for the full disclosure for every association the home belongs to, including reserves and any pending special assessments.

## Location and daily life

Gainey Ranch’s central location is its biggest difference from Scottsdale’s far-north golf communities. Shopping, dining, medical care and the city’s resort corridor are close by, and drive times to the airport and Old Town Scottsdale are shorter than from communities such as Desert Mountain or Troon North.

The setting is also different. Instead of boulders and saguaros, Gainey Ranch is known for its lakes, mature trees and established landscaping. Buyers who want the remote, high-desert feel of the far north may find it too suburban. Buyers who want convenience and a settled neighborhood often find it a strong fit.

## Choosing a home type in Gainey Ranch

**Condos.** The most affordable way into the community. Condo neighborhoods tend to carry higher HOA fees because the association covers more of the building and grounds. They suit part-year owners and downsizers. Ask about reserves, rental rules and any planned building projects.

**Townhomes.** More space and often a small private outdoor area, with published median sales around $800,000 in one source. Ask what the HOA covers on the exterior, and check for shared-wall noise.

**Single-family homes.** Detached homes, many along the golf course or lakes, with published medians around $1.7 million in one source. Owners usually handle more of their own maintenance, though some neighborhoods include landscaping.

**Estate homes.** The largest homes in the community, at the top of the market. Expect higher costs for upkeep, pools and systems.

## Golf course and lake lots

In Gainey Ranch, many homes sit along the golf course or the community’s lakes. Both can add views and value, and both have trade-offs. Golf lots see carts, maintenance crews and the occasional stray ball. Lake lots can bring views and a sense of space, but ask who maintains the water features and how their cost is shared. Compare similar homes on and off the course or water to see what premium you are paying.

## Gainey Ranch for part-year owners

Gainey Ranch appeals to many part-year owners because of its staffed gates and its condo and townhome options. Before you buy, confirm what your neighborhood association maintains while you are away, check the rental rules, and plan for someone to check the home during the summer. The staffed gate adds security, but it does not replace regular home checks.

## How Gainey Ranch compares

Among central Scottsdale’s established communities, Gainey Ranch stands out for its guard-gated neighborhoods and its private 27-hole club. Compared with far-north communities such as Desert Mountain or Silverleaf, it offers a more central location and a wider range of home prices, but a more suburban setting and an owner-operated rather than member-owned club.

## Who Gainey Ranch suits

Gainey Ranch tends to suit buyers who:

- Want guard-gated security in central Scottsdale
- Value short drives to shopping, dining, medical care and the airport
- Want a choice of condos, townhomes and single-family homes
- Like having a resort and a private golf club close by, with membership optional

It tends to suit less well those who:

- Want a member-owned club with a say in its future
- Want large desert lots and mountain views
- Want a community with no HOA layers

## Questions to ask Gainey Ranch Golf Club

If golf is part of your plan, ask the club:

- What membership categories are available, and what does each include?
- What are the current initiation and monthly dues?
- Is there a waitlist?
- What happens to my initiation if I resign?
- How are tee times allocated across the three nines?
- Are there food and beverage minimums or other required charges?
- Does Invited offer access to other clubs it owns, and on what terms?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed professional.

## Buyer due diligence

1. **All HOA documents.** Master and neighborhood associations, fees, reserves, rules and pending assessments.
2. **Rental rules.** These vary by neighborhood. Confirm them in writing.
3. **Club questions.** If golf matters, ask the club about categories, initiation, dues and availability before you buy.
4. **Comparable sales.** Ask for recent sales in the same neighborhood and home type.
5. **Condition.** Many homes date from the mid-1980s onward. Check the roof, plumbing, air conditioning and windows, and price any updates.
6. **Location within the community.** Consider proximity to the golf course, lakes, the resort and main roads.
7. **Insurance.** Get a quote for the specific address before you remove contingencies.
