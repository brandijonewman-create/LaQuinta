Cars are part of the Scottsdale golf lifestyle. The garage often matters as much as the golf bag storage, January brings one of the world’s best-known collector car auctions to the north side of the city, and many owners keep a different car here than they do at home.

This guide covers the practical side of buying a luxury car in Scottsdale: the taxes at purchase, Arizona’s annual vehicle license tax, emissions rules, registration, and what the desert does to a car.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not tax, legal or financial advice. Tax rates, fees and registration rules change. Verify every figure for your vehicle with the Arizona Department of Revenue, ADOT MVD, your dealer and a licensed tax professional before you make a decision.*

## What you pay at purchase

### Sales tax

Arizona calls its sales tax a transaction privilege tax. On a dealer sale in Scottsdale, the combined rate is built from three parts:

- **Arizona state:** 5.6%
- **Maricopa County:** 0.7%
- **City of Scottsdale:** the city’s rate, which has been in the 1.70% to 1.75% range

The City of Scottsdale has published a combined rate of 8.05% for most taxable activities, and the city has also noted a voter-approved rate change. Rather than rely on any single figure, treat the combined rate as **about 8%** and confirm the exact number on your purchase invoice. *Verify with the Arizona Department of Revenue or a licensed tax professional.*

On a $150,000 car, every percentage point is $1,500, so the difference between jurisdictions can be real money. If you live in another Arizona city, the rate on your purchase may depend on where the sale takes place and where the car is delivered. Ask the dealer to show the tax calculation line by line.

**Private-party sales.** Arizona generally does not charge transaction privilege tax on a private sale between individuals. Collector car buyers should still confirm how tax applies to their specific purchase, including auction purchases and vehicles bought out of state.

### Title and registration fees

ADOT lists small fixed fees for title and registration, plus an air quality fee in emissions areas. These are minor next to the vehicle license tax, which is the number to plan for.

## The vehicle license tax: Arizona’s annual car tax

Arizona does not charge a personal property tax on cars. Instead, it charges a **vehicle license tax (VLT)** every year when you register. For a luxury car, it is a meaningful line in the budget.

Here is how ADOT describes the formula:

- **First 12 months:** the assessed value is **60% of the manufacturer’s base retail price**, taxed at **$2.80 per $100** of assessed value.
- **Each year after:** the assessed value is **16.25% less** than the year before, taxed at **$2.89 per $100**.

Two points buyers often miss. First, the tax is based on the manufacturer’s base retail price, not what you negotiated. Second, it falls every year as the assessed value declines, so the first year is the highest.

**A worked example.** For a car with a $150,000 base retail price, the first-year assessed value is $90,000 (60%). At $2.80 per $100, the first-year VLT is about $2,520. In year two, the assessed value drops 16.25% to about $75,375, and at $2.89 per $100 the VLT is about $2,178. These figures are illustrations only; your registration notice is the real number. *Verify with ADOT MVD.*

## Emissions testing

Scottsdale is inside the Phoenix metropolitan emissions area. ADOT says testing is required in the Phoenix and Tucson areas, including for vehicles that commute into them, unless the vehicle is:

- **Five years old or less**
- **Model year 1966 or older**
- **An electric vehicle**

So a new car will not need a test right away, and a vintage car from 1966 or earlier is exempt. Most cars in between need testing once they pass five years. Tests for renewals should be done no sooner than 90 days before your registration expires. The state’s program details, testing locations and exemptions are on myAZcar.com.

## Registering as a new or part-year resident

If you become an Arizona resident, you need to register your vehicles with the Arizona Motor Vehicle Division. For owners who split the year between Scottsdale and another home, residency is not always simple, and it affects where you register and pay tax. Talk with a tax professional about your situation, and confirm the rules with ADOT MVD. Our [second-home tax guide](/blog/scottsdale-second-home-tax-guide-2026) covers the broader residency questions part-year owners face.

## January is car month: Barrett-Jackson

Every January, the Barrett-Jackson collector car auction takes over WestWorld of Scottsdale, at 16601 North Pima Road in north Scottsdale. For 2027, Barrett-Jackson has expanded the Scottsdale auction to **nine days, January 23 to 31, 2027**, with a second full weekend of live bidding.

Whether you plan to bid or simply watch, a few practical notes:

- North Scottsdale is close to WestWorld, which makes the auction easy to visit from many of the area’s golf communities.
- The auction draws large crowds, and January is also peak golf season, so plan tee times and dinner reservations early.
- If you plan to bid, register as a bidder through Barrett-Jackson’s official site well ahead of time, and arrange inspection, transport and insurance before the gavel falls.
- Ask a tax professional how tax applies to an auction purchase in your situation.

## Owning a luxury car in the desert

Scottsdale is kind to cars in some ways: no road salt and long dry stretches. The heat is the challenge.

**Parking and storage.** A garage is the single best protection for paint, interiors, rubber and electronics. Many golf homes here are built with oversized or climate-conditioned garages for exactly this reason. Our [luxury home features guide](/blog/luxury-features-scottsdale-golf-homes) covers what buyers look for.

**Paint and interior.** Intense sun is hard on paint, leather and dashboards. Window film, protective coatings and regular care help. Our [car detailing and ceramic coating guide](/blog/car-detailing-ceramic-coating-luxury-cars-scottsdale) goes into detail.

**Batteries and tires.** Heat is tough on batteries and tires. Ask your service department how often to check both, especially on a car that sits for weeks.

**Summer storage.** If you leave for the summer, plan for the car you leave behind. Ask your dealer or service center how to store your specific model: battery maintainers, tire pressure and whether someone should drive it periodically.

**Monsoon season.** Summer storms can bring sudden heavy rain, dust and hail. Covered parking matters.

## Planning the garage

For many golf households, the garage holds more than cars. It may need room for a golf cart, golf bags, a workbench and a vehicle that only comes out in season. If you are buying, building or remodeling, think through the garage early:

- **Count the bays you really need.** Include guests’ cars, a golf cart and any car you plan to add.
- **Ceiling height and depth.** Larger SUVs, roof racks and car lifts need more room than a standard garage offers.
- **Cooling and insulation.** A garage that stays cooler is easier on cars, batteries, golf equipment and anything stored there.
- **Charging.** If you drive or may buy an electric vehicle, plan for a dedicated circuit and charger location.
- **Golf cart access.** In golf communities, a separate cart door or cart garage is common and convenient.
- **Lighting and finishes.** Bright lighting and durable floors make care and detailing easier.
- **Security.** Ask about cameras and access control, especially if the home will sit empty in summer.

In gated communities, exterior changes to a garage usually need design review approval, so check the guidelines before you plan an addition.

## Choosing a dealer or seller

For a new car, the decision is usually brand first, then dealer. For a pre-owned or collector car, the seller matters as much as the car. Whatever you buy:

1. **Get the out-the-door price in writing,** with tax, fees and any add-ons listed separately.
2. **Ask for the VLT estimate** for your first year of registration.
3. **Check the warranty,** including what is covered in Arizona heat.
4. **For pre-owned cars,** get an independent pre-purchase inspection and a vehicle history report.
5. **For collector cars,** ask for documentation of ownership history, restoration work and authenticity.
6. **Ask about service.** Where will the car be serviced, and is loaner or pickup service offered?
7. **Ask about delivery.** If you are out of town, can the car be delivered to your home and registered for you?

## The bottom line

Buying a luxury car in Scottsdale costs more than the sticker in two predictable ways: sales tax at purchase and the vehicle license tax every year after. Both are easy to estimate before you buy. Plan for them, protect the car from the sun, and January will give you a reason to visit WestWorld.
