Desert Mountain Club is the largest private golf club in the Scottsdale area, with seven courses and a membership spread across a community of about 8,300 acres. It is also a club whose prices have changed sharply in recent years. Figures that were accurate a few years ago now understate the cost by a wide margin.

This guide lays out what is known about Desert Mountain membership cost, separating the club’s own published figures from third-party estimates, and shows how to turn those ranges into a realistic budget. For the HOA side of the equation, see our [guide to living in Desert Mountain](/blog/living-in-desert-mountain-hoa-rules-costs-2026).

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal, tax or financial advice. Club fees and terms change. Verify every figure directly with Desert Mountain Club and review the membership documents with a licensed real estate, legal or financial professional before you make a decision.*

## How the club is structured

Desert Mountain Club has been member-owned since 2011, when members bought the club’s six golf courses, related facilities and about 500 acres of developable land from Crescent Real Estate Holdings for a reported $73.5 million. Members elect the board that sets dues, approves capital projects and decides on assessments.

The club is separate from the community’s homeowners associations. Owning a home in Desert Mountain does not make you a member, and club dues do not cover HOA assessments. You budget for each separately.

## The three membership categories

The club currently lists three categories.

**Full Golf.** Full access to the six Jack Nicklaus Signature courses (Renegade, Cochise, Geronimo, Apache, Chiricahua and Outlaw), along with No. 7 and the club’s dining, fitness, racquet and social facilities.

**Seven Golf.** Built around No. 7 at Desert Mountain, the par-54 course designed by club members Bill Brownlee and Wendell Pickett that opened in 2019. It suits members who want golf that is shorter, more social and less time-consuming than a full 18-hole round on a championship course.

**Lifestyle.** Dining, fitness, racquet sports and social programs without full golf. It suits non-golfers and households where golf is a minor part of club life.

Choosing a category is the most important cost decision you will make. The difference between Full Golf and Lifestyle is large, and moving between categories later can come with its own costs and conditions. Ask the club how upgrades and downgrades work before you choose.

## How to choose a category

The right category depends on how your household will actually use the club. A few practical tests:

**Count your rounds.** If you will play 18 holes on a championship course several times a week, Full Golf is the category built for you. If your golf is mostly shorter, social rounds, Seven Golf may fit better at a lower cost.

**Look beyond golf.** If one person in the household golfs and the other uses the fitness, racquet and dining programs, ask the club how household memberships work in each category.

**Think about the season.** Part-year residents should weigh full-year dues against the months they are actually in Arizona. Ask whether any seasonal or non-resident options exist.

**Plan for change.** Your use of the club may change over time. Ask whether you can move between categories later, what it costs and whether a wait list applies to upgrades.

**Ask about family.** If children or grandchildren will use the club, ask how dependents are covered and at what age they need their own membership.

The cheapest category is not always the best value. A Lifestyle membership that leaves you paying guest fees every time you want to play may cost more over a year than the category above it. Run the numbers on your expected use.

## What the club last published

The most recent official fee schedule we found is the club’s 2021 FAQ. It listed initiation fees of:

- **Full Golf:** $70,000
- **Seven Golf:** $33,750
- **Lifestyle:** $22,250

The same document noted that all fees may be revised. That same year, the club announced a new membership model. These figures are useful as a reference point for how the categories relate to each other, but they should not be used for a current budget.

## What third-party sources say now

Current figures circulate only through third parties, and they do not agree. Here is what we found:

- **Golf membership tied to a home purchase:** about $200,000 initiation and about $2,242 a month in dues, from one undated listing
- **Seven Golf:** about $125,000 initiation and about $1,311 a month in dues, from the same source
- **Lifestyle:** about $100,000 initiation, from the same source
- **Other sources:** a general Desert Mountain initiation of about $200,000, and a broad range of $150,000 or more with annual dues of $15,000 or more

Taken together, a reasonable planning range is roughly **$100,000 to $200,000 or more for initiation**, depending on category, and roughly **$1,300 to $2,250 a month for dues**. Treat both as estimates until the club confirms current figures.

The gap between the 2021 schedule and today’s estimates is the single most important thing to understand. If you see an online figure in the $20,000 to $70,000 range, it is almost certainly out of date.

## Wait lists and membership-included homes

Availability affects cost as much as price does.

**Wait lists.** The club notes that wait lists may apply to all membership categories. Ask how long the current wait is for the category you want, how the list is ordered and whether a deposit is required.

**Seven Desert Mountain.** Memberships in all three categories have been reserved for approved applicants who buy a home in the Seven Desert Mountain enclave, and those memberships have not been subject to the wait list.

**Membership-included listings.** The club has promoted home listings that include a membership. If you are buying, ask whether a home you are considering comes with a membership, what category it is and what you would pay at closing.

If you want to join soon, these routes can matter as much as the initiation itself. Raise them with your agent and the club before you make an offer.

## What you actually pay in year one

Here is how to build a first-year budget, using the published ranges. Replace each line with the club’s actual figures once you have them.

1. **Initiation:** roughly $100,000 to $200,000 or more, depending on category
2. **Dues:** at roughly $1,300 to $2,250 a month, about $15,700 to $26,900 a year
3. **Food and beverage minimum:** ask whether one applies and how much it is
4. **Assessments:** ask about any current or planned assessments
5. **Other charges:** guest fees, lockers, storage and service charges
6. **HOA assessments:** separate from the club, set by the Master Association and your village association

For the long view, spread the initiation over the years you expect to stay. A $150,000 initiation over ten years is about $15,000 a year before any refund. Over five years, it is about $30,000 a year.

## Is any part of the initiation refundable?

Desert Mountain is member-owned, but ownership does not automatically mean a refund. At equity clubs in general, refund terms are set by the club’s documents and range from a large share of the initiation to nothing. Refunds, where they exist, are often paid only when a membership is resold, and transfer fees can reduce what you receive.

We could not confirm Desert Mountain’s current refund and transfer terms from public sources. Ask for the membership plan and have a licensed professional review the sections on resignation, refunds and transfers.

## Questions to ask the membership office

- What are the current initiation fees and dues for each category?
- Is there a wait list for the category I want? How long is it?
- Does the home I am considering include a membership?
- Is there a food and beverage minimum?
- Are any assessments planned or being paid now?
- How do upgrades and downgrades between categories work, and what do they cost?
- Is any part of the initiation refundable, and on what terms?
- What happens to my membership if I sell my home?

Get the answers in writing and review them with a licensed professional.

## How Desert Mountain compares

Based on published estimates, Desert Mountain’s initiation sits below estimates for Silverleaf, where recent figures put Golf initiation at roughly $400,000 to $500,000, and Scottsdale National, where most sources report around $300,000 or more. What sets Desert Mountain apart is not price but scale: seven courses, multiple clubhouses and a large membership. That gives members more options and less pressure on tee times, at the cost of a less intimate club culture than smaller clubs offer.
