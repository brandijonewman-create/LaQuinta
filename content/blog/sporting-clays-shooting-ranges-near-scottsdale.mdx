Golfers and clay shooters often turn out to be the same people. Sporting clays, in particular, has a lot in common with golf: you move through a course of stations, each presenting a different challenge, you keep score against yourself and your friends, and much of the fun is the walk and the company. It is no surprise that many golf groups add a clays outing to their trips.

This guide explains sporting clays and its cousins, the major public shooting facilities near Scottsdale, what a first visit involves and how to plan a group outing.

*Important: This is general editorial information, not legal advice. Facility hours, prices, access rules and offerings change. Confirm details directly with each facility before you go, follow every range rule, and comply with all applicable firearms laws.*

## Golf with a shotgun

**Sporting clays** is often called golf with a shotgun. A course has a series of stations spread across the landscape. At each one, targets are thrown at different angles, speeds and distances, designed to simulate birds or game in flight. Like golf, no two stations are the same, and the course rewards adaptability as much as raw skill.

**Trap** uses a single field, with targets thrown away from the shooter from a house in front of them at varying angles.

**Skeet** uses a semicircular field, with targets thrown from high and low houses on either side as shooters move through set stations.

**Five-stand** is a compact variation, with several throwers and a few shooting positions, offering sporting-clays-style variety in a small space.

For golfers who enjoy variety and course management, sporting clays is usually the most natural fit.

## Shooting facilities near Scottsdale

These are two of the best-known public-access facilities in the Phoenix area. Check each one’s current offerings, hours and rules before you go.

### Ben Avery Shooting Facility (north Phoenix)

- **Operator:** the Arizona Game and Fish Department
- **Size:** about 1,650 acres
- **History:** founded in 1957, formerly known as the Black Canyon Shooting Range
- **Scale:** described as one of the largest publicly operated shooting facilities in the country, with well over 100,000 shooters a year in published figures
- **Disciplines:** a rifle and pistol range, a clay target center and an archery range

The **Ben Avery Clay Target Center** sits on the grounds and is owned by the Arizona Game and Fish Commission. Check with the facility for the current mix of clay disciplines and hours.

### Rio Salado Sportsman’s Club (Mesa)

- **Location:** on Usery Pass Road in Mesa, on about 140 acres of Arizona Game and Fish Department land
- **Sporting clays:** a 13-station course
- **Other clay games:** a combination field with trap, wobble trap and 5-stand
- **Hours:** the club’s site lists the main range and shotgun range as open 7 a.m. to 5 p.m., seven days a week
- **Notes:** sporting clays may be closed around registered events, and listings differ on public versus membership access, so check current rules before visiting

There are also indoor ranges and private clubs in the area, as well as hunting preserves farther afield that offer clays. Ask your golf friends where they shoot.

## Your first visit

1. **Call or check online** for hours, prices, rentals and any requirements.
2. **Book a lesson.** An instructor will cover safety, gun fit, stance and how to read targets. It is the fastest way to enjoy the sport.
3. **Rent equipment** if you do not own a shotgun. Ask what is available.
4. **Bring eye and ear protection,** or ask whether the facility provides it.
5. **Dress for the weather.** Closed-toe shoes, layers in winter, a hat and sun protection in warm months.
6. **Arrive early** to check in, sign any waivers and listen to the safety briefing.

## Safety basics

Safety comes first at every range. Follow the facility’s rules and instructors at all times. Some universal principles:

- **Treat every firearm as loaded.**
- **Keep the muzzle pointed in a safe direction.**
- **Keep the action open** and the gun unloaded until you are at the station and ready to shoot.
- **Keep your finger off the trigger** until you are ready to fire.
- **Wear eye and ear protection** at all times on the range.
- **Never mix alcohol and shooting.** Save the celebration for afterward.

If you are new, an instructor will walk you through all of this.

## How a round of sporting clays works

A typical round follows a familiar rhythm for golfers:

1. **Check in** and get your scorecard or target count.
2. **Move through the stations** with your group, often on foot or by cart.
3. **Watch the targets first.** At each station, the group can usually view the target presentations before shooting, much like reading a green.
4. **Take turns shooting,** with a set number of targets per station.
5. **Keep score,** counting the targets each shooter breaks.
6. **Compare at the end.** Friendly competition is part of the fun.

## Etiquette on the course

- **Wait your turn** and stay behind the shooter.
- **Keep quiet** while someone is shooting.
- **Pick up spent shells** if the facility asks you to.
- **Keep pace,** and let faster groups play through where the course allows.
- **Respect the facility’s rules** on cart use, guns in vehicles and where loading is allowed.

## Gun fit and equipment

For beginners, rental guns and instruction are the best start. If you decide to buy, gun fit matters: a shotgun that fits your build and shooting style helps you point naturally. Ask a qualified instructor or fitter for advice before buying, and make sure you understand and follow all laws that apply to purchasing, transporting and storing firearms.

Other useful gear includes a shooting vest or pouch for shells, comfortable shoes, shooting glasses with lenses suited to the light and quality ear protection.

## Families and juniors

Many facilities welcome juniors with appropriate supervision, and some offer youth programs or instruction. Age rules and requirements vary, so check with each facility before bringing children or teenagers.

## Heat and the season

Like golf, clays in the Phoenix area is most comfortable from fall through spring. In summer, shoot early, carry water, use sun protection and take breaks in the shade. Check whether facilities adjust hours in summer.

## Why golfers take to it

Golfers often find sporting clays familiar from the first station:

- **Course management.** Each station needs a plan, just like each hole.
- **Routine.** A consistent pre-shot routine helps in both sports.
- **Tempo.** A smooth, unhurried move usually beats a rushed one.
- **Reading conditions.** Wind, light and angles matter, much like reading a green or judging a crosswind.
- **Scoring.** Both reward steady play over the whole round rather than one great shot.

Many golfers also enjoy that clays is a fresh challenge where nobody is carrying a handicap, so a group of mixed golf ability starts on level ground.

## Combining golf and clays on a trip

On a multi-day golf trip, a clays morning can give legs and backs a rest while keeping the competitive spirit. A common pattern is golf on most days and a clays session on a rest day, followed by a relaxed group dinner. Check facility hours and book instruction ahead, especially in peak season.

## Planning a clays outing for a golf group

A clays session makes a great rest day on a golf trip or a fun event for a golf group.

- **Book ahead,** especially for groups and on weekends in peak season.
- **Ask about group packages,** including instruction, rentals, targets and ammunition.
- **Book an instructor** for beginners in the group.
- **Plan a friendly competition,** scored like a round of golf.
- **Arrange transportation,** since facilities are a drive from most Scottsdale resorts and neighborhoods.
- **Save the drinks for after,** at a restaurant or back home.

## What it costs

Costs vary by facility, discipline and what you rent. Expect to pay for:

- **Range or target fees,** often by round or number of targets
- **Rentals,** such as a shotgun, cart or vest
- **Ammunition,** if not included
- **Instruction,** for lessons or guided rounds
- **Membership,** at clubs that require or offer it

Ask each facility for its current prices before you go.

## Questions to ask a facility

1. What disciplines do you offer, and which are open on the day I plan to visit?
2. Are you open to the public, or is membership required?
3. Do you offer lessons and rentals?
4. What are your prices for targets, rentals and instruction?
5. Do you provide eye and ear protection?
6. Do you offer group packages?
7. Are there any closures for events, weather or maintenance?
