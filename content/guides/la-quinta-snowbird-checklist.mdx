## 1. The residency conversation, before you offer

If your primary home and income source are in another state, owning a second home in California typically does not automatically make you a California resident for income-tax purposes. *Typically* is doing real work in that sentence. California Franchise Tax Board residency is a facts-and-circumstances test:

- Days spent in California (the 183-day rule is a starting point, not the only test)
- Source of income (CA-source income, even small, can trigger filing)
- Family, voter registration, driver's license, professional licenses, social ties

Before you make an offer, have a one-hour conversation with a tax professional who understands FTB practice. We are not tax advisers; this is general guidance only.

## 2. HOA short-term-rental rules — in writing

La Quinta city ordinance allows STRs in some communities and prohibits them in others. HOA rules layer on top and can be more restrictive. PGA West has multiple sub-associations with different STR rules. Get CC&Rs and the current STR policy in writing before the inspection period ends.

## 3. Club access transfer

Club membership rarely transfers automatically with the home. Some clubs require you to apply and be approved. Some require a fresh initiation deposit. Some have a waitlist. Confirm with the club office — not the listing agent.

## 4. Operational readiness checklist

- **HVAC service contract** — desert summers stress AC systems. Non-optional.
- **Pool service contract** — weekly minimum.
- **Pest control contract** — bark scorpions exist on properties up against the mountains.
- **Mail-forwarding strategy** — USPS hold has a 30-day cap.
- **Insurance** — vacant-home riders, wildfire deductibles, theft-while-empty coverage.
- **Smart home** — leak sensors, freeze-stat, garage-door alerts, smart thermostat.

## 5. The first-year tax calendar

- **April:** California Form 540NR if you have any CA-source income
- **April:** Property tax 2nd installment due
- **December:** Property tax 1st installment due
