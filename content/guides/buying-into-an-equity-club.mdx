## What an equity membership actually is

In an equity club, members collectively own the club. The membership itself is a security — a transferable ownership interest — governed by the club's bylaws and a membership agreement that you sign and the club countersigns.

Key consequences:

- **Initiation deposit:** A large upfront payment, structured as refundable or partially-refundable. Some clubs hold it until you exit; others refund a percentage on resale.
- **Annual dues:** Cover operating costs. Adjusted annually.
- **Capital assessments:** Special levies for major capital projects. These are real and recurring. Ask for the last 10 years of assessment history.
- **Transfer mechanics:** When you sell, the membership may transfer to your buyer (if approved) or revert to the club.

## The four contracts buyers consistently miss

When you buy a home in an equity club, you are signing or receiving at least four distinct contracts:

1. **The real estate purchase contract** (familiar territory).
2. **The HOA CC&Rs and rules** (often overlooked but binding).
3. **The club membership agreement** — a separate legal document, often signed weeks after closing.
4. **The club bylaws** — a third legal document governing how the club is run.

Read all four. The membership agreement and bylaws are where the meaningful terms live: refundability, transfer rules, dues escalation, assessment authority, expulsion procedures, voting rights.

## Refundability — the most-asked question

Clubs vary widely:

- **Fully refundable on exit:** Some historic clubs refund the full initiation deposit when you sell.
- **Partially refundable:** Most common. Clubs refund 50–80% on resale, often only after a new member buys in.
- **Non-refundable:** A growing trend at newer clubs.

Never assume. Always ask for the current refund schedule in writing.

## Transfer with the home

- **Automatic transfer:** Rare.
- **New application required:** Most common. New buyer must apply, be approved, and pay the prevailing initiation deposit.
- **Bundled at closing:** Membership is reset and the new buyer pays initiation.

A home advertised as "club access included" may not actually deliver that for the buyer. Verify before removing contingencies.

## Three questions to ask the club office before you commit

1. What is the current refund schedule on the initiation deposit, and what is the average time-to-refund after a member resigns?
2. How many capital assessments has the club levied in the last 10 years, and what is on the projected capital plan for the next 5 years?
3. Is there a waitlist, an approval process, or any transfer fee beyond the standard initiation?

The answers, in writing, are the single most useful diligence step you can perform before signing a membership agreement.

## A note on legal counsel

If you are signing into a meaningful equity position (initiation deposits at La Quinta private clubs commonly run six figures), having a real-estate attorney review this club's specific documents is worth the modest hourly cost.
