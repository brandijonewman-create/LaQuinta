# Backlink Outreach Playbook — Scottsdale Golf Lifestyle

## What this site actually is (read first)
**Scottsdale Golf Lifestyle is an independent editorial publication.** It is not a real-estate brokerage. The owner and creator, Brandi Jo Newman, is referenced ONLY as "owner and creator" — never as an agent, broker, or licensed real-estate professional. The site supports itself through display advertising (AdSense), the gated lead-magnet system, and partnerships — not through commission income on home sales.

**This positioning is a feature, not a bug.** Editorial sites earn different (and often better) backlinks than agent marketing sites: from journalists, publishers, civic organizations, and topical authorities — not from real-estate marketplace directories.

The outreach posture, the credentials we claim, and the directories we target all flow from this. Every template and target list below has been written for an editorial publication, not a brokerage.

---

## Hard rules (do not violate, ever)
1. ❌ NO inventing or quoting transaction volume, GCI, or closed-deal stats. We have no sales record — the site has never closed a transaction.
2. ❌ NO claiming agent credentials (CLHMS, CRS, REALTOR®, etc.). The owner is not a licensed agent.
3. ❌ NO publishing a physical address, phone number, or email on the site or in outreach unless and until real, verified contact info has been supplied by the owner.
4. ❌ NO directories that require a real-estate license (Realtor.com agent profiles, Zillow Premier Agent, Redfin Partner Agent, Homes.com agent listings).
5. ✅ Refer to Brandi Jo Newman ONLY as "owner and creator."
6. ✅ All outreach must accurately represent the site as an independent editorial publication, not a brokerage.

## Tier 2 — Earned media (weeks 3-8)

### HARO / Qwoted / Connectively
Free services where journalists post requests for expert sources. We respond as an **editorial publisher with topical expertise on Scottsdale golf real estate** — not as a licensed agent giving transactional advice. Our angle is editorial context, market interpretation, and historical framing, not specific deal advice.

**Response template (honest, editorial framing):**

```
Hi [Journalist Name],

Responding to your query about [topic].

I'm the owner and creator of Scottsdale Golf Lifestyle (scottsdalegolflifestyle.com), an independent editorial publication covering luxury golf real estate in Scottsdale. We are not a brokerage and do not represent buyers or sellers — we publish editorial guides, market analysis, and community deep-dives.

On [their specific question]: [3 sentences of substance + 1 piece of editorial context that other sources won't provide + 1 quotable observation].

Happy to provide additional context or point to specific published guides on the site if useful.

If quoting, please attribute to: "Scottsdale Golf Lifestyle, an independent editorial publication on Scottsdale luxury golf real estate (scottsdalegolflifestyle.com)."

— Brandi Jo Newman, owner and creator
```

Reasonable for an editorial publication that covers the local market:

- Greater Phoenix Chamber (media tier)

**Don't join as a real-estate professional** — join as an independent media publication that covers the city.

### Sponsorships
A small editorial publication can sponsor local events to earn community goodwill and (often) a backlink from the organization's website. ROI depends on the organization's domain authority.

Reasonable targets:
- Phoenix Children's Hospital golf tournament (media sponsor tier)
- Local high school golf team booster events
- Scottsdale civic events with publication-friendly sponsorship tiers

Skip: events that only offer "agent of the year" or transactional sponsorship slots. Wrong fit.

### HOA & community newsletter editorial partnerships
Some Scottsdale HOA newsletters will accept editorial contributions from local publishers in exchange for a publisher credit + link. This is different from a paid sponsorship — it's editorial syndication. Pitch as such.

## Tier 4 — Content-driven & passive (ongoing)

### Resource-page outreach
Search Google for phrases like:
- `"Scottsdale" "resources" "real estate"`
- `"Scottsdale golf" "resources"`
- `"Arizona relocation" "resources"`

Email the page owner: *"I publish an independent editorial guide to Scottsdale golf real estate at scottsdalegolflifestyle.com. Would you consider adding our Buyer's Guide ([URL]) to your resources page? It's free, ad-supported, and editorially independent."*

### Broken-link building
Find dead links on Scottsdale real-estate or golf sites with Ahrefs or the Check My Links extension. Email site owner: *"I noticed [URL] is broken — we have a similar editorial resource at [URL] if it would serve your readers as a replacement."*

### Wikipedia citations (use sparingly and honestly)
Our market reports and community guides can serve as citations on Wikipedia articles about Scottsdale neighborhoods, golf courses, or Arizona real estate IF they meet Wikipedia's reliable-source criteria (independent editorial, not promotional). Wikipedia editors revert promotional edits quickly — add citations only where our content is the most authoritative public source.

Good candidate articles: "Real estate in Phoenix, Arizona," "Scottsdale, Arizona," individual golf course pages.

### Embeddable widgets
Build (or commission) lightweight embeddable widgets — a "Scottsdale Golf Community Comparison" or a "Market Snapshot" widget — that other blogs and resource pages can drop into their content with attribution. Each embed becomes a backlink and a citation. *(This is one of the highest-ROI passive-link strategies for editorial sites.)*

## Tier 5 — Things to AVOID

- ❌ Realtor.com / Zillow Premier Agent / Redfin Partner Agent / Homes.com agent listings — these require a real-estate license and we don't qualify
- ❌ Paid link networks (PBN/blog-network providers) — fast-track penalty risk
- ❌ "Featured in" mockup widgets that imply press mentions we haven't actually earned
- ❌ Press releases distributed through low-quality wire services — Google has heavily discounted these
- ❌ Mass blog-comment outreach — universally treated as spam
- ❌ Reciprocal-link schemes — devalued by Google for over a decade
- ❌ Any directory or platform that lets you claim a fake address or phone — even if you put real info there, anyone else can spam-list a fake one and your NAP consistency breaks
